# Round 1, paediatric cancer-predisposition oncologist (panel 2)

This child has had an embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma and is in surveillance in a checkpoint-deficient host. Three questions decide my ranking: does the candidate change residual relapse or second-primary risk, does it compromise the next line of therapy, and can the existing visits carry its readout.

Two numbers frame everything. F4: a treated child faces a residual, not a lifetime, risk, so every break-even threshold is multiplied by 1/s. At a residual share of 20 percent and added harm of 5 percent, a drug must cut cancer risk by 64 percent to pay for itself, and no approved drug has any measured effect on cancer incidence in MVA (02 section 6; G7). F2: the negative mosaicism result bounds a clonal trisomy in blood at about 4 percent and a tissue-confined clone at nothing.

## 1. Ranked list

**1. A written avoid-and-substitute rule, with a carve-out for curative-intent therapy.** Step: kinetochore-microtubule attachment and renal calcium handling, the two already weakened. Out, with substitutes: colchicine, griseofulvin, albendazole and mebendazole, all aneugens (colchicine is the OECD TG 487 reference aneugen, PMID 20362070; griseofulvin PMID 25308540; use terbinafine, or an NSAID for colchicine indications); acetazolamide and topiramate (carbonic anhydrase, PMC5976810; use levetiracetam, since seizures occur in 45 percent of MVA1); loop diuretics (B2, PMID 19460127); high-dose intermittent vitamin D (PMID 2386055); growth hormone and mecasermin (PMID 25839904, PMID 36588761). Tier: human genotoxicity and label data per agent, inference for the transfer, since no MVA cell has been tested against any of them (01 section 4.3). Readout: compliance. Rank: the only item with positive expected value under the F4 thresholds.

The carve-out is what I will argue for. Vincristine, vinorelbine and dactinomycin stay in curative-intent protocols. The taxane sensitisation data come from engineered knockdown tumour cells, not patient material (Janssen 2009, PMID 19855003), and the direction in a BubR1-deficient host is unknown both ways (02 section 4). RMS 2005 maintenance carries a randomised 5-year overall survival of 85.0 versus 72.4 percent (Bisogno 2019, PMID 31562043). I will not trade that for a mechanistic prediction.

**2. Metformin.** Step: AMPK, and the energy stress aneuploid cells tolerate poorly. Tier: cell, now including human patient cells. AICAR selectively eliminated the isochromosome 12p population in Pallister-Killian patient fibroblasts, leaving euploid cells (bioRxiv 2022.01.27.478025, via G5), extending Tang 2011 (PMID 21315436) from mouse MEFs to a human mosaic aneuploidy patient. AICAR is unapproved; metformin is the approved AMPK stand-in and was not tested, which is the named experiment. Paediatric: licensed from 10 years, oral solution, off-label by age here; oncology phase I 666 to 2000 mg/m2/day (PMC9972017). Next-line fit: that phase I combined metformin with vincristine, irinotecan and temozolomide, the EpSSG standard salvage after prior alkylator (VIT-0910), the only published co-administration data with the regimen this child would receive at relapse. Harm: lactic acidosis with falling filtration alongside nephrocalcinosis. Readout: micronucleated reticulocytes per 1e6 from a draw already taken, plus cystatin C.

**3. Amiloride added to hydrochlorothiazide plus potassium citrate.** Step: distal calcium reabsorption via TRPV5, additive to thiazide and potassium-sparing (B3, J4; Am J Med 1981). Tier: human, adult stone formers, paediatric by extrapolation. Licensed, generic. Fit: renal reserve gates contrast at every surveillance interval and any alkylator salvage. Harm: hyperkalaemia and acidosis on top of citrate. Readout: spot urine calcium to creatinine at scheduled visits, potassium and bicarbonate at weeks 1 and 4, cystatin C not creatinine. Rank: it protects deliverability of the next line, not the disease.

**4. Cyproheptadine, with mirtazapine as the alternative.** Step: appetite, engaging no growth-factor or steroid axis (B4, PMID 18989154; J11). Tier: human, small uncontrolled paediatric series. Licensed. Harm: sedation; the weight gained may be fat, not the deficient lean mass. Readout: mid-upper arm circumference and weight-for-age z-score at the three-monthly visit (H5). Rank: the non-hormonal route to an endpoint the family tracks.

**5. Dexrazoxane, conditional on an anthracycline entering a relapse regimen.** Tier: human, paediatric, standard in COG practice; the EU label restricts under-18 use unless cumulative anthracycline exceeds 300 mg/m2 (I). Fit: BubR1-insufficient mice die of cardiac failure even after senescent-cell clearance improved every other outcome (Baker 2011, PMID 22048312), and a SIRT2 transgene reversed an ECG J-point defect (North 2014, PMID 24825348). Harm: a topoisomerase II catalytic inhibitor with a second-malignancy label flag cuts both ways here. Readout: echocardiogram, troponin, and a baseline ECG read for the J point.

**6. Intermittent dasatinib, three days per month, quercetin omitted.** Step: senolysis, the strongest published argument in the syndrome (Baker 2011; p16 ablation, Baker 2008, PMID 18516091). Tier: mouse and genetic, human only in adults (NCT04733534). Licensed from age 1 in Ph+ CML. Harm: the p16-high compartment in a growing child includes growth plate and satellite cells (J, idea A); a labelled growth warning; QT stacking with ondansetron on chemotherapy days (I). No senolytic drug has been given to a BubR1 mouse (01, correction 1). Readout: height velocity and bone age against the child's own trajectory. Rank: last, and I would not start it during surveillance.

## 2. Creative-round ideas

Adopted: J12, read contraindications backwards, as rank 1, with B5 and A8/A10 folded in; B2, nephrocalcinosis reattributed to prematurity and furosemide first; B3 and J4, amiloride; B4 and J11, appetite agents; G5, AICAR in Pallister-Killian fibroblasts, why metformin ranks 2; C2 and C1/J3, dish first and parental lymphocytes as first exposed tissue; C3 and J1, micronucleated reticulocytes and buccal micronuclei; C10 and C12, natural history first and a pre-declared negative control; D4 and D6, N1002 at the surface and the clean MT-RNR1, CYP3A5 \*3/\*3 and G6PD calls; F4, the thresholds; H, the diet.

Rejected. B10, replacing vinorelbine maintenance with metronomic cyclophosphamide: discards a randomised survival benefit for an unmeasured mechanism. A1, digoxin: D2 shows a stabilised transcript still terminates at 737 and yields no kinase domain. A3, ataluren: authorisation not renewed 28 March 2025, and the minus-1 G context predicts poor response (A0). A5, A6 and B8, chaperone induction and phenylbutyrate: D4 places N1002 at the surface, the wrong mechanism, and relieving proteotoxic stress may improve premalignant clone fitness. A9, low-dose paclitaxel: published directions are opposite and the sensitising one was measured in this genotype class. B1, interferon score, and J2, raising NK clearance: research-grade, and it taxes the same p16-high compartment. E, CDK4/6 inhibitors: myelosuppression, a growth signal on the palbociclib label, no MVA evidence.

## 3. Drugs excluded

Growth hormone and mecasermin, as above; mecasermin is labelled contraindicated with a malignancy history. Sirolimus and everolimus: immunosuppression and malignancy warnings, trough chaos the moment an azole starts for febrile neutropenia, and aneuploid yeast grow better under rapamycin. Hydroxychloroquine: retinopathy monitoring, QT stacking with ondansetron every cycle, and a conflict of direction, since autophagy blockade kills aneuploid cells in Santaguida 2015 while autophagy activation protected neural stem cells in the Drosophila CIN microcephaly model (PMID 41820377). Hydroxyurea: it raises micronucleated reticulocytes, the readout itself. Aminoglycosides: nephrotoxicity in the organ under treatment. Proteasome inhibitors: they block mitotic exit. Megestrol and oxandrolone: adrenal suppression, accelerated epiphyseal fusion. Quercetin, fisetin, nicotinamide riboside, NMN and navitoclax: not approved medicines.

## 4. Strongest objection to my own top candidate

An avoidance rule is not a treatment. It cannot fail visibly, so it cannot be falsified, and its content is class-based inference: no MVA or BUB1B patient cell has been tested against colchicine, griseofulvin or any spindle agent (01 section 4.3). The failure mode is also asymmetric. A one-page list circulating where most clinicians will never see another MVA case is likely to be read as caution about the vinca class generally, and a de-escalated curative regimen would cost more than every aneugen exposure it prevents. The carve-out must be on the same page in the same size type.

## 5. What would change the ranking

A quantified residual BubR1 percentage in this child's cells, which exists for no MVA patient (01 section 2.2). RNA from the child, converting the NMD prediction to a measurement. A metformin dose-response on micronucleus or premature chromatid separation frequency in the child's fibroblasts and each parent's lymphocytes, with a pre-declared advance threshold (C2). A published series of vinca or dactinomycin toxicity in MVA, which would move the carve-out either way; there is one dactinomycin case (Ochiai 2019, PMID 31081598) and no vinca case. A within-patient variance estimate for any endpoint over 12 untreated months (C10). And UGT1A1 \*28 and \*6 read now from the WGS in hand, since irinotecan is the salvage backbone.

## 6. What the surveillance visits can carry

Three-monthly renal ultrasound to age 7 is already scheduled (Nakano 2024, PMID 39264246). Four additions cost no extra visit. Extend the scan to a full pelvic view including bladder wall and, in girls, vagina, because MVA rhabdomyosarcoma is characteristically genitourinary and botryoid (Schneider 2020, PMID 33209717). Add micronucleated reticulocytes by flow to blood already drawn (PMC3277805), with spot urine calcium to creatinine and cystatin C on the same sample. Take mid-upper arm circumference and weight there, not at a dietetic appointment. Buccal micronucleus swabs (J1) are family-collected. Surveillance must not stop at 7: an adult-onset MVA1 gastrointestinal phenotype exists (Rio Frio and Foulkes 2010, PMID 21190457).
