# Round 2, paediatric clinical pharmacologist

## (a) Objections

**CIN biologist.** Metformin first is an exposure error: their text has AICAR at 0.2 to 0.5 mM and metformin as the weaker activator, against plasma of 8 to 15 micromolar (e1). Second: rule 2 bars agents relieving energetic stress, yet AMPK activation is that response.

**Nephrologist.** The gate is right and I adopt it, but section 4 defers on 61 to 90% resolution by 12 to 24 months (PMID 16705005), a window now past. Second: they reject niacin and nicotinamide together, though the accumulation and thrombocytopenia data are nicotinamide's (A section 8).

**Endocrinologist.** Their rule bundles a hard label contraindication (mecasermin, FDA 021839s021) with a soft one (GH, Raman 2015 PMID 25839904), inviting dismissal of both. Second: "not cleared by lowering occupancy" rests on continuous-exposure imatinib data.

**Immunologist.** The mTOR exclusion rests on a harm they concede they cannot measure; my objection is to its rank, since item 3, vaccination, is the only one that moves an outcome. Second: excluding hydroxychloroquine on TLR7/9 antagonism overstates it; the limits are retinopathy and QT.

**Lab geneticist.** Their section 4 states the sign objection to aneugen avoidance better than I could. Second: T2 claims one RT-PCR returns phase and NMD efficiency together. It does not (e2).

**Predisposition oncologist.** The carve-out is right and I adopt it. Rank 2 raises metformin's tier to "human patient cells", but that result is AICAR, from a preprint G section 5 calls unpublished. Second: their F thresholds treat reversible and permanent harms alike.

**Family representative.** Their top candidate matches mine, so the objection is the one in (d). Rank 5 places niacin partly because a family "will meet it anyway", a reason to write about it, not to rank it.

## (c) Factual errors

CIN biologist rank 1: metformin is not "the only approved drug with a published direction of effect against aneuploid cells in patient material". That result is AICAR in Pallister-Killian fibroblasts (G section 5); Tang 2011's metformin data are mouse MEFs (PMID 21315436). The oncologist repeats it.

## (d) The disagreement not to paper over

Position A, CIN biologist with the oncologist and family representative: metformin leads the mechanism-directed candidates. Position B, mine: at any paediatric dose it falls 13 to 63 fold short of the concentration at which selection was shown. Settling experiment: metformin dose-response in Pallister-Killian or MVA fibroblasts at 5, 15, 50 and 500 micromolar, AICAR as control, aneuploid fraction by FISH. Selection at or below 15 micromolar refutes me.

## (e) Cross-checks

**(1) Does the arithmetic exclude metformin?** Phase I range 666 to 2000 mg/m2/day; at BSA 0.62 m2 that is 413 to 1240 mg/day, and creative I proposes about 400 mg/day. Metformin base MW 129.16, steady-state plasma Cmax about 1 to 2 mg/L, so 7.7 to 15.5 micromolar. AICAR acted at 200 to 500 micromolar (dossier 01 section 5). Ratio 13 to 63 fold. The plasma figure is label-level pharmacokinetics, mine, not a dossier's; OCT1-driven uptake favours liver, gut and kidney cortex, not muscle or fibroblast. So the arithmetic excludes metformin as a dose-matched AICAR substitute, which is how it is being used, but not outright, since Tang 2011 tested metformin itself and reported impaired accumulation of trisomy 13 and 16 cells at a concentration no round-1 file states. The Pallister-Killian result does not change this, being AICAR at AICAR concentrations. Verdict: demote to ex vivo dose-response first, do not exclude. Overturned by that concentration, or by selection in patient fibroblasts at 5 to 15 micromolar.

**(2) The single RT-PCR.** Not from one uninhibited reaction. The 796 nt separation sits inside a long-read amplicon, so molecules covering both sites give phase, but only if the c.2210 allele is in the mRNA. Efficient NMD deletes exactly those reads, so the expected result mimics cis. It works run paired, with and without a translation-inhibition NMD block, needing fibroblasts or a lymphoblastoid line, not blood. c.3006 escapes decay in the final exon, so the raw ratio also confounds NMD with allele-specific transcription. Cost: one RNA source plus amplicon sequencing, a trio-test order, the cell line the real expense. Outcomes: both variants on one molecule voids the list; the nonsense allele at 5 to 15% makes readthrough a combination question; near 50% means a truncated product may exist.

**(3) Should a rule outrank a drug?** Yes, conditionally. A rule carries near-zero added harm, so its expected value is positive at any nonzero exposure probability, while every drug must deliver a 22 to 64% relative cancer reduction to break even (F section 4). The condition is the carve-out, above the list in the same type size: "This list governs elective, non-oncological prescribing only. It does not apply to curative-intent or relapse chemotherapy. Vincristine, vinorelbine, dactinomycin, ifosfamide and irinotecan are given at full protocol dose and schedule. No reduction, substitution or omission within an oncology protocol may be made on the basis of this list (dossier 01 section 4.3)."

**(4) Is "no clone above about 10 percent" still usable?** No. Creative F section 2 bounds a single clonal trisomy in blood at about 4%, needs 21% burden if spread over five chromosomes, and is blind to a clone confined to kidney or muscle. It supports neither "too little aneuploidy to matter" nor "a euploid reserve exists to repopulate", which the CIN biologist's section 4 flags as unproven. Interphase FISH or single-cell sequencing must precede any aneuploidy-selective candidate, hence my conditional rank 3.
