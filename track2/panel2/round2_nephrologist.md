# Round 2, paediatric nephrologist

## (a) Objections

**CIN biologist.** Metformin at 1: his readout is FISH aneuploid fraction in blood, and his §4 concedes a clone confined to kidney or muscle is invisible at any fraction (F2), so the drug is dosed against a compartment he cannot see. Second: rule 2 bars chaperone inducers and rapalogs for relieving the stress that limits aneuploid cells, while rank 1 activates AMPK, the canonical survival response to energy stress. He names the risk and ranks it first anyway.

**Endocrinologist.** The no-somatotropin rule at 1 is right and changes nothing this month: nobody has offered this child growth hormone, while the scheduled exposures are cycle dexamethasone and shop vitamin D. Second: his item 4 gives vitamin D "to a measured level", while dossier 02 §3 records supplementation in hypercalciuria as unsafe (PMC7551195); treat documented deficiency only, daily rather than intermittent.

**Family representative.** HCTZ plus citrate at 1, ungated: her §5 says a work-up showing distal RTA or ifosfamide tubulopathy "would change candidate 1's drug, not its rank". It changes both: in proximal tubulopathy the alkali requirement is larger, phosphate replacement enters, and thiazide raises serum calcium in a polyuric child. Second: "all vitamin D and calcium supplements stop" reads across to dietary calcium in a kitchen, where dossier 02 §3 and file H §1 say keep age-appropriate calcium inside meals.

**Immunologist.** The mTOR exclusion at 1 is already carried by the label (proteinuria, delayed tubular recovery) and by the cancer risk; ranking it on an unmeasured clearance mechanism claims more than any MVA immune datum supports, as he concedes. Second: baricitinib is renally cleared with dose reduction at reduced filtration, so at this eGFR uncertainty it belongs off any list.

**Laboratory geneticist.** Aneugen avoidance at 1 names withholding curative chemotherapy as its own harm, yet flags taxanes and vincas without the carve-out inside the rule. Second: his rank 2 says a tubular work-up comes first, then ranks the drug regardless; either the work-up gates the prescription or it does not.

**PK pharmacologist.** HCTZ plus citrate at 1 with "no concentration gap to argue about" because the site is urine: thiazide acts at the distal convoluted tubule and depends on sodium and fluid delivery there, so in proximal tubulopathy urine output is not evidence the drug reached its transporter. Second claim: see (c).

**Predisposition oncologist.** The avoid-and-substitute rule at 1 carves out oncology only while barring loop diuretics outright; a child with fluid overload or acute kidney injury needs furosemide, and a rule read as a lifetime ban gets obeyed at the wrong moment. Second: amiloride does not "protect deliverability of the next line"; hyperkalaemia and acidosis on top of citrate are the likelier reason a drug is held.

## (b) Revised ranking

1. Work-up-gated hydrochlorothiazide plus potassium citrate. Unchanged; the laboratory geneticist reached the gate independently.
2. Renal and aneugen avoidance rule set, with the oncologist's carve-out wording extended to acute indications (e3).
3. Amiloride rather than more thiazide. Unchanged, minus the deliverability claim.
4. Phosphate plus alfacalcidol if ifosfamide Fanconi. Unchanged.
5. Cyproheptadine, new, from the endocrinologist, family representative and oncologist: renally neutral, no QT signal, reaches the endpoint the family names.
6. Metformin, demoted on the appetite and exposure arguments, conditional on an ex vivo arm and stable weight velocity.

Hydroxychloroquine leaves the list: QT stacking over thiazide hypokalaemia plus unguided retinal screening, for a cell-only hypothesis.

## (c) Factual error

The PK pharmacologist excludes metformin partly because OCT1 partitioning "concentrates it in liver and gut rather than muscle or kidney parenchyma". Metformin is cleared unchanged renally by OCT2 uptake at the proximal tubule basolateral membrane then MATE1 and MATE2-K secretion, which is why trimethoprim and cimetidine raise levels, as his file I row states. Renal cortex sees multiples of plasma. The muscle half stands; the kidney half is wrong.

## (d) The disagreement not to paper over

Position A (family representative, PK pharmacologist, endocrinologist item 4): start thiazide plus citrate now, licensed and cheap, the only thing reaching the child this month. Position B (this seat, and the laboratory geneticist's rationale): prescribe nothing until one tubular panel returns, because two plausible causes make thiazide the wrong drug and one makes it harmful. Experiment: one visit, serum and paired urine for calcium, phosphate, bicarbonate, magnesium, creatinine, cystatin C, urine pH and low molecular weight protein, plus the neonatal drug record. Hypercalciuria with normocalcaemia and normal tubular phosphate reabsorption confirms A; low phosphate reabsorption with proximal acidosis moves treatment to alkali and phosphate.

## (e) Cross-checks

**1.** The right objection applied to the wrong pair. AICAR's active range does not set metformin's, and Tang 2011 reported metformin qualitatively, without a killing curve, so its effective concentration is unknown rather than unreachable. Tissue beats plasma: the proximal tubule concentrates metformin (see c), muscle does not. The Pallister-Killian result was AICAR only, so it raises the value of running that arm rather than the drug's rank. Metformin stays, conditional, at 6.

**2.** The assay design is outside my competence; I defer on whether one amplicon carries 796 nt at single-molecule depth. Two points from this seat: phase can also be settled by parental testing, needing no RNA logistics, so the panel should say which route it wants; and blood is the accessible tissue while the phenotype sits in kidney and muscle, so a normal blood result closes nothing.

**3.** Yes for the renal entries: they govern prescriptions otherwise written by clinicians who never see another MVA case, at no added risk. Wording, above the list in the same size type: "This list governs elective, non-oncological prescribing. It does not alter any curative-intent oncology protocol. It does not withhold a loop diuretic, corticosteroid, antibiotic or antiemetic given for an acute indication where no equivalent alternative exists. Any change to a cancer regimen is made by the treating oncologist, not by this list."

**4.** No. The WGS negative bounds a single clonal trisomy in blood near 4% and a variegated burden barely at all (F2), and is blind to a clone confined to one organ. Kidney is such an organ and carries the documented lesion. The premise describes one accessible tissue, not the child, so no candidate resting on "little to remove" or "much to remove" can cite it. Interphase FISH or single-cell sequencing comes first.
