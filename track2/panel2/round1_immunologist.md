# Round 1, clinical immunologist (panel 2)

Lens: cytosolic DNA sensing, NK and T cell surveillance of aneuploid and senescent cells, immunosuppressant harm.

**There is no measured immune phenotype in MVA1.** A PubMed search on 5 Sep 2026 for BUB1B with immunodeficiency, lymphopenia or immune returned zero records; "mosaic variegated aneuploidy" with the same terms returned eight, the only one with recurrent infection being a double-homozygous CEP57 plus DYNC2H1 patient in whom the infections are not attributable to MVA (PMID 31943948). No subset panel, immunoglobulin level, vaccine response or NK cytotoxicity measurement exists for any MVA patient (dossier 01 section 7, dossier 02 section 6). The "weakened immunity" in the one public family account (dossier 03 section 2, source S2) sits in a narrative that also contains prematurity, open heart surgery and chemotherapy. It is not evidence of a primary immune defect.

**The mechanism runs the opposite way from an immunodeficiency.** Mis-segregation produces micronuclei that rupture and expose DNA to cGAS, driving STING-dependent type I interferon and NF-kB output (PMID 28738408; PMID 28759889; PMID 29342134); it also generates arrested cells with senescence features and pro-inflammatory signalling that recruits immune clearance (Santaguida 2017, PMID 28633018), and senescent aneuploid cells are cleared by NK cells (PMC8339690, in creative J idea 2). **[inference]** That sensing should be tonic here, and clearance is a candidate rate-limiting step.

## 1. Ranked list

**1. Avoidance rule: no chronic mTOR inhibitor (sirolimus, everolimus, temsirolimus) as a repurposing candidate.** Step: NK and T cell clearance of aneuploid and senescent cells. Tier: cell and mouse for the clearance step (PMID 28633018; PMC8339690), human label for the harm (creative E rows 9 to 11 carry immunosuppression, malignancy, myelosuppression and nephrotoxicity flags on all three). Paediatric status: everolimus approved from age 1 for TSC-SEGA, sirolimus off-label at 0.8 mg/m2 twice daily (dossier 02 section 5; creative I). Phenotype: infection and mucositis risk on top of failure to thrive, and a proteinuria warning with nephrocalcinosis present. Harm: suppressing the arm that removes the mis-segregated cells, against a residual cancer risk of order 39 to 75% (dossier 02 section 1; creative F section 4). Readout: NK count, CD107a degranulation against K562, CD4 count and micronucleus frequency, before and 12 weeks after exposure. Rank: it removes candidates other lenses rank first and needs no trial.

**2. Gate rule: no immunomodulator without a measured interferon signature and a full immunophenotype, done twice.** Step: cGAS-STING output. Tier: cell and mouse for mechanism, human for the assay (Rice 2013, PMID 24183309, six-gene whole-blood score). Paediatric status: routine, two EDTA tubes. Harm: acting on a single score. Readout: the score in the child and both carrier parents, plus immunoglobulins, pneumococcal and tetanus titres, T, B and NK subsets, micronucleus frequency. Rank: it decides whether items 4, 5 and 6 are candidates or errors.

**3. Vaccination and serology rule.** Step: adaptive protection against the infections the family reports. Drugs: approved inactivated paediatric vaccines including annual influenza and PCV, with post-vaccination serology; live vaccines withheld during any immunosuppressant here. Tier: human in other populations, inference for MVA. Phenotype: chemotherapy history sets revaccination timing. Harm: false reassurance if titres go unchecked. Readout: protective titres at 4 to 6 weeks; infection days per year. Rank: the only licensed item that moves the outcome the family names.

**4. Immunoglobulin replacement, conditional.** Step: antibody-mediated defence. Drug: IVIG or SCIG, approved paediatric, 0.4 to 0.6 g/kg every 3 to 4 weeks, only on documented hypogammaglobulinaemia or failed specific responses from item 2. Tier: human in primary antibody deficiency, none in MVA. Phenotype: non-sucrose iso-osmolar product at a slow rate, since sucrose-containing IVIG is associated with acute kidney injury. Harm: renal, thrombosis, volume, masking of later serology. Readout: IgG trough, infection days. Rank: item 2 decides whether it is a candidate at all.

**5. Dasatinib, intermittently only, with baseline NK and T counts.** Step: senolysis. Tier: mouse for schedule, adult human for dose (PMID 29988130; NCT04733534); no paediatric senolytic dose exists (dossier 02 section 6). Paediatric status: approved from age 1 for Ph+ CML, oral suspension 10 mg/mL (creative I). Phenotype: labelled growth warning, QT stacking with ondansetron, absorption cut by acid suppression. Immunological harm: dasatinib inhibits LCK and SRC-family kinases and blocks TCR and NK activating signalling within hours. Continuous exposure is a sustained surveillance deficit, a pulse a transient one. Readout: T cell p16 transcript, NK count and CD107a at trough and 72 h, height velocity and bone age at 6 and 12 months. Rank: the objection to creative B idea 6 is to the schedule, not a veto.

**6. Baricitinib, conditional, time-limited, last.** Step: JAK1/2 downstream of the type I interferon receptor. Tier: human in a different interferonopathy (PMID 29649002). Paediatric status: EMA-approved from 2 years for juvenile idiopathic arthritis and atopic dermatitis, oral suspension available (EMA EPAR Olumiant, checked 5 Sep 2026). Phenotype: renal dose adjustment, cystatin C rather than creatinine at low muscle mass. Harm: JAK1/2 blockade removes IFN-gamma signalling and IL-15-driven NK maturation, the arm implicated in clearing aneuploid cells; the class carries malignancy and infection warnings on the label. Readout: interferon score at 0, 4 and 12 weeks with a pre-declared stopping rule, plus NK count. Rank: last, and a candidate only if item 2 returns a high score with attributable inflammatory morbidity.

## 2. Creative ideas adopted and rejected

- Adopt **B1** (interferon score first): correct gate, correct assay, stops before prescribing.
- Adopt **B6 with a modification**: the hit-and-run argument holds, but omits the acute T and NK blockade, which is what the pulse buys back.
- Adopt **J2 inverted**: protect clearance rather than raise it; no approved lever raises NK function, and raising it would strip the p16-high muscle and fat compartment J itself names.
- Adopt **C1 and C2**: the score in both carrier parents is the specificity control for item 2.
- Adopt **F section 3**: a micronucleus endpoint needs 12-week periods, which my readouts inherit.
- Reject **B7** (lamivudine): the cytosolic DNA here is micronuclear, not retroelement-derived, so the drug acts on the wrong source, as B states itself.
- Reject **A5** (arimoclomol, teprenone): HSF1 overexpression is the aneuploidy-tolerating mutation in human cells (PMID 25205676), so it raises the fitness of the pre-malignant compartment.
- Reject **E rows 1, 2, 5 and 8** (trilaciclib, abemaciclib, ribociclib, palbociclib): trilaciclib's mechanism is deliberate transient marrow arrest, and CDK4/6 blockade suppresses lymphocyte proliferation.

## 3. Drugs excluded

Hydroxychloroquine: TLR7 and TLR9 antagonism plus antigen-presentation blockade, with retinopathy and QT commitments (creative I), for the effect Tang 2011 called the weakest of three tested. Tocilizumab and IL-6 blockade: no MVA data, and it removes CRP as the infection signal in a child who will have febrile neutropenia. Interferon-alpha and STING agonists: wrong direction. Systemic corticosteroids beyond replacement. G-CSF outside protocol-defined neutropenia: an MVA1 patient developed MDS with monosomy 7 at age 9 (PMC6500003), and chronic marrow stimulation in a checkpoint-deficient compartment is the exposure I would least want on the record. Live vaccines during any of the above.

## 4. Strongest objection to my own top candidate

The mTOR exclusion rests on label-level immunosuppression and an inferred clearance mechanism, with no NK or T cell measurement in any MVA patient. Against it: monoallelic BubR1 sarcopenia correlates with mTORC1 hyperactivity (Sieben 2020, PMID 31738183), TOR depletion dampened damage in the Drosophila CIN microcephaly model (creative G, PMID 41820377), and low-dose intermittent rapamycin improves rather than suppresses vaccine responses in older adults. It may discard the candidate with the most animal support on the basis of a harm I cannot measure, so it reads as a hold pending item 2, not a permanent bar.

## 5. Evidence that would change the ranking

An interferon score and NK cytotoxicity measurement in MVA1 patients and carriers. A subset, immunoglobulin and vaccine-response panel in more than one patient, settling whether "weakened immunity" is a phenotype or a narrative. Any patient-cell demonstration that NK clearance is rate-limiting for aneuploid burden, which would turn item 1 from a hold into a bar. An infection-rate denominator across the roughly 40 known patients, which the UK MVA National Audit (creative G lead 10) could supply.
