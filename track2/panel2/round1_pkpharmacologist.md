# Round 1, paediatric clinical pharmacologist (PK lens)

Panel 2, written without reading track2/panel/. Reference child 15 kg (creative I). Muscle atrophy (HP:0003202) makes creatinine eGFR overread, so renal rules key to cystatin C (dossier 02 section 3). From D section 6: CYP3A5 *3/*3 non-expressor, G6PD normal, MT-RNR1 clean. My test: does exposure at a defensible dose reach the concentration at which the cited effect was seen, and does it survive the RMS backbone and its antiemetics?

## 1. Ranked candidates

**1. Hydrochlorothiazide plus potassium citrate, amiloride as conditional add-on.** Step: distal tubular calcium reabsorption, not the BubR1 axis. Tier: human other (PMID 11433085; amiloride in adult stone formers, B section 3). HCTZ 0.5 to 1 mg/kg/day from 6.25 mg twice daily; citrate 0.3 to 0.6 mmol/kg/day (creative I). Exposure: the site of action is urine and the dose is titrated against measured output, so no concentration gap exists to argue about, uniquely in this list. Harm: hypokalaemia feeding the ondansetron QT stack, volume contraction poorly tolerated at failure to thrive. Readout: urine calcium-to-creatinine ratio. Rank: exposure certainty, and a finding documented in this child rather than in a mouse.

**2. Hydroxychloroquine, the aneuploidy-selective autophagy arm.** Step: lysosomal and autophagic degradation, on which aneuploid cells depend (Tang 2011 PMID 21315436, cell; Santaguida 2015 PMID 26404941, cell). Dose 5 mg/kg, 75 mg daily as 25 mg/mL (creative I). Exposure: whole blood at 5 mg/kg is low micromolar and the drug is lysosomotropic, so concentration at the site of action exceeds plasma by orders of magnitude (inference). G6PD normal removes the haemolysis bar. Harm: retinopathy; QT with the ondansetron in every VAC or IVA cycle, over thiazide hypokalaemia; and a directional conflict, since activating autophagy protected neural stem cells in a Drosophila CIN microcephaly model (G section 5). Rank: the only mechanism-directed candidate that plausibly reaches its cited concentration here.

**3. A written avoidance rule set, dispensed as a pharmacy alert.** Approved aneugens (colchicine, griseofulvin, albendazole, mebendazole) act where the genotype is already weak (dossier 01 section 4.3; A idea 10). Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors in established nephrocalcinosis (PMC5976810). Loop diuretics, the strongest risk factor for preterm nephrocalcinosis (B idea 2). Acid suppression with dasatinib, which cuts AUC 43 to 61% (creative I). Tier: human genotoxicity and label data, inference for transfer to this host. Rank: on harm-benefit it outranks 4 to 6, but it is a rule, not a hypothesis.

**4. Niacin, the approved route to NAD+ repletion.** Step: SIRT2 deacetylates BubR1 at K668 and blocks its degradation; the SIRT2 transgene extended BubR1H/H median lifespan 58%, 123% in males and none in females (North 2014 PMID 24825348, BubR1 model). Tier: human other for the NAD+ effect (750 to 1000 mg/day raised blood NAD+ eightfold and muscle NAD+ to control level, PMID 32386566), inference for the BubR1 link. Exposure: 14 mg/kg/day there, so about 210 mg/day at 15 kg, from 25 mg upward, immediate release only, with no paediatric dose for this indication. Nicotinamide is the wrong salt (A section 8). Rank: what was shown was a transgene, not repletion.

**5. Sirolimus, if an mTORC1 arm is pursued at all.** Step: mTORC1, hyperactive in BubR1 carriers with sarcopenia predisposition (Sieben 2020 PMID 31738183). Dose 0.8 mg/m2 twice daily, trough 10 to 15 ng/mL (Adams 2016 PMID 26783326). CYP3A5 *3/*3 gives standard weight-based dosing, which matters less than the co-medications: an azole for febrile neutropenia moves the trough several fold overnight, aprepitant inhibits CYP3A4, dexamethasone induces. Direction unresolved, since rapamycin improves aneuploid yeast fitness (dossier 02 section 5). Harm: immunosuppression against a 39 to 75% cancer risk. Rank: dose-predictable, sign unestablished.

**6. Dasatinib, 3 days per month, as a senolytic pulse.** Step: senescent-cell clearance, which improved sarcopenia, fat loss and cataract in BubR1H/H mice by genetic INK-ATTAC clearance, not by a drug (Baker 2011 PMID 22048312). Dose 40 mg daily at 10 to under 20 kg as oral suspension (creative I). Exposure objection: the human data are dasatinib plus quercetin (Hickson 2019), and the in vitro work pairs high nanomolar to low micromolar dasatinib with tens of micromolar quercetin, a supplement that is out of scope. The approved half alone has never cleared senescent cells in humans (inference). Harm: labelled growth warning, QT, and P-gp inhibition raising vincristine neuropathy. Rank: last, on exposure.

## N-of-1 skeletons, top two

**Amiloride added to established HCTZ plus citrate.** Randomised-order ABAB crossover, four 6-week periods, no washout needed for a urinary endpoint. Dose 0.4 to 0.6 mg/kg/day, capped at 10 mg. Primary endpoint: second-void urine calcium-to-creatinine ratio, three samples per period. Negative control filed first: height velocity over the same 24 weeks (C idea 12). Stop for potassium above 5.3 mmol/L, bicarbonate below 18, or a cystatin C eGFR fall above 25%. Not within 4 weeks of ifosfamide.

**Hydroxychloroquine.** Baseline, treatment and withdrawal, 12 weeks each with a 4-week washout, about 11 months, because the endpoint tracks a slow-turnover pool (F section 3). Dose 75 mg daily, 4 mg/kg below a cystatin C eGFR of 45. Primary endpoint: micronucleated reticulocytes per 1e6 by flow, monthly; secondary, FISH aneuploid fraction on 1000 nuclei, minimum detectable effect 22% (F section 3b). Pre-gate: the child's own stimulated lymphocytes at 1 to 10 micromolar, threshold declared first (C idea 2). Stop for QTc above 480 ms, potassium below 3.3, or any retinal change.

A idea 4, caffeine citrate, needs supratherapeutic concentrations. A ideas 5 and 6 with B idea 8, phenylbutyrate and chaperone induction, because D section 4 puts N1002 partially exposed. B idea 10, dropping vinorelbine, discards a randomised survival benefit (PMID 31562043).

## 3. Drugs excluded

Metformin, on arithmetic. AICAR acted at 200 to 500 micromolar (Tang 2011); metformin at usual paediatric doses gives plasma near 8 to 15 micromolar, and OCT1 partitioning concentrates it in liver and gut rather than muscle or kidney parenchyma, so the gap widens (inference). It is also cleared renally through OCT2 and MATE, which trimethoprim inhibits, in a child whose creatinine eGFR overreads. Aminoglycosides: the mitochondrial bar is gone, the nephrotoxicity bar is not. Growth hormone and mecasermin: no MVA efficacy, label contraindication. Proteasome inhibitors: block mitotic exit in a checkpoint-weak child. HSP90 inhibitors: wrong direction for this missense allele. Low-dose paclitaxel: Janssen 2009 (PMID 19855003) measured sensitisation in this genotype class.

## 4. Strongest objection to my top candidate

It is standard nephrology care, not a repurposing hypothesis, and does nothing to BubR1, aneuploidy or cancer risk, so ranking it first reads as declining the question. My defence: no source establishes nephrocalcinosis as an MVA feature (dossier 02 section 1), so it is worked up on its own merits, and its exposure at the site of action is the only measurable one here.

## 5. What would change the ranking

An RNA sample, converting every NMD statement from prediction to measurement (D section 7). A functional assay on p.Asn1002Lys separating the destabilising from the specific-defect class, which decides whether chaperone-directed agents belong here. A metformin arm in the Pallister-Killian AICAR experiment (G section 5): a positive result at achievable plasma concentrations moves it to the top three. Within-patient variance for both endpoints over 12 months untreated (C idea 10), without which they are unpowered by assumption.
