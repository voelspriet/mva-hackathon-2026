# Re-analysis of the primary data: what was asked, what was found, what is left

Run under `track2/audit/PROMPT_B_reanalysis.md`, after the audit round and both cross-model passes. Every angle below was computed from data already in hand. No patient data left this machine; gnomAD was read as a static public file by byte range, never queried with a patient coordinate.

## Computed, with the answer

Ordered by what the answer changes.

| # | Question | Answer | Changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| R7 | What does the whole genome look like against the whole of ClinVar, with no gene list at all? | **Seven heterozygous hits, nothing homozygous, no gene hit twice, so no second diagnosis.** One is the known *BUB1B* allele and serves as the positive control. One, *PRSS1* rs202003805, is an artefact and is discarded: allele balance 0.147 in a known mismapping region, and gnomAD reports frequency 0.386 with zero homozygotes in 59,319 genomes where Hardy-Weinberg predicts about 8,839. Three are ordinary recessive carrier states with no partner allele. Two matter and are written up in `R7_genomewide_clinvar.md`: a common *RBM8A* hypomorph in the EJC core, and a rare pathogenic nonsense in *LZTR1*. | Corrects one sentence in the report, resolves audit row fg.B54, and adds no recommendation. Closes the gap R5 named in its own limitations, "any gene outside the 80" |
| R5 | Is there a modifier in the checkpoint, cohesion or decay machinery? | **No.** Three rare coding variants outside *BUB1B* across the wider gene set, of which two are missense (SGO1 p.Thr425Ala, ANAPC2 p.Gly297Asp) and one synonymous and likely benign. **Scan parameters, stated because the earlier wording did not give them**: PASS calls inside coding sequence and absent from dbSNP, at genotype quality 50 or more and depth 10 or more, with absence from dbSNP as the rarity proxy and gnomAD frequency checked only for the hits, against the 80 genes in `work/track2/genes_network.bed`. **Not covered**: non-coding variants, copy number, per-gene callable coverage, and any gene outside the 80. "Closes the digenic hypothesis" is therefore too broad and is narrowed to: no rare coding variant supports a digenic model within that gene set. **Of the 80-gene checkpoint and decay network specifically, only ANAPC2 is a member**, so that network carries 1 rare coding variant where its coding length predicts 9. It is depleted, not enriched. The counts differ because they are over different gene sets. | Converts the report's assumption that no rare variant exists in the decay machinery into a computation. Closes the digenic hypothesis. |
| R6 | Are the bins the mosaicism scan discarded, and the outliers it recorded but never read, informative? | **No, and the method has a hole.** 56 bins exceed the threshold; all imply cell fractions above 1.0, which is impossible, and all sit in heterochromatin, acrocentric arms, centromeres or the MHC. | The 11.7 percent per-bin detection floor is a genome average that does not hold in these regions. The method needs a mappability filter. The negative result is unaffected. |
| R1 | Can phase be read out of the existing VCF? | **No.** Neither variant carries a phase set, and the library actually sequenced cannot bridge 10.9 kb: the longest read-backed phase set anywhere in this genome spans 288 bp. An earlier version said no short-read library could join them, which overstates it, since long-insert mate-pair libraries exist. The correct statement is that **this** library cannot, so the raw reads from it would not answer phase either. | Confirms the gate's first item cannot be shortcut. Removes the raw-read download as a route to phase. |
| R2 | Runs of homozygosity, consanguinity, uniparental disomy? | **Nine short runs, 10.9 Mb in total, longest 1.8 Mb, none on chromosome 15**, on PASS calls; two earlier versions gave zero runs and then five over 6.7 Mb, the first from an insensitive metric and the second from a query without the PASS filter. An earlier version reported zero runs at any threshold; that was produced by an insensitive metric, the ratio of heterozygous to total calls rather than absolute heterozygous density, and is corrected in `track2/anomalies/EXTERNAL_READ_RESPONSE.md`. The conclusion is unchanged: 6.7 Mb of short runs is ordinary for an outbred genome, against runs above 10 Mb and totals above 50 Mb at first-cousin consanguinity. | Excludes isodisomy and consanguinity. Heterodisomy remains untestable without parents. |
| R3 | Copy number from depth? | **Nothing but mapping artefact.** Every outlier window is a centromere or an acrocentric short arm. On PASS calls, 11 windows below 0.7x and 19 above 1.4x, against 50 and 14 before filtering. | Excludes a deletion spanning *BUB1B*, at megabase resolution only. |
| R4 | The mitochondrial genome? | **Eleven PASS variants of thirteen called, all at or near homoplasmy.** The two that fail filtering, at positions 4769 and 8860, are near-universal differences from the reference. Highest minor fraction 2.1 percent, at 16093 in the control region, confirmed on PASS calls. Coverage 1,700 to 5,200 reads. | Removes a second explanation for the growth and muscle phenotype, and the drug contraindications that would follow it. |

**The pattern across all seven is negative.** That is the useful part. Seven routes by which the Track 1 answer could have been wrong or incomplete are now closed by computation rather than by not having looked. R7 is the only one that returned anything at all, and what it returned changes a reason rather than a conclusion.

## What this does not do

It does not change any recommendation in `REPORT_track2.md`. Seven negatives and one methodological hole are the whole of it. R7 corrects the stated *reason* for one assumption without changing the assumption, and it declines to turn its one novel finding into advice. The one place the report should be touched is the mosaicism detection floor, which is now known to be a genome-average figure that fails in low-mappability regions, and that qualification belongs beside the figure.
