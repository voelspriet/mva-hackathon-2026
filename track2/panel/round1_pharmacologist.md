# Round 1: paediatric clinical pharmacology

Lens: paediatric formulation and dose, dosing under nephrocalcinosis and possibly reduced GFR, interaction with thiazide, potassium citrate, antiepileptics and chemotherapy, safety record in children, and a monitored n-of-1 design. Age and weight are not given, so each candidate is judged on whether it can be given below age 10 at all. Two constraints run throughout: muscle atrophy with failure to thrive makes creatinine-based eGFR an overestimate, so cystatin C or measured clearance precedes any renally cleared drug (02, §3, [Inferred] there); and mg/kg dosing on low lean mass underdoses hydrophilic and overdoses lipophilic drugs (inference). Citations are by dossier file and section.

## 1. Hydroxychloroquine (or chloroquine)

**Mechanism, tier.** Lysosomal alkalinisation blocking autophagic flux. Aneuploid cells carry lysosomal and proteotoxic stress, activate TFEB and depend on autophagic degradation (01, §5; Santaguida 2015, PMID 26404941); chloroquine was one of three aneuploidy-selective compounds in Tang 2011 (PMID 21315436), there the weakest. Cell model, plus inference for the chloroquine-to-hydroxychloroquine substitution.
**Dose, phenotype, harm.** 5 to 6.5 mg/kg/day in paediatric rheumatology, capped at 5 mg/kg actual body weight and 400 mg/day (02, §5); tablets only, extemporaneous suspension routine; about a fifth renally cleared with no adjustment until advanced impairment, the least GFR-sensitive candidate here. No calcium or citrate handling effect and no spindle action, so no competition with candidate 2 and no added aneugenic risk. Harm: retinopathy occurs in children with no paediatric screening guideline, and screening needs cooperation with fields and OCT (02, §5), which developmental delay can make impossible; QT effect adds to azoles or ondansetron.
**Readout, rank.** Micronuclei per 1,000 binucleated lymphocytes (OECD TG 487, 01, §4.3) at baseline and 12 weeks, p16^INK4a in PBMC secondary (NCT04733534, 02, §6). First, as the only candidate tied to measured aneuploid-cell biology that is also giveable to a small child at a defined mg/kg dose.

## 2. Thiazide plus potassium citrate

**Mechanism, tier.** Distal tubular calcium reabsorption and urinary citrate repletion; not a BubR1 mechanism. Human other, including arrest of nephrocalcinosis progression in X-linked hypophosphataemia (Seikaly 2001, PMID 11433085). Nephrocalcinosis has no published MVA1 frequency, so it needs its own workup (02, §1, §3).
**Dose, harm.** Hydrochlorothiazide 0.5 to 1 mg/kg/day in two divided doses, usual 12.5 to 25 mg/day; potassium citrate 0.1 to 0.2 g/kg/day against urine pH, held below 7.5 (02, §3). Hypokalaemia and volume depletion are less well tolerated with failure to thrive, partly offset by the citrate salt; prior ifosfamide would leave a proximal tubulopathy changing urine chemistry and the choice of alkali (02, §3).
**Readout, rank.** Urine calcium/creatinine ratio, citrate, potassium, bicarbonate, renal ultrasound at baseline and 6 months. Second, as the only entry with directed paediatric evidence and a hard endpoint; preserved GFR is a precondition for everything else.

## 3. Metformin

**Mechanism, tier.** AMPK activation standing in for AICAR, which killed trisomic MEFs at 0.2 to 0.5 mM; metformin impaired accumulation of trisomy 13 and 16 cells in the same work (01, §5; 02, §5). Cell model, and the AICAR-to-metformin substitution is labelled inference in 01, §7.
**Dose, harm.** Approved from age 10 in type 2 diabetes; oral solution 500 mg/5 mL, 500 mg twice daily to a maximum 2,000 mg/day ([Riomet label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=05d4df4b-dfe8-4828-b423-a3d4f2c4114a), web check 1). Below 10 it is off-label, with a paediatric oncology phase I at 666 to 2,000 mg/m²/day as precedent (02, §5). Renally cleared with lactic acidosis the labelled risk: nephrocalcinosis, unreliable eGFR and dehydration during illness aim that risk at this phenotype and compound thiazide volume depletion, so sick-day rules and a hold before contrast imaging are required.
**Readout, rank.** Micronuclei and lymphocyte aneuploidy fraction, with lactate and bicarbonate as safety. Third: an oral liquid and a paediatric dosing precedent, against an inferred mechanism and a renal risk pointed at this kidney.

## 4. Dasatinib, intermittent senolytic monotherapy

**Mechanism, tier.** Senescent-cell clearance. p16-positive cell ablation improved muscle fibre diameter, fat mass and treadmill distance in BubR1^H/H mice (01, §4.1; Baker 2011, PMID 22048312), but that was genetic and no senolytic drug has been given to a BubR1 mouse (01, §7). Inference from a genetic experiment.
**Dose, harm.** Approved from age 1 for Ph+ chronic-phase CML, with a powder for oral suspension at 72 mg/m² approved alongside the tablet in November 2017 ([OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/fda-approves-dasatinib-for-pediatric-ph-chronic-phase-cml), web check 2); weight-band tablets 40 to 100 mg daily (02, §5). Quercetin, the partner in every published D+Q regimen, is a supplement and out of scope, so this is monotherapy at a senolytic dose nobody has established. The label warns on effects on growth, in a child with short stature and failure to thrive, and myelosuppression or bleeding would confound the surveillance.
**Readout, rank.** p16 in PBMC and senescence-associated secretory markers at 60 days (NCT04733534). Fourth: the most BubR1-specific rationale here, on the drug least giveable to this child.

## Excluded

Everolimus and sirolimus: the mTORC1 correlation in BubR1 mice (01, §3) is offset by aneuploid cells growing better under rapamycin (02, §5), by stomatitis 43% and mouth ulceration 32% in a child failing to thrive (EXIST-1, 02, §5), and by lost exposure with enzyme-inducing antiepileptics, which 45% of MVA1 children may need (02, §1, §5); the dispersible paediatric formulation with trough monitoring is the one argument for it. Growth hormone: no efficacy in MVA2, no MVA1 data, background cancer risk of order 40 to 75% (02, §2). Mecasermin: contraindicated with prior malignancy (02, §5). Acetazolamide and topiramate: carbonic anhydrase inhibition drives hypercalciuria, which also constrains any antiepileptic choice (02, §3, §5). Vitamin D above replacement: unsafe with hypercalciuria (02, §3). Hydroxyurea: raises micronucleated reticulocytes, which is the readout (02, §5). Colchicine and griseofulvin: approved aneugens (01, §4.3). Taxanes: SAC-weakened cells are sensitised and errors amplified (01, §4.3). Quercetin, fisetin, NMN, nicotinamide riboside, AICAR, 17-AAG, navitoclax, KIF18A inhibitors: not approved. Statins: no evidence either way, plus myopathy risk (02, §5).

## Strongest objection to my top candidate

Tang 2011 tested chloroquine, not hydroxychloroquine, in engineered trisomic mouse cells at concentrations not obviously reachable at 5 mg/kg/day in a child, and it was the weakest of three hits. The benefit crosses a molecule change, a species change and an unverified exposure, while the retinopathy risk is concrete and here possibly unmonitorable. A defensible reading puts candidate 2 first.

## N-of-1 protocol, candidate 1

Single patient, open label, prospectively registered, per the standards in 03, §5 (Defelippe 2023, PMID 37356616; Jonker 2025, PMID 40438698). Three baselines over 8 weeks establish within-child variance before dosing. Hydroxychloroquine 5 mg/kg/day actual body weight, once daily, extemporaneous suspension, no titration, 24 weeks, with withdrawal and 12-week re-challenge if the endpoint moves. Primary endpoint: micronuclei per 1,000 binucleated lymphocytes, blinded scorer, pre-specified as a 30% fall from the baseline mean. Secondary: p16^INK4a in PBMC, weight-for-age z-score, hospital days. Stop for retinal change on 12-weekly OCT, QTc above 480 ms, a GFR fall over 25%, a new malignancy, or micronuclei above baseline. Candidate 2 and the surveillance schedule continue unchanged.

## What would change my ranking

Measured residual BubR1 protein in this child's lymphocytes, which no MVA paper reports as a percentage (01, §2.2), would say whether the cell sits near the level where mis-segregation is tolerated or where added stress is lethal. A functional assay sorting p.Asn1002Lys into the destabilising or the specific-defect class matters for anything acting on protein stability. A senolytic dosed in a BubR1 mouse with a healthspan endpoint moves dasatinib up. Chloroquine or hydroxychloroquine on MVA patient cells at achievable concentrations moves candidate 1 to first with evidence, or off the list. An identified cause for the nephrocalcinosis turns candidate 2 from empirical into directed and sets the ceiling for every renally cleared drug.
