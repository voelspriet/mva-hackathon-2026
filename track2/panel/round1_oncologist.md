# Round 1: paediatric oncologist (rhabdomyosarcoma and cancer-predisposition clinic)

Lens: a child with treated embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma, three-monthly surveillance, nephrocalcinosis, low muscle mass, failure to thrive and a constitutional spindle-checkpoint defect. I rank by what I could deliver in clinic and what survives a relapse requiring chemotherapy. Nothing here treats MVA1 itself; BubR1 loss of function is not druggable (02_clinical_care_drugs.md §7).

## 1. Ranked candidates

**1. Hydrochlorothiazide plus potassium citrate.** Target: distal tubular calcium reabsorption and urinary citrate. The link is to the phenotype, not to BubR1: nephrocalcinosis is the organ constraint governing every future cytotoxic decision here. Tier: human other (thiazides arrest nephrocalcinosis progression in X-linked hypophosphataemia, PMID 11433085; 02 §3). Paediatric: HCTZ 0.5-1 mg/kg/day in two doses, potassium citrate 0.1-0.2 g/kg/day, standard practice (02 §3). Interaction: ifosfamide, hyperhydrated cyclophosphamide and platinum all demand renal reserve, and with low muscle mass creatinine-based eGFR overreads function, so dose on cystatin C (02 §3, inference). Harm: hypokalaemia and volume depletion in a child with failure to thrive; urine pH above 7.5 precipitates calcium phosphate. Readout: urine calcium/creatinine ratio, citrate, potassium, bicarbonate, renal ultrasound at baseline and 6 months (PMC5976810). Rank: the only candidate with a paediatric dose, a monitored endpoint, and a direct effect on whether this child can be treated if the sarcoma returns.

**2. Dexrazoxane, if an anthracycline is used.** Target: topoisomerase IIβ and iron-mediated cardiac injury. Link: BubR1-insufficient mice die of cardiac failure even after senescent-cell clearance improves every other outcome (Baker 2011, PMID 22048312), and BubR1^H/L1002P mice show low cardiac stress tolerance and an ECG J-point defect (Sieben 2020, PMID 31738183; 01 §4.1). Tier: BubR1 mouse for the vulnerability, human paediatric oncology for the drug. Paediatric: approved, 10:1 with doxorubicin, which European IVA-based high-risk RMS adds (02 §4). Harm: additive myelosuppression and the unresolved second-malignancy question. Readout: serial echocardiographic global longitudinal strain and troponin. Rank: conditional on relapse, but the mechanism-to-organ link is the tightest here and the drug is already in paediatric sarcoma practice.

**3. Metformin.** Target: AMPK, as the approved stand-in for AICAR, which killed trisomic MEFs at 0.2-0.5 mM through p53-Ser15-Bax (Tang 2011, PMID 21315436; 01 §5). Tier: cell model, and the AICAR-to-metformin substitution is explicitly untested (01 §7, inference). Paediatric: approved from age 10; paediatric oncology phase I at 666-2000 mg/m²/day with vincristine, irinotecan, temozolomide (PMC9972017). Interaction and harm: lactic acidosis risk rises with reduced GFR, which nephrocalcinosis plus prior alkylators makes non-theoretical, and it removes calories from a child who has none to spare. Readout: micronucleus frequency in blood lymphocytes and mosaic aneuploid fraction by interphase FISH or low-pass single-cell sequencing at baseline, 6 and 12 months, with weight-for-age as the safety endpoint. Rank: cheapest hypothesis with a real mechanism, but two inferential steps from the data.

**4. Hydroxychloroquine.** Target: lysosomal acidification and autophagic flux, on which aneuploid cells depend (Tang 2011; Santaguida 2015, PMID 26404941). Tier: cell model. Paediatric: rheumatology dosing 5 mg/kg actual body weight (02 §5). Interaction: MVA carries cataract and other ocular anomalies at high frequency (García-Castillo 2008, PMID 18548531), so the dose-limiting toxicity lands on an organ already at risk and there is no paediatric retinopathy screening guideline. Readout: as for metformin, plus annual OCT and fields where age allows. Rank: below metformin because the toxicity collides with the syndrome's own eye phenotype.

**5. Cyclophosphamide with mesna in place of ifosfamide (VAC rather than IVA).** Target: the same alkylating backbone without chloroacetaldehyde proximal tubulopathy. Tier: human other; both regimens are standard of care with comparable outcomes (COG 2023 Blueprint, PMC10519430; 02 §4). Interaction: the decision that most protects an already calcified kidney, and it preserves the vinorelbine plus low-dose oral cyclophosphamide maintenance that moved 5-year overall survival from 72.4% to 85.0% in RMS 2005 (Bisogno 2019, PMID 31562043). Harm: haemorrhagic cystitis, gonadal toxicity, alkylator-driven second malignancy. Readout: tubular phosphate and bicarbonate handling, urine beta-2 microglobulin. Rank: a regimen choice rather than a repurposing hypothesis, which is why it sits mid-list, but it has the highest probability of changing this child's course.

**6. Dasatinib as single-agent senolytic (off-label).** Target: clearance of p16-positive senescent cells, shown genetically in this model (INK-ATTAC, Baker 2011; p16 ablation, Baker 2008, PMID 18516091). Tier: BubR1 mouse for the target only; dasatinib has never been given to a BubR1 model, and the published pairing uses quercetin, a supplement, tested in Ercc1 and aged mice (01 §4.2, correction 1). Paediatric: approved for paediatric Ph+ CML, 40-100 mg daily by weight band. Interaction: the label warns on effects on growth, in a child with short stature and failure to thrive, myelosuppression complicates surveillance bloods, and no paediatric senolytic dose exists (02 §6). Readout: p16^INK4A in peripheral blood T cells, quadriceps cross-sectional area, age-appropriate function test. Rank: last, because the mechanistic case is the strongest on the list and the paediatric harm case is also the strongest.

## 2. Excluded

- **Growth hormone**: no efficacy in MVA2, no MVA1 data, background cancer risk of 39-75% (02 §2; PMID 25839904, PMID 36588761).
- **Mecasermin**: labelled contraindication with a history of malignancy (FDA 021839s021).
- **Taxanes**: partial BubR1 reduction sensitises cells to low-dose taxol with amplified segregation errors (Janssen 2009, PMID 19855003), while SAC suppression also causes paclitaxel resistance (Sudo 2004, PMID 15059905); both directions are bad and neither is measured in patient material.
- **Colchicine, griseofulvin**: approved aneugens, colchicine the reference OECD micronucleus positive control (01 §4.3).
- **Hydroxyurea**: raises micronucleated reticulocytes by 3 months (PMC3277805); not defensible in a constitutional instability disorder.
- **Acetazolamide, topiramate**: carbonic anhydrase inhibition drives hypercalciuria and nephrocalcinosis (PMC5976810), and seizures occur in 45% of MVA1 (Pavone 2022, PMID 35804254), so this will be proposed.
- **Chronic sirolimus or everolimus**: immunosuppression in a child whose mis-segregated cells are cleared by pro-inflammatory surveillance (Santaguida 2017, PMID 28633018); aneuploid yeast grow better under rapamycin (02 §5).
- **Navitoclax, fisetin, quercetin, AICAR, 17-AAG, Mps1 and PLK1 inhibitors**: not market-approved, out of scope.

## 3. Strongest objection to my own top candidate

Thiazide plus citrate does nothing to BubR1, to aneuploidy or to cancer risk: supportive nephrology dressed as a repurposing hypothesis. The aetiology of the nephrocalcinosis is also unknown, since no published MVA series reports it as a syndrome feature (02 §1), so if this is a post-ifosfamide proximal tubulopathy or a distal renal tubular acidosis, alkali is the treatment and the thiazide adds volume depletion and hypokalaemia without correcting the lesion. Defensible only after a urine chemistry work-up that has not been done in front of me.

## 4. What would change my ranking

A measured residual BubR1 level and a micronucleus or interphase FISH rate in this child's lymphocytes would show whether there is a biomarker to move at all; without one, candidates 3, 4 and 6 have no readout and should not be started. Functional data placing p.Asn1002Lys with Q921H, which was indistinguishable from wild type (Suijkerbuijk 2010, PMID 20516114), would weaken the premise. One MVA patient-derived line tested against vincristine, vinorelbine and paclitaxel would settle the question that governs this child's next relapse and would move chemotherapy modification above every senolytic. Any paediatric senolytic dosing study, in any progeroid condition, would move dasatinib to third. A documented case of vinca hypersensitivity or resistance in MVA, alongside the one dactinomycin toxicity report (Ochiai 2019, PMID 31081598), would change the next protocol more than anything else listed.

Maintenance note: RMS 2005 maintenance is six months of vinorelbine, a spindle poison, in a child with a constitutional spindle-checkpoint defect, and no BubR1-specific vinca hypersensitivity paper exists (01 §4.3). I would not de-escalate on theory against a 12.6-point overall survival gap, but I would document toxicity prospectively and treat this child as the index case.
