# Round 3: moderator's record

## 1. Method

Five members took part: clinical geneticist (G), mitosis cell biologist (C), paediatric oncologist (O), clinical pharmacologist (P), patient-family representative (F, public text only). Each read, in the order set by BRIEF.md, the five dossiers in track2/research (00 to 04) and PROMPT_track2.md, then ranked at most six market-approved candidates with tier, dosing, phenotype interaction, harm and readout. In round 2 each objected to the other four by name, revised the list, named one factual error in a round-1 file, and named one disagreement not to be averaged. I took part in neither round and added or removed no candidate. Between rounds the lists converged on renal management at rank 1 in all five files and split on senolytic therapy, which went from rank 1 in one file to excluded in three (round-2 files of O, P and F). Three arguments drove that: no aneuploid clone above about 10% of cells in blood (PROMPT_track2.md, Facts), leaving the aneuploidy-selective agents without a demonstrated target; hydroxychloroquine's retinal toxicity landing on an organ the syndrome already affects; and C's protective-senescence objection against his own rank 1. Two entries entered in round 2 from O's file alone: cyclophosphamide with mesna for ifosfamide, and dexrazoxane.

## 2. Agreements after round 2

**Findings.** (i) No BubR1-directed approved drug rises above the inference tier: BubR1 loss of function is not directly druggable, no approved drug is indicated for aneuploidy or CIN, and the interventions that worked in the mouse are genetic (01 §4.1, §5; 02 §7). Every candidate targets a consequence. (ii) No senolytic has been given to a BubR1 mouse; the D+Q work is in Ercc1 and aged mice, and quercetin is a supplement (01 §4.2, correction 1). (iii) Residual BubR1, here and in any MVA patient as a percentage of control, is unmeasured (01 §2.2). (iv) p.Asn1002Lys has no functional data; its class is positional inference, and Q921H in the same domain behaved as wild type (01 §1.3). (v) Renal management is the only entry with a measured target and tests no BubR1 hypothesis, conceded by four members against their own rank 1 (round-1 §Strongest objection of G, O, F; round2_cellbiologist §b). (vi) Shared harm screen: approved aneugens (colchicine, griseofulvin, taxanes), hydroxyurea, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, growth hormone, mecasermin, vitamin D above replacement (01 §4.3; 02 §2, §3, §5). (vii) Out of scope as non-approved: navitoclax, fisetin, quercetin, AICAR, 17-AAG, NMN, KIF18A and Mps1 inhibitors.

**Procedural.** (viii) A measurement gate precedes every mechanism-directed prescription: BubR1 immunoblot, baseline PCS and micronucleus rate, parental phasing (round1_geneticist entry 2; adopted by P and F; a condition on round2_oncologist entries 4-5; a gate rather than a ranked slot per round2_cellbiologist §a). (ix) Renal therapy is sequenced: urine chemistry, then citrate, thiazide only on documented hypercalciuria (all five round-2 entry 1). (x) Dose on cystatin C, since low muscle mass makes creatinine eGFR an overestimate (02 §3). (xi) Every entry carries "hypothesis for follow-up", and no illustrative protocol may read as a prescription (round1_family item 1). (xii) Nothing may weaken three-monthly renal ultrasound to age seven (02 §2).

## 3. Disagreements

**1. Is senescence here the lesion or the brake?** Position A (C, round1 rank 1; round2 entry 2 and §d): p16-positive cells are the one consequence with a causal intervention in this gene's own mouse, where p16 ablation and INK-ATTAC clearance improved muscle fibre diameter, fat mass, treadmill distance and cataract onset (01 §4.1). Position B (O, P and F exclude dasatinib; G holds it at 6 on genotype grounds): mis-segregating cells arrest with senescence features and pro-inflammatory signalling driving immune clearance (Santaguida 2017; 01 §5), aneuploid proliferation is p53-limited, p19Arf ablation worsened the same mouse, and Baker 2011 never measured tumour incidence, against a cancer risk reported as 38.7% to about 75% (02 §1). A's holder states the counter-argument himself. Settled by a senolytic in BubR1^H/H or ^H/L1002P mice with tumour incidence as co-primary endpoint; not done (01 §4.2), and named in four files.

**2. Is the next course of chemotherapy inside the remit?** Position A (O, round2 entry 2 and §d): the cyclophosphamide substitution and the vinorelbine maintenance question is the decision most likely to change this child's course. RMS 2005 maintenance moved 5-year overall survival from 72.4% to 85.0% (02 §4); SAC suppression yields paclitaxel resistance (Sudo 2004) while partial BubR1 reduction amplifies errors under low-dose taxol (Janssen 2009; 01 §4.3), so the defect predicts both reduced tumour killing and increased normal-tissue damage. Position B (G, C, F in round 1): vincas and dactinomycin inside RMS therapy are "an oncology decision, not a repurposing candidate" (round1_geneticist §Excluded); O calls that a scope ruling presented as an evidence judgement. P and F moved to A in round 2; G and C did not. Settled by one MVA patient-derived line tested against vincristine, vinorelbine and paclitaxel; none exists (01 §4.3 Gap).

## 6. Readouts

Renal: urine calcium/creatinine, citrate, pH, potassium and bicarbonate, ultrasound at baseline and six months (02 §3), in all five files. Aneuploidy-selective candidates: a burden endpoint, interphase FISH or low-pass single-cell sequencing of the aneuploid fraction (round2_cellbiologist §b). Senolytic hypothesis if pursued: p16^INK4A in sorted T cells with a muscle-function measure, the NCT04733534 pairing (02 §6), plus a parent-observable measure (round1_family item 6). Dexrazoxane: strain and troponin. Chemotherapy substitution: tubular phosphate and bicarbonate handling.

Micronucleus frequency was rejected as primary readout for the aneuploidy-selective agents on two grounds, both accepted by the member who proposed it. Mechanism-endpoint mismatch: it scores ongoing mis-segregation, while chloroquine's activity in Tang 2011 was selective killing of cells already aneuploid, so a fall is not a predicted effect and its absence would not falsify the hypothesis (round2_cellbiologist §a; 01 §5). And no baseline exists here, so a near-normal one puts a 30% fall inside OECD TG 487 variance (round2_oncologist §a). Four of five round-1 files named this endpoint, a shared error rather than a consensus. It stays in the measurement gate as baseline and stopping rule.

3. **Metformin.** "Cheapest hypothesis with a real mechanism, but two inferential steps from the data" (round1_oncologist rank 3). Hypothesis for follow-up.
4. **Hydroxychloroquine.** "The only approved drug with a published aneuploidy-selective signal, but the target is undemonstrated in this child" (round1_geneticist rank 3). Hypothesis for follow-up.
5. **Dexrazoxane, with an anthracycline.** "It buys nothing unless relapse occurs" (round2_oncologist entry 3). Hypothesis for follow-up.
6. **Dasatinib, intermittent, as a senolytic.** Unresolved, not ranked: "the mechanistic case is the strongest on the list and the paediatric harm case is also the strongest" (round1_oncologist rank 6). Hypothesis for follow-up.

## 8. What the panel could not answer from published data

1. **BubR1 immunoblot as a percentage of control:** which consequence dominates, and whether entries 3 to 6 have a premise. None exists for any MVA patient (01 §2.2).
2. **A functional assay on p.Asn1002Lys in the Suijkerbuijk 2010 design:** destabilising versus specific-defect class (01 §1.3).
3. **Parental testing or read-backed phasing:** trans versus cis, and which mouse models apply.
4. **Baseline PCS and micronucleus karyotype with aneuploid fraction by tissue:** whether the aneuploidy-selective candidates have any accessible target.
5. **A senolytic in BubR1 mice with tumour incidence as co-primary endpoint:** disagreement 1 (01 §4.2).
6. **Any approved drug in a BubR1 model with a healthspan endpoint.** None exists (01 §4.2); one would outrank every inference in §5.
7. **Chloroquine or hydroxychloroquine on MVA patient cells at achievable concentrations:** whether entry 4 survives the molecule and species changes.
8. **One MVA patient-derived line against vincristine, vinorelbine and paclitaxel:** disagreement 2 (01 §4.3 Gap).
9. **A per-genotype embryonal-tumour fraction in MVA:** how much harm is acceptable, and Orphanet's ~75% against Pavone's 38.7% (01 §7).
10. **A paediatric senolytic dose in any condition.** None found in any childhood progeroid syndrome (02 §6).
11. **The cause of this child's nephrocalcinosis:** whether entry 1 is directed or empirical, which alkali is correct, and the ceiling for renally cleared drugs (02 §3).
