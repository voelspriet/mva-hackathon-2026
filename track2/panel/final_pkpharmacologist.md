## 4. The verified facts

**Metformin.** V1 strengthens my objection. I argued a 13 to 63 fold gap by analogy to AICAR; the drug's own numbers give 32 to 130 fold (0.5 to 1.0 mM against 7.7 to 15.5 micromolar), with 13-point and 10-point separations from wild type.

No dose or formulation closes it. Metformin is not appreciably protein bound, so no unbound-fraction argument applies; distribution is transporter-driven, so lipophilicity cannot be engineered around it; extended release lowers Cmax rather than raising it; and the ceiling is toxicological, since plasma above roughly 5 mg/L, about 39 micromolar, is the range associated with lactic acidosis (clinical toxicology, mine, not a dossier). That allows a further 2.5 to 5 fold at most, at a cost no child should pay, against a shortfall of 32 to 130 fold. Portal and hepatocyte concentrations run a few fold above systemic, short of 0.5 mM, and the liver is not the compartment at issue. Ranking unchanged: ex vivo pre-gate only, and I endorse rank 6 provided the gap and its non-closability are printed next to it.

**731X.** This changes one position. In round 2 I rejected creative A1 partly because "a stabilised transcript still stops at 737". V1 section 2a shows the nearest tested truncation rescues checkpoint, alignment and unperturbed segregation and reaches 1.44 times wild-type protein once NMD is bypassed, so that ground was wrong, within V1's boundaries: 731X carries 715 residues and no construct terminating at 737 was tested. My surviving objection to digoxin is its therapeutic index in a 15 kg child with unestablished renal function, which section 6(d) records correctly. It revives transcript stabilisation as a class but changes no rank, since no approved NMD inhibitor has a reachable exposure.
