# Final round, paediatric nephrologist (team 2)

## 1. Errors

**§3.** "nephrocalcinosis of prematurity, where 61 to 90 percent resolve by 12 to 24 months (PMID 19460127; 16705005)". PMID 19460127 is the preterm risk-factor study identifying furosemide and carries no resolution figure. The resolution numbers are two cohorts: 70 percent at 12 months and 90 percent at 24 months (PMID 16705005), and 61 percent at 12 to 18 months in a VLBW cohort (PMC9587072). Cite risk factor and resolution separately.

**§7.** "61 to 90 percent of preterm nephrocalcinosis resolves untreated by 24 months (PMID 16705005)". The range spans both cohorts, and neither reports untreated arms; both are observational follow-up. Required: "resolves during follow-up in 61 to 90 percent by 12 to 24 months (PMID 16705005; PMC9587072)".

**§6(a)1.** The panel is said to decide "the ceiling for every renally cleared drug". One cystatin C sets a baseline, not a ceiling. Required: "gives the baseline cystatin C eGFR from which renal thresholds are set, repeated before and during any renally cleared drug". Attribution in §3 and §5 disagreement 4 is correct as recorded.

## 2. Overreach

**§6(c) item 2**, "thiazide only on documented hypercalciuria", needs one more condition: **with normocalcaemia**. A thiazide raises serum calcium, so hypercalciuria driven by hypercalcaemia is where it does harm.

**§7** replaces the six-week urine readout with a two-year ultrasound trajectory. The trajectory is right for the lesion and leaves nothing measurable in the first year. Required: keep urine calcium to creatinine at baseline and six weeks as the short-interval readout, with the ultrasound as the outcome measure.

## 3. Omission

The treatment of an ifosfamide Fanconi appears nowhere in §6, although §3 names that tubulopathy as one of the two competing causes. If the panel returns low tubular phosphate reabsorption with proximal acidosis, the treatment is alkali with phosphate and an active vitamin D analogue, a combination that is itself the standard cause of nephrocalcinosis in X-linked hypophosphataemia, which is why a thiazide was studied there (PMID 11433085). It matters twice: it is the branch on which the ranked renal item changes drug, and §6(b) bars "high-dose intermittent vitamin D" in a list that must not read as barring alfacalcidol for a tubulopathy.

Second: dietary calcium. §6 stops supplements and says nothing about intake, while dossier 02 §3 and creative H §1 keep age-appropriate calcium inside meals, because restriction raises oxalate absorption and threatens bone. A family reading "no calcium" removes milk.

## 4. The verified facts

V1 corrects me. In round 2 I wrote that Tang 2011 reported metformin "without a killing curve, so its effective concentration is unknown rather than unreachable". Wrong: Figure S1A and Table S1 give 0.5 and 1.0 mM in Ts13 and Ts16 MEFs at 72 hours, Ts13 73±2.9 against WT 86±2.4, Ts16 72±2.4 against WT 82±2.1, both P<0.005. The gap to paediatric plasma of 7.7 to 15.5 micromolar is 32 to 130 fold. My correction to the PK seat stands (OCT2 uptake then MATE secretion concentrates metformin in renal cortex) and does not bridge it: cortical accumulation is a few fold, not two orders. Metformin leaves my list as a prescription and stays as the ex vivo pre-gate at §6(c)6. V2 does not touch my ranking, and 731X does not return digoxin, a narrow-index renally cleared drug in a child whose filtration is unmeasured.

## 5. Sign-off

§6 represents my position: the gate, the two competing causes and the resolution figure are stated correctly once the citations in item 1 are fixed. Print this panel in the gate section.

**Serum:** sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate (venous gas acceptable), albumin-corrected or ionised calcium, phosphate, magnesium, creatinine, cystatin C, alkaline phosphatase, urate, PTH, 25-OH-D.
**Paired second-void urine:** calcium, citrate, protein, retinol binding protein or alpha-1-microglobulin, sodium, magnesium, urate and glucose, each as a ratio to urine creatinine, plus amino acids and pH on a fresh sample.
**Derived:** tubular phosphate reabsorption (TmP/GFR), fractional excretion of calcium, phosphate, magnesium, urate and sodium, cystatin C eGFR.
**Records:** neonatal furosemide and parenteral nutrition calcium and vitamin D exposure; cumulative ifosfamide in g/m2 and any platinum; every current product containing vitamin D or calcium, including growing-up milk.
**Imaging:** baseline ultrasound reported for medullary versus cortical pattern and stone burden, anchoring the two-year trajectory.
Acid loading is not part of this panel; if bicarbonate is low and urine pH will not fall below 5.3, the renal team decides, not this page.

**Decision rule to print with it.** Hypercalciuria for age with normocalcaemia and normal TmP/GFR: thiazide plus potassium citrate, amiloride as escalation. Low TmP/GFR with low molecular weight proteinuria, glycosuria, aminoaciduria or bicarbonate wasting: alkali with phosphate and alfacalcidol first, thiazide as adjunct. Normal urine calcium with a resolving ultrasound: no drug.

Dissent to be recorded in one sentence: "The tubular panel is not a formality before a thiazide: if it shows proximal tubulopathy, the first prescription is alkali with phosphate and an active vitamin D analogue, and the thiazide enters only as an adjunct against the calcification that the replacement itself causes."
