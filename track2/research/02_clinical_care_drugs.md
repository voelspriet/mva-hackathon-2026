# Clinical management of Mosaic Variegated Aneuploidy syndrome (MVA1, *BUB1B*) and the approved-drug landscape for repurposing

Compiled from public sources, September 2026. Disease-level only; no patient- or family-level searching was performed.

Throughout, **[Shown]** marks a statement supported by the cited source. **[Inferred]** marks reasoning that goes beyond what any cited source states.

---

## 1. Natural history and case series

**Definition and genetics.** MVA is a recessive condition of constitutional mosaicism for gains and losses of multiple chromosomes, with growth retardation, microcephaly and childhood cancer. Hanks et al. identified biallelic *BUB1B* mutations (encoding BUBR1, a spindle assembly checkpoint protein) in five of eight MVA families, two of which included embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma; this was the first germline spindle-checkpoint defect linked to a human disorder [Shown; Hanks 2004, Nat Genet 36:1159-1161, PMID 15475955]. The locus is MVA1, OMIM 257300. Later genes: *CEP57* (MVA2) [Shown; Snape 2011, PMID 21552266]; *TRIP13* (MVA3) [Shown; Yost 2017]; *MAD2L1BP*/p31comet, with juvenile granulosa cell tumours [Shown; PMC10721328]; and *CEP192* in a tetraploid-predominant variant [Shown; medRxiv 2023.07.22.23292907, preprint].

**Numbers of reported patients.** Kajii noted "some two dozen probable or definite cases" worldwide as of 2001, reporting five infants from four families with MVA and total premature chromatid separation (PCS) [Shown; Kajii 2001, Am J Med Genet 104:57-64, PMID 11746029]. Callier tabulated 28 reported cases and described the first patient without microcephaly [Shown; Callier 2005, PMID 16059936]. García-Castillo reviewed 30+ cases and delineated clinical subtypes [Shown; García-Castillo 2008, PMID 18548531]. The most recent *BUB1B*-specific tabulation counted 31 MVA1 cases plus four prenatally diagnosed cases ending in fetal demise or termination [Shown; Pavone 2022, Neurol Sci, PMID 35804254]. Later additions: Frattini 2025 (case plus literature review) and a 2024 Nature Reviews Genetics synthesis [Shown; doi:10.1002/ajmg.a.63901; PMID 39169218; both paywalled, cited from indexing records].

In García-Castillo's severely affected subgroup: Dandy-Walker complex 7/8, cataracts 6/6, Wilms tumour 7/8 [Shown; García-Castillo 2008].

**Cancer spectrum and age.** Of Kajii's five infants, four developed Wilms tumour; across the wider PCS/MVA series Wilms tumour occurred in 3, rhabdomyosarcoma in 2 and acute leukaemia in 1, with tumour diagnosis at 2-16 months, younger than in sporadic cases [Shown; Kajii 2001, via OMIM 257300]. Orphanet states cancer occurs in roughly one third of individuals (Wilms tumour, rhabdomyosarcoma, acute lymphoblastic leukaemia and ovarian granulosa cell tumour, generally before age 5), and that *BUB1B*-mutated individuals have a cancer incidence of approximately 75% [Shown; Orphanet ORPHA 1052]. Orphanet's ~75% and Pavone's 38.7% are not reconciled in the literature [Inferred: plausibly ascertainment, since early series were tumour-ascertained; treat as an uncertainty range]. MDS with monosomy 7 is also reported [Shown; Orphanet J Rare Dis 2019;14:87, PMC6500003]. In *TRIP13*-related MVA3, all six reported biallelic loss-of-function individuals developed Wilms tumour, while microcephaly, developmental delay, seizures and mosaic aneuploidy were variable [Shown; Yost 2017].

**Renal findings and nephrocalcinosis.** Renal dysfunction is tabulated at 12.9% in MVA1 [Shown; Pavone 2022]; renal cysts are described [Shown; Kajii 2001]. Targeted searching retrieved no published report of **nephrocalcinosis** as a recognised MVA feature, and no frequency figure. **[Inferred]** Nephrocalcinosis in a child with MVA1 should therefore be worked up on its own merits (hypercalciuria, distal renal tubular acidosis, vitamin D excess, drug causes, ifosfamide tubulopathy) rather than attributed to the syndrome.

**Neurological and ocular findings.** Severe microcephaly, brain hypoplasia with Dandy-Walker complex or other posterior fossa malformation, uncontrollable clonic seizures and cataracts characterise the PCS/MVA infants [Shown; Kajii 2001]; refractory infantile spasms are reported [Shown; Pediatr Neurol 2013, S0887-8994(13)00344-5]. Ocular anomalies include cataract, corneal opacities, microphthalmia and glaucoma [Shown; Orphanet ORPHA 1052].

**Survival.** No pooled survival analysis was found. Outcomes range from early infant death to survival into the second decade: an 11-year-old boy with MVA1 developed MDS at age 9 and died on day +156 after HSCT [Shown; PMC6500003]. **[Inferred]** Survival cannot be stated as a median; it is reported case by case.

**Reference resources.** OMIM 257300/614114/617598; Orphanet ORPHA 1052; MedlinePlus Genetics; GARD 3007; GTR C1850343. **No GeneReviews chapter exists for mosaic variegated aneuploidy or *BUB1B*** [Shown; GeneReviews title index searched; the nearest chapter is "Resources for Genetics Professionals: Mosaicism", NBK585455]. Wilms tumour predisposition has its own chapter (NBK1294).

## 2. Surveillance and management recommendations

**Wilms tumour surveillance.** The AACR Childhood Cancer Predisposition Workshop series recommends renal ultrasound every 3 months from birth or diagnosis through the seventh birthday for syndromes with increased Wilms tumour risk, and names MVA among the rare recessive disorders in which Wilms tumour has been reported in multiple patients [Shown; Kalish 2017, Clin Cancer Res 23:e115-e122, PMID 28674120]. The 2024 update on genomic instability disorders repeats renal ultrasound every 3 months from birth to age 7 for all MVA subtypes per SIOP-Europe guidance; documents increased cancer risk primarily for MVA1 and MVA3; recommends regular clinical review of systems for signs of rhabdomyosarcoma and other malignancies, with awareness of masses, swelling, haematuria and hypertension; and states explicitly that **radiation exposure should be avoided** [Shown; Nakano 2024, Clin Cancer Res 30:5009-5020, PMID 39264246]. Companion papers: Kratz 2017 and Achatz 2017 in the same series; SIOP-Europe Host Genome Working Group review [Shown; Eur J Cancer 2021, S0959-8049(21)00322-1].

**Rhabdomyosarcoma screening.** No validated imaging-based RMS surveillance protocol for MVA was found; the guideline recommends clinical review of systems rather than a defined imaging interval [Shown; Nakano 2024]. **[Inferred]** Any whole-body MRI schedule would be extrapolated from Li-Fraumeni practice.

**Growth hormone.** The Pediatric Endocrine Society Drug and Therapeutics Committee concluded that GH in paediatric cancer survivors does not appear to increase recurrence risk but may increase subsequent primary neoplasms, and that in patients already at higher cancer risk the evidence is insufficient to say whether GH adds risk [Shown; Raman 2015, PMID 25839904]. The Endocrine Society guideline recommends GH in survivors with confirmed GH deficiency, suggesting one disease-free year after completing therapy for malignancy [Shown; Sklar 2018, PMID 29982476]; a 2022 consensus statement covers GH safety in survivors [Shown; PMC9066587]. In cancer predisposition syndromes GH is generally treated as contraindicated with cautious exceptions [Shown; Raman 2015; Sklar 2018].

**GH in MVA specifically.** The available reports concern MVA2 (*CEP57*): patients with severe short stature showed no response to GH, and the authors concluded MVA2 patients probably do not benefit, unlike *PTPN11* Noonan syndrome [Shown; PMID 36588761, PMC9801314; ESPE 2022 abstract hrp0095p1-129]. No MVA1 GH data were found. **[Inferred]** Two arguments against GH here: no efficacy evidence in MVA, and a background cancer risk of order 40-75% in MVA1, against which the Raman 2015 "insufficient evidence" position is not reassuring.

**Bub1b models and the GH/IGF-1 axis.** BubR1^H/H hypomorphic mice show postnatal growth failure, cachectic dwarfism, lordokyphosis, cataracts, loss of subcutaneous fat, impaired wound healing, infertility and a median lifespan of about six months [Shown; Baker 2004, Nat Genet 36:744-749, PMID 15208629], with p16^Ink4a and p19^Arf selectively induced in skeletal muscle, fat and eye [Shown; Baker 2008, PMID 18516091]. A mono-allelic BubR1 MVA mutation reduces life- and healthspan [Shown; Wijshake 2012], allelic dose drives phenotypic heterogeneity [Shown; JCI 2019, doi:10.1172/JCI126863], and increased BubR1 expression protects against aneuploidy and cancer while extending healthy lifespan [Shown; Baker 2013]. No GH or IGF-1 administration study in Bub1b-mutant mice was found. **[Inferred]** The low-muscle, low-fat, small-stature phenotype in MVA1 resembles the mouse phenotype, which in the mouse is senescence-driven and corrected by senescent-cell clearance rather than by growth signalling.

## 3. Nephrocalcinosis in a child: approved therapies

**Mechanistic frame.** Nephrocalcinosis in children most often follows hypercalciuria, hypocitraturia or both, with contributions from distal renal tubular acidosis, vitamin D excess and drugs [Shown; Hoppe, Kidney Int, S0085-2538(15)54999-5].

**Thiazides.** They reduce renal calcium excretion by increasing distal tubular reabsorption and, via volume contraction, proximal reabsorption; indicated in severe hypercalciuria and may improve bone density where hypercalciuria has reduced it. Paediatric dosing: hydrochlorothiazide 0.5-1 mg/kg/day, usual range 12.5-25 mg/day, in two divided doses for 24-hour cover [Shown; Kidney Int review; NHS GGC paediatric guideline 973]. Thiazides arrest progression of nephrocalcinosis in X-linked hypophosphataemia [Shown; Seikaly 2001, PMID 11433085]. Monitor serum potassium after initiation and each increment [Shown; NHS GGC 973].

**Alkali citrate.** Potassium citrate 0.1-0.2 g/kg/day (0.3-0.6 mmol/kg/day), preferably the potassium rather than sodium-potassium salt, titrated against urine pH: alkalinisation above pH 7.5 risks calcium phosphate precipitation [Shown; Kidney Int review; NHS GGC 973].

**Dietary and vitamin D.** Maintain age-appropriate calcium intake with sodium restriction and generous fluids; do not calcium-restrict [Shown; Kidney Int review]. Vitamin D supplementation with urolithiasis and hypercalciuria is regarded as unsafe, and high-dose intermittent vitamin D prophylaxis in infancy has caused nephrocalcinosis and hypercalciuria [Shown; PMC7551195; PMID 2386055].

**Drugs to avoid.** Carbonic anhydrase inhibition promotes stone and nephrocalcinosis formation: topiramate causes renal tubular acidosis and hypercalciuria, with nephrolithiasis/nephrocalcinosis resolving on withdrawal and about 5% asymptomatic nephrolithiasis in treated children; the monitoring proposed there (serum electrolytes, urine calcium/creatinine ratio, renal ultrasound at baseline and every 6 months) is a reasonable generic template [Shown; PMC5976810]. **[Inferred]** The same mechanism applies to acetazolamide, making it a poor choice with established nephrocalcinosis; no acetazolamide-specific paediatric series was retrieved.

**Interactions with low muscle mass, growth and chemotherapy history. [Inferred]** Three points, none directly evidenced for MVA: creatinine-based eGFR overestimates renal function when muscle mass is low, so cystatin C is preferable for dosing; thiazide-induced hypokalaemia and volume depletion are less well tolerated in a child with failure to thrive; and prior ifosfamide can leave a proximal tubulopathy with phosphate and bicarbonate wasting, changing both the interpretation of urine chemistry and the choice of alkali.

## 4. Rhabdomyosarcoma treatment, and the mitotic-checkpoint problem

**Standard regimens.** North American practice uses vincristine + dactinomycin (VA) ± cyclophosphamide (VAC), and VAC alternating with vincristine/irinotecan (VAC/VI) at intermediate risk, within risk-adapted multimodal therapy; 5-year survival roughly 90% / 50-70% / 20% for low / intermediate / high risk [Shown; COG 2023 Blueprint, PMC10519430]. European practice uses ifosfamide, vincristine, dactinomycin (IVA) ± doxorubicin. RMS 2005 (EpSSG, 102 hospitals, 14 countries) randomised patients in remission after nine IVA-based cycles to stop or to six cycles of vinorelbine 25 mg/m² IV days 1, 8, 15 plus oral cyclophosphamide 25 mg/m²/day days 1-28: 5-year disease-free survival 78.1% vs 70.1%, overall survival 85.0% vs 72.4%, making maintenance standard of care [Shown; Bisogno 2019, Lancet Oncol, PMID 31562043; JCO 2025]. Both induction and maintenance rest on microtubule-targeting vinca alkaloids.

**Microtubule agents in a checkpoint-deficient host.** Antimitotic killing depends on a functional spindle assembly checkpoint: suppressing MAD2 and BUBR1 abolishes checkpoint function and produces paclitaxel resistance, and restoring MAD2 restores sensitivity [Shown; Sudo 2004, Cancer Res 64:2502-2508, PMID 15059905]. Reduced BubR1, not MAD2, characterises acquired paclitaxel resistance in SKOV3-TR30 cells, which fail to arrest in M phase under paclitaxel or nocodazole [Shown; PMID 17234259]. A genome-wide CRISPR screen found knockdown of *BUB1B*, *BUB3* and *TTK* attenuates paclitaxel-induced death [Shown; Sci Rep 2024, s41598-024-66244-1]. In SAC-impaired cells, aneuploidy generates proteotoxic stress and DNA damage alongside p53-mediated post-mitotic apoptosis [Shown; PMC4506520]. **[Inferred]** These data concern tumour cells and taxanes. Extrapolation to vincristine against an RMS in a constitutionally BUBR1-deficient child is unsupported in either direction: reduced arrest-dependent killing and increased missegregation catastrophe are equally available predictions, and no clinical series resolves it.

**Reported chemotherapy toxicity in MVA.** A 38-month-old boy with PCS/MVA, biallelic germline *BUB1B* mutations and bilateral Wilms tumour received dactinomycin monotherapy after right nephrectomy; a severe adverse reaction prevented further chemotherapy, and left nephrectomy was done without postoperative chemotherapy. He survived 15 months on home peritoneal dialysis without relapse [Shown; Ochiai 2019, Pediatr Blood Cancer, PMID 31081598]. In the one reported MVA1 HSCT (treosulfan-based reduced-intensity conditioning, TCRαβ/CD19-depleted unrelated donor, for MDS with monosomy 7), conditioning toxicity was limited to mild toxidermia and transaminitis, but graft rejection on day +47 was followed by fatal pancytopenia and sepsis on day +156 [Shown; PMC6500003]. **[Inferred]** Two cases are not a toxicity profile; the first is consistent with reduced tolerance of cytotoxic therapy, the second is a graft-survival rather than toxicity signal.

**Radiation.** No MVA-specific cellular radiosensitivity study was retrieved. The surveillance guideline nonetheless states radiation exposure should be avoided in genomic instability disorders including MVA [Shown; Nakano 2024]. Generic data show high chromosomal instability increases radiation sensitivity in cancer cells and xenografts [Shown; PMC11429890], and *bub-3*/SAC mutants in *C. elegans* are hypersensitive to ionising radiation [Shown; PMC5714485]. **[Inferred]** The avoidance statement rests on the class-level premise and second-cancer risk, not on measured MVA patient-cell radiosensitivity.

**Senescence rationale specific to BUBR1.** In BubR1^H/H mice, genetic clearance of p16^Ink4a-positive senescent cells via INK-ATTAC attenuated sarcopenia, fat loss and cataract, delayed onset when applied lifelong, and slowed progression of established disorders when applied late [Shown; Baker 2011, Nature 479:232-236; Baker 2008]. **[Inferred]** This is the strongest published argument for a senolytic hypothesis in MVA1, but it is genetic clearance in a mouse, not a drug in a child; the leap to D+Q in a paediatric patient with cancer predisposition and nephrocalcinosis crosses several unbridged gaps (no paediatric senolytic dosing, quercetin renal signal, dasatinib growth warning).

## 6. Registered trials

- **ClinicalTrials.gov API v2, September 2026: "mosaic variegated aneuploidy" returned zero studies; "BUB1B" returned zero studies** [Shown]. No MVA or *BUB1B* interventional trial was identified in EU CTR/CTIS either. **[Inferred]** Absence in these searches is not proof of absence, but no registered interventional trial in MVA was found.
- **NCT04733534**, phase 2, senescence and frailty in survivors of childhood cancer: dasatinib 100 mg/day + quercetin 500 mg twice daily on days 1-3 and 30-32, or fisetin 20 mg/kg/day on days 1-2 and 30-31; n≈110; endpoints walking speed and blood p16^INK4A at 60 days. Population is **adult** survivors, not children [Shown].
- **Fisetin, all adult**: NCT03430037 (AFFIRM), NCT03675724 (AFFIRM-LITE), NCT06431932, NCT06133634, NCT05595499, NCT06796374, NCT07195318; NCT04313634 (senolytics, skeletal health) [Shown].
- **Progeria**: NCT02579044 (lonafarnib + everolimus), NCT03895528 (lonafarnib), NCT00879034 (zoledronic acid + pravastatin + lonafarnib) [Shown]. No metformin-in-progeria trial was identified.
- **Metformin, paediatric**: NCT01528046 and a phase I with vincristine/irinotecan/temozolomide [Shown; PMC9972017].
- **Rhabdomyosarcoma**: ARST2032, NCT02567435 (VAC/VI ± temsirolimus), NCT07466316 [Shown].

**[Inferred]** No senolytic trial enrolling children, and none in any progeroid syndrome of childhood, was found. A paediatric senolytic proposal in MVA would have no dosing precedent to cite.

## 7. Precedents for approved-drug repurposing in ultra-rare paediatric genetic disease

The clearest precedent is **lonafarnib** in Hutchinson-Gilford progeria syndrome. A farnesyltransferase inhibitor developed as an oncology candidate and never approved for cancer, it was repurposed on the argument that progerin requires farnesylation, and received FDA priority-review approval as Zokinvy in November 2020 for HGPS and processing-deficient progeroid laminopathies from age one, with an EMA CHMP positive opinion in May 2022. With up to 11 years of follow-up, treated patients showed a survival benefit of about 2.5 years versus untreated patients [Shown; FDA approval summary, Genet Med 2023, PMID 36507973]. The pattern is instructive for MVA: a single-gene ultra-rare paediatric disease, a mechanism-first argument, a drug with existing human safety data, natural-history rather than randomised controls, and a modest measurable survival endpoint. The programme continued into combination repurposing, adding everolimus to lonafarnib on the rationale that mTOR inhibition reduced ROS and DNA damage in HGPS vascular cells [Shown; NCT02579044; Sci Rep 2023].

Three further precedents span the range. **Everolimus** moved from transplant immunosuppression and oncology to a paediatric dispersible formulation approved from age 1 for TSC-associated SEGA on EXIST-1, and later for TSC-associated seizures on EXIST-3 [Shown; PMID 27351628]. **Sirolimus** in complicated vascular anomalies was used off-label on PI3K/mTOR reasoning, with a prospective paediatric trial in 2016, and remains off-label there [Shown; Adams 2016, PMID 26783326]. **Alpelisib**, a breast-cancer PI3Kα inhibitor, gained accelerated FDA approval in April 2022 as Vijoice for severe PIK3CA-related overgrowth spectrum from age two, on EPIK-P1 (NCT04285723), a single-arm study built on real-world compassionate-use data in a population where most had failed sirolimus; paediatric dosing 50 mg daily for ages 2 to <18, escalable to 125 mg after 24 weeks from age 6 [Shown; Clin Cancer Res 30:23]. **Metformin** marks the low-cost end: Lafora orphan designations (EMA 2016, FDA 2017), a Fanconi anaemia pilot, and a paediatric oncology phase I [Shown; J Transl Med 2024; Blood Adv 2022; PMC9972017]. **[Inferred]** The approvals that happened rested on a direct molecular mechanism (progerin farnesylation, PI3Kα gain of function, mTOR hyperactivation) or an existing paediatric formulation, and used single-arm or natural-history comparators. MVA1 has neither an actionable target of that kind, since BUBR1 loss of function is not directly druggable, nor any trial infrastructure.
