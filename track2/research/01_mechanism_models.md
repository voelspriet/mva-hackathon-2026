# BUB1B / BubR1 and Mosaic Variegated Aneuploidy 1 (MVA1, OMIM 257300)
## Mechanism and model-system dossier

Compiled 5 September 2026 from public sources (PubMed/PMC, UniProt, Ensembl, RefSeq, ClinVar, gnomAD, MGI, publisher pages). OMIM 257300 and one paywalled review (PMID 39169218) could not be retrieved; no figures are taken from either. Every PMID cited below was resolved against a live NCBI record. Claims that are inference rather than published observation are marked **[inference]** and are collected again in section 7.

Case context, as published in the hackathon submission: compound heterozygous *BUB1B*, one nonsense allele NM_001211.6:c.2210T>G p.(Leu737Ter) (exon 17 of 23; ClinVar pathogenic, rs759242053) and one missense allele p.(Asn1002Lys) in the C-terminal kinase domain (gnomAD singleton, described in the brief as having no ClinVar record). No other patient information is used or sought.

**Two variant-level corrections to that framing, established in section 2.4 below.** The nonsense allele now carries a two-star ClinVar entry from two submitters, not one, and it is present in gnomAD v4 at AF 7.87e-5 with 115 heterozygotes; it is not an allele reported in any published MVA patient. The missense allele does now have a ClinVar record (VCV004600147.1, uncertain significance, Ambry Genetics, 19 Sep 2025).

## 1. BubR1 protein: domains, functions, and the pseudokinase question

### 1.1 Architecture

Human BUBR1 (UniProt O60566, gene *BUB1B*) is 1050 amino acids, 119,545 Da, encoded by 23 exons (Ensembl ENST00000287598 / BUB1B-201, MANE canonical, 23 exons over 60,054 bp). Annotated and experimentally mapped elements, N to C:

| TPR domain ("BUB1 N-terminal") | 62-226 | Divergent TPR fold; concave groove binds Blinkin/KNL1; kinetochore recruitment | D'Arcy 2010, PMID 20220147 (PDB 2WVI); Bolanos-Garcia 2011, PMID 22000412 (PDB 3SI5) |

The N-terminal region alone acts as a soluble inhibitor of APC/C-CDC20-mediated cyclin B degradation in interphase (Malureanu 2009, PMID 19154723). So checkpoint signalling (MCC assembly, CDC20 inhibition) maps to the N-terminal half; attachment stability and checkpoint silencing map to KARD plus the C-terminal domain.

### 1.2 Active kinase or pseudokinase

**Pseudokinase side.** Suijkerbuijk 2012, *Dev Cell* 22:1321 (PMID 22698286, *Dev Cell*, not *Curr Biol*) showed human BUBR1 retains the catalytic triad (K795, D882, D911) but that catalysis is dispensable: mutating the catalytic aspartate D882 to Asn or Ala, or removing the kinase domain (731X), did not affect error-free segregation. K795 and D911 mutants were defective, but through loss of conformational stability, recombinant K795A denatured at 34.7 °C versus 39.1 °C for wild type, and growth at 27 °C or 33 °C restored protein levels. The paper notes that earlier "kinase-dead" studies (Mao 2003; Kops 2004; Huang 2008; Malureanu 2009) used that same destabilising K795-equivalent mutation. Supporting: parallel subfunctionalisation across eukaryotes removed kinase function from Mad3/BubR1-like paralogues (Tromer 2016, PMID 28003474), and the domain has a demonstrated non-catalytic scaffolding role (Gama Braga 2020, PMID 33207204).

**Catalytic side.** Chan 1999, *J Cell Biol* 146:941 (PMID 10477750) detected hBUBR1 kinase activity in mitosis, stimulated by spindle disruption; UniProt cites it for EC 2.7.11.1. Mao 2003, *Cell* 114:87 (PMID 12859900) identified CENP-E as the activator and proposed a bifunctional model, noting "only a small fraction need be kinase competent". Huang 2019, *Cell Res* 29:562 (PMID 31201382) solved the *Drosophila* BubR1 kinase domain in a catalytically competent conformation, reported phosphorylation of CENP-E, and described an antagonist (bubristatin).

**Where this leaves the human protein.** Both sides agree the *Drosophila* domain is non-degenerate, so the fly structure does not settle the human case, and no human BUBR1 kinase-domain structure exists in the PDB (only TPR 2WVI, KNL1 complex 3SI5, B56-KARD complex 5K6S). *Drosophila* kinase-dead flies have a robust SAC but thin kinetochore fibres, an attachment defect, not a checkpoint defect (Rahmani 2009, PMID 19951912). No kinase-dead *Bub1b* knock-in mouse exists; the MGI allele list holds the H hypomorph, null, X753, L1002P, the Tmats L hypomorph and the K243R acetylation mutant, and no ATP-lobe knock-in.

### 1.3 Do patient kinase-domain missense variants act through stability?

Yes, for every MVA allele tested. Suijkerbuijk 2010, *Cancer Res* 70:4891 (PMID 20516114) analysed MVA missense alleles R727C, L844F, I909T, Q921H and L1012P (versus Y155C in the TPR and R550Q outside). All mutants in or near the kinase domain showed a 5-10 fold decrease in protein level versus wild type, and forced overexpression of the poorly expressed mutants I909T and L1012P to wild-type levels fully restored the nocodazole response. The paper's conclusion is that MVA mutations fall into two classes, those imposing specific defects in checkpoint activity or microtubule attachment, and those that simply lower BUBR1 abundance, and that low abundance results from "absence of transcripts from truncating mutants combined with high protein turnover of missense mutants", with the missense change consistently occurring in or near the kinase domain.

**Positional note on this case.** Leu737 sits in the linker immediately upstream of the UniProt-annotated kinase domain (766-1050); a stop there removes the entire domain. Asn1002 sits inside the domain, in the C-lobe, ~48 residues from the C-terminus and 10 residues N-terminal to the PLK1 site T1008. The nearest characterised MVA missense allele by position is L1012P. **[inference]** By the Suijkerbuijk classification, p.Asn1002Lys is positionally a candidate for the destabilising class rather than the specific-defect class, but it has not been tested; no functional data on N1002K exist. Do not confuse human L1012P with mouse BubR1 L1002P, which is the mouse equivalent of human L1012P (Sieben 2020).

## 2. Consequence of a stop at codon 737

### 2.1 NMD prediction

The empirical rule is that a premature termination codon triggers nonsense-mediated decay when it lies more than 50-55 nucleotides upstream of the final exon-exon junction (Nagy & Maquat 1998, *Trends Biochem Sci*, PMID 9644970; review Lykke-Andersen & Jensen 2015, *Nat Rev Mol Cell Biol*, PMID 26397022). Computed directly from the RefSeq record NM_001211.6 (23 exons; CDS 153..3305, 3,153 nt, 1,050 aa):

- Codon 737 = c.2209-2211 = transcript positions 2361-2363, inside **exon 17** (transcript 2296..2436). Wild-type codon **TTA (Leu)**; c.2210T>G gives **TGA**, confirming p.Leu737Ter.
- Last exon-exon junction = transcript 3109/3110 (exon 22 / exon 23).
- **PTC-to-last-junction distance = 747 nt**, with **6 downstream exon-exon junctions** available for EJC deposition.
- For contrast, the native stop codon (3303-3305) sits 196 nt into the final exon with zero downstream junctions, consistent with normal NMD escape.

**[inference from the rule, not a measurement on this allele]** the c.2210T>G transcript is predicted to be degraded by NMD, and no truncated protein is expected from it. Since codon 737 precedes the kinase domain (766-1050), any escaping product would lack the entire domain.

### 2.2 What has actually been measured for truncating *BUB1B* alleles

Matsuura 2006, *Am J Med Genet A* 140:358 (PMID 16411201), seven Japanese PCS/MVA families: transcripts from the 1833delT and splice-site alleles were "significantly reduced, probably due to nonsense-mediated mRNA decay". Patient cells showed reduced BubR1 with diminished kinetochore localisation and abolished CDC20 kinetochore association despite normal CDC20 expression; microcell transfer of chromosome 15 restored all three and the colcemid response. Conclusion: a >50% decrease in BubR1 expression or activity is involved in the syndrome. Their "monoallelic" status was later resolved as a G>A substitution 44 kb upstream of a *BUB1B* transcription start site, confirmed by TALEN knock-in (Ochiai 2014, *PNAS* 111:1461, PMID 24344301).

Suijkerbuijk 2010, *Cancer Res* 70:4891 (PMID 20516114, PMC2887387) supplies the quantitative data, from patient lines absent/I909T, 386X/R727C, 753X/R814H and 731X/Y155C. Full-length substitution-mutant BubR1 was **decreased 2-6 fold** by quantitative immunoblot; **none of the truncated proteins (386X, 731X, 753X) was detectable** in patient or parental lysates; heterozygous parent lines were intermediate. The 386X transcript was undetectable by Northern blot and the 731X/WT line showed 2-fold mRNA reduction, yet **both 386X and 731X are expressed once NMD is bypassed by plasmid-driven expression** in HeLa/U2OS, the closest existing evidence that *BUB1B* truncating transcripts are NMD substrates. Transfected kinase-domain substitutions ran 5-10 fold below wild type; Y155C and R550Q did not reduce levels and Q921H was indistinguishable from wild type. Turnover of I909T and R727C was ~2-fold increased (cycloheximide, 5 h), HSP90-dependent and proteasome-mediated. Under STLC, 50% of cells exited mitosis within 68 min (753X/R814H) and 114 min (731X/Y155C), with misalignment up 2-3 fold. The paper states 37% of MVA patients develop cancer, mostly within the first 3 years. A frequently quoted threshold, mis-segregation in most cells at ~6% residual BubR1, almost none at ~13%, comes from a **HeLa shRNA titration in that paper, not from patient cells**.

Suijkerbuijk 2012 (PMID 22698286) provides the counterfactual for a hypothetical protein product: an engineered 731X truncation, ectopically expressed, was functional in their segregation assays. **[inference]** If a Leu737Ter product escaped NMD and were stably expressed, loss of the kinase domain alone would not necessarily be the dominant defect; the expected mechanism for this allele is loss of transcript, not a harmful truncated protein.

No published quantification of residual BubR1 as a **percentage of control** in MVA patient fibroblasts or lymphocytes exists, only fold-changes (above) and a relative statement (">50% decrease", Matsuura 2006). Hanks 2004 contains no western blot, so residual-protein claims must not be attributed to it. The best-quantified number is murine: BubR1^H/H expresses ~10% of wild-type BubR1 (Sieben 2020, PMID 31738183).

### 2.3 Genotype-phenotype

- Hanks 2004, *Nat Genet* 36:1159 (PMID 15475955): 8 pedigrees screened, both alleles mutated in 5. Every biallelic family paired one missense with one truncating or absent-transcript allele; the paper states explicitly that no case had biallelic missense or biallelic truncating mutations. Five of six missense mutations were in the kinase domain (R550Q was not). Cancers: exactly two, both embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma (soft palate at 7 years; vagina at 5 months), no Wilms tumour, so 2 of 5 biallelic families. This is the same truncating-plus-missense configuration as the present case, but because *all five* biallelic families had that configuration, the cohort cannot separate it from other biallelic genotypes.
- García-Castillo 2008, *Am J Med Genet A* 146A:1687 (PMID 18548531) reviewed 21 of 35 MVA patients screened for *BUB1B* and delineated three groups. Patients with **monoallelic** *BUB1B* mutations were severely affected: Dandy-Walker complex 7/8, cataracts 6/6, Wilms tumour 7/8, PCS in 8/8 probands and 7/7 carrier parents. Patients with **no** *BUB1B* mutation were mildly affected, no cancer, no Dandy-Walker, no cataract, PCS in 1/7. Patients with **biallelic** *BUB1B* mutations showed a moderate phenotype. The abstract gives no separate rhabdomyosarcoma count. This points in the opposite direction to the assumption that biallelic truncating-plus-missense is the highest-risk class, but the "monoallelic" group is very likely the Japanese families whose second allele is the upstream regulatory variant found in 2014 (PMID 24344301), so the grouping is a pre-genotyping artefact rather than a real allele count.
- Yost 2017, *Nat Genet* 49:1148 (PMID 28553959) restratified risk by gene: all six individuals with biallelic *TRIP13* loss developed Wilms tumour; "individuals with biallelic TRIP13 or BUB1B mutations have a high risk of embryonal tumors" and their cells show severe SAC impairment; MVA from biallelic *CEP57* mutations or of unknown cause is not associated with embryonal tumours and those cells show minimal SAC deficiency.
- Snape 2011, *Nat Genet* 43:527 (PMID 21552266) established biallelic loss-of-function *CEP57* as MVA2. Nakano 2024, *Clin Cancer Res* (PMID 39264246), the current surveillance consensus, records that none of the 15 published MVA2 individuals developed cancer, and recommends renal ultrasound every 3 months from birth to age 7 for the cancer-associated genotypes.

Mouse allelic-series data map directly onto this genotype class. Sieben 2020, *J Clin Invest* 130:171 (PMID 31738183) built BubR1^L1002P (modelling human L1012P), BubR1^X753 (truncating) and combined them with BubR1^H. BubR1^X753/L1002P and BubR1^H/X753 died prematurely; BubR1^H/L1002P was viable with MVA features (dwarfism, lipodystrophy, sarcopenia, low cardiac stress tolerance, cancer predisposition), median lifespan 343 days versus 196 days for BubR1^H/H despite a comparable total BubR1 reduction. The conclusion is that allelic identity, not just protein level or aneuploidy, drives heterogeneity.

### 2.4 The two alleles in this case, as the databases hold them

**c.2210T>G p.(Leu737Ter), rs759242053.** ClinVar Variation 533901 (VCV000533901.9, updated 17 May 2025), GRCh38 chr15:40209701. **Pathogenic/Likely pathogenic**, review status *criteria provided, multiple submitters, no conflicts* (two stars), from Labcorp Genetics/Invitae (SCV000762865.8, Pathogenic, 9 Oct 2024, condition MVA1, citing PMIDs 15475955 and 21190457) and GeneDx (SCV004031025.1, Likely pathogenic, 17 Aug 2023, condition not provided). Invitae's comment states the population frequency at this position is "considered unreliable, as metrics indicate poor data quality". gnomAD v4 exomes: AC 115 / AN 1,461,846, **AF 7.87e-5, 0 homozygotes**; genomes AC 5 / AN 152,174; joint 0 homozygotes. gnomAD v2.1.1: AC 7 / AN 251,486.

Two cautions. **p.Leu737Ter is not a Hanks 2004 allele and no published MVA patient carrying it was found**; it is easily confused with the neighbouring Hanks family 2/3 allele c.2208_2211dup p.Ser738fs (ClinVar 6763) and with Suijkerbuijk's engineered 731X. And 115 heterozygotes is high for an allele asserted pathogenic in a rare recessive disorder. **[inference]** The classification reads as reasoning from mechanism (nonsense in exon 17 of 23), not from patient evidence.

**p.(Asn1002Lys).** Codon 1002 is AAT at c.3004-3006, so the change is c.3006T>A or c.3006T>G. It lies in **exon 23, the final exon**, inside the kinase domain. ClinVar does hold a record: **VCV004600147.1**, uncertain significance, one star, 19 Sep 2025, single submitter Ambry Genetics (SCV007198955.1), condition "Inborn genetic diseases" rather than MVA, noting a conserved position, conservative substitution and inconclusive in-silico prediction. gnomAD: c.3006T>A absent from v4 and v2.1.1; c.3006T>G appears once in v4 exomes (AF 6.84e-7, 0 homozygotes). No submission links this variant to MVA and no functional data exist. For scale, 79 *BUB1B* records carry a P/LP classification with condition MVA1, out of 2,493 *BUB1B* records.

## 3. Downstream cellular and organismal phenotypes

**Patient cells.** Impaired mitotic checkpoint, chromosome alignment defects, low BubR1 abundance, rescued by ectopic BubR1 (Suijkerbuijk 2010, PMID 20516114). Loss of BubR1 kinetochore localisation and of CDC20 kinetochore association, with loss of the colcemid response, rescued by chromosome 15 transfer (Matsuura 2006, PMID 16411201). Total premature chromatid separation in 48.5-83.2% of lymphocytes in two karyotyped infants, and 3.5-41.7% in carrier parents (Kajii 2001, PMID 11746029). Severe SAC impairment and high mis-segregation rates in biallelic *BUB1B* and *TRIP13* cells, minimal in *CEP57* (Yost 2017, PMID 28553959).

**BubR1 hypomorphic mice.** Baker 2004, *Nat Genet* 36:744 (PMID 15208629): low BubR1 causes progressive aneuploidy plus progeroid features, short lifespan, cachectic dwarfism, lordokyphosis, cataracts, loss of subcutaneous fat, impaired wound healing; graded BubR1 reduction in MEFs increases aneuploidy and senescence; both sexes are infertile with meiotic segregation defects; BubR1 declines with normal ageing in multiple tissues.

Baker 2008, *Nat Cell Biol* 10:825 (PMID 18516091): skeletal muscle and fat, the two earliest-affected tissues, carry high p16^Ink4a and p19^Arf. p16^Ink4a inactivation attenuates senescence and premature ageing in those tissues; p19^Arf inactivation exacerbates them. So p16 is an effector and p19Arf an attenuator in this model.

Baker 2013, *Nat Cell Biol* 15:96 (PMID 23242215): sustained high BubR1 expression corrects checkpoint impairment and kinetochore-microtubule attachment defects, reduces aneuploidy and tumorigenesis even against oncogenic Ras, and extends median and maximum lifespan with delayed age-related deterioration.

Wijshake 2012, *PLoS Genet* (PMID 23300461): a knock-in of the human monoallelic MVA mutation (BubR1^+/GTTA, 2211insGTTA) reduces median and maximum lifespan with cataract, lordokyphosis, muscle wasting, impaired exercise capacity and fat loss.

Sieben 2020 (PMID 31738183): monoallelic carriers are prone to selected MVA-related pathologies later in life, sarcopenia predisposition correlating with mTORC1 hyperactivity. On tumours the paper is careful and citations should be too: BubR1^H/H mice are sensitive to carcinogen-induced tumours but "do not live long enough to assess predisposition to spontaneous tumors", so the absent spontaneous excess is censoring by a 5-7 month median lifespan, not demonstrated resistance. BubR1^+/− and ^+/L1002P showed wild-type-like spontaneous incidence and spectrum but **significantly reduced tumour latency**, and on DMBA no incidence increase but significantly greater multiplicity and size.

Carcinogen data: BubR1^H/H mice are "highly susceptible to DMBA-induced tumor formation" and prone to lung tumours (Baker 2006, *J Cell Biol*, PMID 16476774; protocol DMBA on dorsal skin at postnatal day 5, biopsy at 5 months; numeric incidence is in a supplementary figure and was not retrievable). *Bub1b*^+/− mice develop lung and intestinal adenocarcinomas after carcinogen (Dai 2004, *Cancer Res*, PMID 14744753), specifically azoxymethane-induced colon and lung tumours; on an Apc^Min/+ background, Apc^Min/+ alone averages under one colonic tumour per mouse versus over four for BubR1^+/−;Apc^Min/+ (Rao 2009, *Carcinogenesis*, PMID 19372138).

Two results complicate any simple "BubR1 loss causes cancer" claim. BubR1 K243R/+ acetylation-deficient heterozygotes **do** develop spontaneous tumours with massive mis-segregation, dissociating protein level from protein function (Park 2013, *J Cell Biol*, PMID 23878276). And CENP-E^+/− reverses the polarity: aneuploidy drives spontaneous lymphomas and lung tumours in aged mice, yet acts as a more effective inhibitor than initiator of tumorigenesis in induced settings (Weaver 2007, *Cancer Cell*, PMID 17189716). The best-supported summary across the H/H, +/− and +/GTTA alleles is that reduced BubR1 changes tumour latency, multiplicity and growth more than initiation. Note that a 2014 review table (Lee 2014, *Mol Cells*, PMID 25256220) attributes increased DMBA lung tumour incidence to BubR1^+/GTTA, contradicting Wijshake 2012, which reports incidence and multiplicity "very similar" (n=19 versus 20) with only size increased (P<0.01). Follow Wijshake.

**Overlap with the human syndrome.** Short stature/growth retardation, low muscle mass/sarcopenia, cataracts and cancer predisposition are shared between BubR1-insufficient mice and MVA patients. Microcephaly, Dandy-Walker malformation and seizures are prominent human features (Kajii 2001, PMID 11746029; García-Castillo 2008, PMID 18548531) that are not the headline phenotypes of the mouse models. Infertility is a mouse phenotype (Baker 2004) with a human counterpart in the PCS trait found during male infertility work-up.

## 4. Interventions that changed outcomes

### 4.1 Tested in BubR1 hypomorphic mice

| Intervention | Regimen | Outcome and effect size | Reference | Approved medicine? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| p16^Ink4a genetic ablation | germline cross | Attenuates senescence and premature ageing in skeletal muscle and fat | Baker 2008, PMID 18516091 | Not a drug |
| p19^Arf genetic ablation | germline cross | **Worsens** senescence and ageing | Baker 2008, PMID 18516091 | Not a drug |
| INK-ATTAC senescent-cell suicide + AP20187 | 0.2 µg/g IP every 3 days; lifelong from 3 weeks, or late-life from 5 months | Fat mass, gastrocnemius fibre diameter, treadmill distance, cataract and lordokyphosis onset all improved (P<0.05). Lifespan **not** substantially extended; mice still died of cardiac failure | Baker 2011, *Nature* 479:232, PMID 22048312 | AP20187 is a research chemical, not approved |
| SIRT2 transgene on BubR1^H/H | constitutive overexpression | +58% median and +21% maximal lifespan versus BubR1^H/H (n=31 vs 33, P=0.0384); +123% median in males, no change in females; reversed the ECG J-point defect. Dwarfism, lordokyphosis and cataract not reversed | North 2014, *EMBO J* 33:1438, PMID 24825348 | Not a drug |
| BubR1 overexpression (transgene T23) | constitutive, wild-type background | Median and maximum lifespan extended, fewer spontaneous tumours, delayed age-related aneuploidy | Baker 2013, PMID 23242215 | Not a drug |

Contradicting evidence on the sirtuin arm: SIRT2 transgenic overexpression did not extend lifespan in a later study (Wu 2023, *Aging Cell*, PMID 38009412).

### 4.2 Adjacent evidence, not tested in BubR1 mice

- **NMN.** North 2014 (PMID 24825348) gave NMN 500 mg/kg/day IP for 7 days to young and 30-month C57BL/6 mice; NAD+ rose in heart and testes and BubR1 protein increased in testes. NMN was never given to BubR1^H/H mice and that paper contains no NMN lifespan or healthspan data. NMN and nicotinamide riboside are supplements, not medicines, in the US or EU.
- **Dasatinib + quercetin. Correction to the brief:** Zhu 2015, *Aging Cell* 14:644 (PMID 25754370) did not test D+Q in BubR1^H/H mice. The progeroid model was *Ercc1^−/Δ*; the cardiac result was in chronologically old mice 5 days after a single dose, and the exercise-capacity result was in irradiated-limb mice, sustained ≥7 months after one dose. Regimen: dasatinib 5 mg/kg + quercetin 50 mg/kg by oral gavage. Xu 2018, *Nat Med* (PMID 29988130): the same doses bi-weekly from 24-27 months in naturally aged mice gave +36% median post-treatment lifespan, mortality hazard 64.9%, P=0.01. **Dasatinib is approved (Sprycel, FDA and EMA 2006, Ph+ CML and Ph+ ALL, generics available); quercetin is a supplement.**
- **Navitoclax / ABT-263.** Senolytic in HUVEC, IMR90 and MEFs but not human preadipocytes (Zhu 2016, PMID 26711051); 50 mg/kg/day oral, 7 days per cycle × 2 cycles, depleted senescent HSCs and muscle stem cells in irradiated and aged mice (Chang 2016, *Nat Med*, PMID 26657143). **Not approved.**
- **Fisetin.** 500 ppm in chow (~60 mg/kg/day), or 100 mg/kg gavage × 5 days; started at 85 weeks it extended median and maximal lifespan (Yousefzadeh 2018, PMID 30279143). **Supplement, not a drug.**
- **INK-ATTAC in naturally aged mice.** Baker 2016, *Nature* (PMID 26840489).
- **Rapamycin, metformin, resveratrol, N-acetylcysteine, caloric restriction, exercise, GH/IGF-1.** No study of any of these in BubR1 mice or MVA patient cells was found. Rapamycin's lifespan effect in heterogeneous mice (Harrison 2009, PMID 19587680) is background only; sirolimus is approved (Rapamune; nab-sirolimus/Fyarro for PEComa) and metformin is approved (Glucophage, type 2 diabetes), but neither has been tested here, and a PubMed search for aneuploidy plus metformin returns nothing testing aneuploidy-selective killing. Baker 2013 applied paraquat (60 mg/kg IP) and isoproterenol (680 mg/kg) to BubR1 transgenics with no difference in oxidative-stress resistance, arguing against an antioxidant mechanism.

### 4.3 Harm screen: agents that worsen segregation in SAC-compromised cells

- **Taxanes with reduced BubR1 is the most directly relevant hazard.** Janssen 2009, *PNAS* 106:19108 (PMID 19855003): partial reduction of BubR1 or Mps1 in tumour cells caused mild mis-segregation but no lethality, yet made those cells "remarkably more sensitive to low doses of taxol", which amplified the number and severity of segregation errors. Reducing Mad2, a checkpoint-only component, did not sensitise. Untransformed human fibroblasts with reduced Mps1 were not sensitised. Paclitaxel (Taxol, Abraxane) and docetaxel (Taxotere) are approved in the US and EU.
- Complete SAC abrogation kills: reducing BubR1 or Mad2, or inhibiting BubR1 kinase, provokes apoptotic death within six divisions in CIN colorectal cancer cells (Kops 2004, *PNAS*, PMID 15159543).
- Dose-response: low mis-segregation rates promote tumours, high rates cause cell death and tumour suppression, and cells with a pre-existing elevated basal mis-segregation rate are the ones pushed into lethality (Silk 2013, *PNAS*, PMID 24133140). Mis-segregated chromosomes are damaged in the cleavage furrow, so aneugens also become mutagens (Janssen 2011, *Science*, PMID 21960636).
- **Mps1/TTK inhibitors**: reversine (Santaguida 2010, *J Cell Biol*, PMID 20624901), AZ3146 (Hewitt 2010, PMID 20624899), NMS-P715 (Colombo 2010, *Cancer Res*, PMID 21159646), BOS172722 (Anderhub 2019, *Mol Cancer Ther*, PMID 31575759, which states explicitly that a compromised SAC confers sensitivity and that the compound synergises with paclitaxel to produce gross segregation defects). None approved.
- **Aurora B inhibitors**: hesperadin (Hauf 2003, *J Cell Biol*, PMID 12707311) and ZM447439 (Ditchfield 2003, *J Cell Biol*, PMID 12719470, which shows Aurora B targets BubR1 itself to kinetochores). Barasertib likewise. None approved.
- **PLK1 inhibitors**: BI 2536 (Steegmaier 2007, *Curr Biol*, PMID 17291758) and volasertib (Rudolph 2009, *Clin Cancer Res*, PMID 19383823). None approved.
- **Approved aneugens**: colchicine is the reference aneugenic positive control in the OECD TG 487 in-vitro micronucleus validation series (Kirchner 2010, PMID 20362070; Whitwell 2010, PMID 20478394); approved as Colcrys/Mitigare for gout and FMF and as Lodoco for cardiovascular risk. Griseofulvin is a classic spindle aneugen (Andrioli 2014, PMID 25308540) and an approved antifungal. Vinca alkaloids (vincristine, vinblastine, vinorelbine) are approved spindle poisons acting through the same SAC-dependent arrest; no BubR1-specific hypersensitivity paper was found for them, so the taxane data remain the defensible citation.

**Gap:** no published study shows that MVA/*BUB1B* patient-derived cells are hypersensitive to taxanes, Mps1 inhibitors or aneugens. The argument currently rests on engineered BubR1-knockdown tumour cells and SAC-compromised cancer lines, not on patient material.

## 5. Aneuploidy tolerance and cancer

Aneuploidy imposes a shared physiological and proliferative cost. Disomic haploid yeast strains show common defects (Torres 2007, *Science*, PMID 17702937); trisomy for any of four mouse chromosomes impairs proliferation and alters metabolism and immortalisation (Williams 2008, *Science*, PMID 18974345); the transcriptional response is stereotyped across species (Sheltzer 2012, *PNAS*, PMID 22802626); aneuploidy causes proteotoxic stress (Oromendia 2012, *Genes Dev*, PMID 23222101). Nearly all engineered trisomic lines grow poorly in vitro and as xenografts, so single-chromosome gains commonly function as tumour suppressors (Sheltzer 2017, *Cancer Cell*, PMID 28089890). Review: Santaguida & Amon 2015, *Nat Rev Mol Cell Biol*, PMID 26204159.

**Proteostasis.** Human aneuploid cells are impaired in HSP90-mediated folding and show a blunted heat-shock response; HSF1 overexpression was identified as the first aneuploidy-tolerating mutation in human cells (Donnelly 2014, *EMBO J*, PMID 25205676). Protein aggregation mediates complex stoichiometry in aneuploid cells (Brennan 2019, *Genes Dev*, PMID 31196865).

**The three aneuploidy-selective compounds.** Tang 2011, *Cell* 144:499 (PMID 21315436) identified AICAR (energy stress/AMPK), 17-AAG (HSP90) and chloroquine (autophagy). AICAR induced p53-mediated apoptosis in primary MEFs trisomic for chromosome 1, 13, 16 or 19 at 0.2-0.5 mM; 17-AAG inhibited proliferation of all aneuploid cells at 100 nM; chloroquine was selective but "not as dramatic"; AICAR plus 17-AAG was the most effective combination. Follow-ups: aneuploidy-induced stress limits autophagic degradation and triggers a TFEB lysosomal stress response (Santaguida 2015, PMID 26404941 and PMID 26571033).

**Mis-segregation, arrest and immune clearance.** Chromosome mis-segregation generates cell-cycle-arrested cells with complex karyotypes, senescence features and pro-inflammatory signalling that drives immune clearance (Santaguida 2017, *Dev Cell*, PMID 28633018).

**p53.** Proliferation of aneuploid human cells is limited by a p53-dependent mechanism with nuclear p53 and p21 accumulation (Thompson & Compton 2010, *J Cell Biol*, PMID 20123995). The ATM-p53 pathway suppresses aneuploidy-induced tumorigenesis in vivo (Li 2010, *PNAS*, PMID 20663956).

**Modern CIN vulnerabilities.** Across ~1,000 cancer cell lines, aneuploid cells were less sensitive to short-term SAC inhibition but became increasingly sensitive over time, with KIF18A depletion the specific vulnerability (Cohen-Sharir 2021, *Nature*, PMID 33505028). Whole-genome-doubled cells depend on SAC signalling, replication factors, proteasome function and KIF18A (Quinton 2021, *Nature*, PMID 33505027). A first-in-class KIF18A inhibitor exists preclinically (Payton 2024, *Nat Cancer*, PMID 38151625).

**Approval reality check.** Chloroquine and hydroxychloroquine are approved (malaria; HCQ also lupus and RA) but have no oncology indication. 17-AAG (tanespimycin) is not approved anywhere; development stopped after phase II/III. One HSP90 inhibitor, pimitespib (TAS-116), is approved in Japan for advanced GIST but not in the US or EU. AICAR/acadesine is not approved and reached phase I/II in relapsed CLL (Van Den Neste 2013, PMID 23228986). KIF18A inhibitors are not approved. No vulnerability in this section has an approved drug indicated for aneuploidy or CIN.

## 6. Rhabdomyosarcoma in MVA

**Frequency.** Hanks 2004 (PMID 15475955): 2 of 5 MVA families had embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma. Kajii 2001, *Am J Med Genet* 104:57 (PMID 11746029), the largest early PCS/MVA tumour series (5 new plus 5 reviewed = 10 infants): 7 of 10 developed proven or probable Wilms tumours (bilateral in 4, unilateral in 3, cystic in 6, diagnosed at 2-16 months) and 2 of 10 developed botryoid rhabdomyosarcoma. Jacquemont 2002, *Am J Med Genet* (PMID 11932988): 3 of 14 patients (21%) developed a malignancy, rhabdomyosarcoma, ALL and nephroblastoma. Scott 2006, *J Med Genet* 43:705 (PMID 16690728) records Wilms tumour in 10 of 40 published MVA cases (25%) and notes that biallelic *BUB1B* mutations cause around 50% of MVA. Suijkerbuijk 2010 states that 37% of MVA patients develop cancer. A 2022 collation reports malignancy in 38.7% of cases, most often Wilms tumour, rhabdomyosarcoma and leukaemia, though its table carries no genotypes and its denominator is internally inconsistent (Pavone 2022, *Neurol Sci* 43:6529, PMID 35804254). Nakano 2024 (PMID 39264246) records MVA1 tumours as predominantly Wilms tumour and rhabdomyosarcoma, with MDS, AML and ALL also reported; MVA3 predominantly Wilms; MVA2 none in 15 published individuals. García-Castillo 2008 (PMID 18548531) gives no separate RMS count in the retrievable record.

**Subtype and site.** Every MVA case in which histology is stated is embryonal, often botryoid. Limwongse 1999, *Am J Med Genet* (PMID 9916837): embryonal RMS of the soft palate in a 7-year-old. Furukawa 2003, *J Pediatr Hematol Oncol* (PMID 14608201): cystic partially differentiated nephroblastoma plus embryonal RMS. Hanks 2006, *Cancer Lett* (PMID 16182441): CGH on an MVA embryonal RMS showed aneuploidies typical of sporadic E-RMS (gain of 3, 8, 13; loss of 9, 14, X); screening 30 Wilms, 10 ALL, 9 RMS and 11 RMS cell lines found somatic *BUB1B* mutation unlikely to be common in sporadic childhood cancer. Schneider 2020, *Transl Androl Urol* (PMID 33209717) collected 5 genitourinary MVA cases, 3 girls with vaginal RMS (13, 8, 5 months) and 2 boys with bladder RMS (13 and 7 months, one with prostate extension), and states that RMS in MVA is highly likely to be genitourinary, primarily vagina and bladder, and more likely embryonal with botryoid features. Vempuluru 2026, *Orbit* (PMID 42595739) reports orbital embryonal RMS at age 12 in an MVA3 (*TRIP13*) patient who had had Wilms tumour at 3.

**Treatment and toxicity.** The evidence base is thin. Nishitani-Isa 2019, *Pediatr Int* 61:613 (PMID 31184400) is titled "Rhabdomyosarcoma with premature chromatid separation-mosaic variegated aneuploidy syndrome: Reduced-intensity chemotherapy"; no abstract is indexed in PubMed or Europe PMC, so the agents, doses and outcome are not citable without the PDF. The clearest documented toxicity is Ochiai 2019, *Pediatr Blood Cancer* (PMID 31081598): a 38-month-old with PCS/MVA and biallelic *BUB1B* mutations and bilateral Wilms tumour received dactinomycin monotherapy after right nephrectomy, but "severe adverse reaction prevented the patient from receiving further chemotherapy"; left nephrectomy followed without postoperative chemotherapy, and the child survived 15 months on home peritoneal dialysis without relapse or RMS. The nature of the adverse reaction is not specified in the abstract. Laberko 2019, *Orphanet J Rare Dis* (PMID 31053147) reports the first HSCT in MVA1: treosulfan-based reduced-intensity conditioning was tolerated with no severe early toxicity; the graft was rejected on day +47 and the child died on day +156 of sepsis, so the failure was immunological rather than toxic.

**Radiation.** No paper documents a radiosensitivity phenotype, a radiation-toxicity case, or a clonogenic or chromosomal radiosensitivity assay in MVA/PCS patients. Nakano 2024 discusses treatment sensitivity for ataxia-telangiectasia and Nijmegen breakage syndrome but not for MVA. **[inference]** MVA is a spindle-checkpoint disorder, not a double-strand-break repair disorder, so the AT/NBS radiosensitivity precedent (Distel 2003, PMID 12764742, fatal toxicity after 36 Gy craniospinal radiotherapy in unrecognised NBS; Seidemann 2000, PMID 10707797, 9 AT/NBS patients in 1,569 NHL-BFM registrations, none irradiated, 1 toxic death, 5/9 in first remission) should not be transferred to MVA without evidence. Note the confounder in Sari 2009, *Pediatr Blood Cancer* (PMID 19373780), a PCS plus Fanconi anaemia overlap case in which alkylator hypersensitivity has an independent explanation.

## 7. Open questions and inference

**Shown.** Biallelic *BUB1B* mutation causes MVA1 with embryonal tumour predisposition, characteristically one truncating plus one missense allele, with no reported biallelic-missense or biallelic-truncating cases (Hanks 2004; Suijkerbuijk 2010). Truncating alleles act by transcript loss: 386X and 731X proteins are undetectable in patient cells and reappear only when NMD is bypassed (Suijkerbuijk 2010); 1833delT and splice alleles show reduced transcript (Matsuura 2006). Kinase-domain missense alleles act mainly by lowering abundance 5-10 fold, and restoring expression restores checkpoint function (Suijkerbuijk 2010). Human BUBR1 catalysis is dispensable for error-free segregation, while the C-terminal domain has a structural and scaffolding role in PP2A-B56 recruitment (Suijkerbuijk 2012; Gama Braga 2020); evidence for genuine catalysis exists but the decisive structure is from *Drosophila* (Chan 1999; Mao 2003; Huang 2019). BubR1 insufficiency in mice causes aneuploidy, senescence, p16-driven progeroid pathology, dwarfism, sarcopenia, cataracts and infertility, and p16 ablation, INK-ATTAC clearance, SIRT2 overexpression and BubR1 overexpression each improve defined outcomes (Baker 2004, 2008, 2011, 2013; North 2014). SAC-weakened cells are sensitised to taxol (Janssen 2009). Aneuploid cells carry proteotoxic, energy and autophagic vulnerabilities targetable in vitro by AICAR, 17-AAG and chloroquine (Tang 2011). Embryonal, often botryoid and often genitourinary rhabdomyosarcoma is a recurrent MVA tumour, and one MVA patient could not complete dactinomycin because of severe toxicity (Ochiai 2019).

**Inferred, not shown.**
- That c.2210T>G triggers NMD in this patient. Predicted from the 50-55 nt rule and the transcript geometry (PTC 747 nt upstream of the last junction, 6 downstream junctions); not measured on this allele. The class evidence from Suijkerbuijk 2010 concerns other truncating alleles.
- That p.(Leu737Ter) is disease-causing in a patient sense. Its ClinVar call rests on mechanism, not on any published MVA case, and gnomAD v4 holds 115 heterozygotes at AF 7.87e-5.
- That p.(Asn1002Lys) belongs to the destabilising class. Positional analogy only; Q921H, also inside the domain, behaved as wild type in Suijkerbuijk 2010, so position does not predict class.
- That biallelic truncating-plus-missense carries higher embryonal-tumour risk than other MVA genotypes. No source reports a per-genotype tumour fraction: Yost 2017 gives the denominator (14) without a numerator, and all five Hanks 2004 biallelic families shared the same configuration.
- That MVA patient cells are hypersensitive to taxanes or spindle agents. Supported only by engineered knockdown systems.
- That MVA patients are radiosensitive. No evidence either way; the mechanistic argument runs against it.
- That metformin substitutes for AICAR in aneuploidy-selective killing. Not tested.

**Corrections to assumptions in the brief.**
1. Zhu 2015 (PMID 25754370) tested D+Q in *Ercc1^−/Δ*, aged and irradiated mice, not BubR1^H/H. No senolytic drug has been tested in BubR1^H/H mice.
2. Suijkerbuijk's pseudokinase paper is *Dev Cell* 22:1321 (PMID 22698286), not *Curr Biol*.
3. No "Breit 2015 human BubR1 pseudokinase domain structure" exists (PMID 26658523 is about Bub1); no human BUBR1 kinase-domain structure exists in the PDB.
4. Kang/Yu 2008 (PMID 18995837) is on Bub1, not BubR1.
5. No kinase-dead *Bub1b* knock-in mouse exists; the human catalytic lysine is K795, not K802.
6. Leu737 lies just upstream of the kinase domain (766-1050); Asn1002 is inside it and in the final exon.
7. p.(Asn1002Lys) is not absent from ClinVar (VCV004600147.1, uncertain significance, Ambry, 19 Sep 2025).
8. Hanks 2004 contains no western blot; the ~6% and ~13% thresholds come from a HeLa shRNA titration, not patient cells.
9. Snape 2011 does not state that CEP57 patients lack premature chromatid separation; it states that no cancers had been reported. The minimal-SAC-deficiency finding is Yost 2017.
10. "BubR1 H/H mice are not spontaneously tumour-prone" overstates the data (Sieben 2020: they do not live long enough to assess it).
11. Residual BubR1 in the H/H hypomorph is ~10%, not ~11%.

**Unresolved.** No published percentage residual BubR1 in MVA patient cells (fold-changes only). No functional characterisation of p.(Asn1002Lys). No RMS-specific denominators in García-Castillo 2008. No retrievable regimen details for the one "reduced-intensity chemotherapy" MVA RMS report (PMID 31184400). No report of ifosfamide, etoposide or a named VAC regimen in an MVA patient; no MVA patient with delivered radiotherapy and a reported outcome. No systematic review of MVA cancer treatment outcomes for 2015-2026. OMIM 257300 and Malumbres 2024 (PMID 39169218) were not retrievable, so no figures are taken from either.
