# Research question queue

Open questions that remain after the audit round, the cross-model passes, the synthesis and the primary-data re-analysis. Worked one at a time. Each answered question gets a file `track2/research_loop/Q<n>_<slug>.md` and its row here is marked done with the date and the one-line answer.

**Rules for every answer.** Primary sources only. Every claim carries its source and the access level reached. A dead end is written up as a dead end, with the routes tried, so nobody repeats it. Numbers are recomputed, not read across. Nothing requires contact with the family or the organisers. No em or en dashes.

**Ordering.** By what the answer changes per unit of effort, not by how interesting it is.

## Priority 1: clinical framing, the gap no amount of source-checking closes

The audit checks whether claims are true. It cannot check whether the document is the right document. These are the questions that reach written clinical judgement, which is the nearest substitute for a real reader.

| Q1 | Does the 75 percent cancer-risk figure attributed to Orphanet ORPHA 1052 exist, and if so where does it come from? | The 33.3 percent decision threshold row in section 8, which currently rests on a figure this project could not retrieve. If it cannot be sourced, the row comes out. | **done 6 Sep 2026.** No counted series produces it. Pavone 2022 gives 38.7 percent as 12 of 31; Hanks 2004 gives no percentage; a third figure of 37 percent also circulates. The 75 percent is a pre-2012 database summary with no reachable denominator, and section 8 now says so. |
| Q2 | Which of the 28 claims citing Suijkerbuijk 2010 have a second independent source, and which are single-sourced? | The robustness of the whole mechanism section against one 2010 paper. The 731X episode showed what happens when that paper is misread. | **done 6 Sep 2026.** Half survives independently: Matsuura 2006 (Hiroshima, no author overlap) replicates NMD, low BubR1 with a checkpoint defect, and rescue by restoring BUB1B. Four claims are single-laboratory, including the kinase-domain-dispensable result the allele 1 argument rests on. Suijkerbuijk 2010 and 2012 share first and senior author, and 2010 carries the Hanks 2004 authors, so those are not three independent lines. |
| Q3 | Has any MVA or *BUB1B*-deficient patient-derived cell line ever been tested against a spindle agent, in any publication? | The avoidance rule's premise. The report says no such test exists; that is an absence claim and absence claims need a documented search. | **done 6 Sep 2026.** Yes, three times in two independent laboratories: colcemid on MVA fibroblasts (Matsuura 2000 and 2006) and S-trityl-L-cysteine on two patient lines (Suijkerbuijk 2010 Fig 1B). Every readout is checkpoint arrest, none is killing or micronucleus induction, and no clinical agent or concentration was used, so the report's question survives but its absence claim does not. Also found the only published clinical precedent, Nishitani-Isa 2019, an MVA child with rhabdomyosarcoma given reduced-intensity chemotherapy; paywalled, title only. |
| Q4 | What is the real within-patient variance of micronucleus frequency and interphase FISH aneuploid fraction in any human population? | Section 8's open question 10, and therefore whether any power figure in the report can ever become a sample size. | **done 6 Sep 2026.** Derivable for micronuclei: between-subject CV 0.615 (Gajski 2018) with test-retest R2 of 0.83, 0.74, 0.68 (Bolognesi 2019) gives within-subject CV 0.183 to 0.257. The assumed 0.20 is inside the range at its optimistic end, and no conclusion in section 8 changes. Both sources adult, different cohorts, none in this disease. Also corrected a misattributed PMID in the appendix, 16140629 to 16140632. |
| Q5 | Is there a published human within-subject repeatability figure for the aneuploid fraction specifically, as opposed to micronuclei? | Whether the FISH endpoint the gate leads with can be powered at all. | **done 6 Sep 2026. Dead end, and that is the finding.** No study measures the aneuploid fraction by interphase FISH twice in the same healthy people. Nearest is Auer 1999, a four-year repeat of trisomy 12 in 41 CLL patients, which bounds clonal drift not measurement noise. So the FISH row is the only one of the three with no biological term and none available, and its apparent advantage is a missing term rather than a property of the assay. Two baseline draws would settle it for this child. |

| Q21 | The de Wolf thesis chapter 3 claims "a striking correlation between the mutations in these genes and the occurrence of specific childhood cancers in MVA patients". Does the published version hold, and does it let surveillance be targeted by genotype? | Whether the four surveillance additions should be uniform or genotype-directed. This is the single most actionable clinical claim found so far. | **done 6 Sep 2026.** Holds, and is published as Yost 2017 (Nat Genet 49:1148, PMC5493194). The predictor is severity of checkpoint failure, not gene identity: BUB1B and TRIP13 carry embryonal tumour risk with severe SAC impairment, CEP57 and unknown-cause MVA do not and retain the checkpoint. Their words: these individuals "require close surveillance". Control cells hold mitotic arrest over 2 hours, high-risk genotypes escape within 1, so the gate's arrest-duration readout is prognostic and not merely mechanistic. No per-tumour-type breakdown within the high-risk pair. |
