# Q3. Has any MVA or *BUB1B*-deficient patient cell ever been tested against a spindle agent?

**Asked because** `REPORT_track2.md` section 9 states "no MVA cell has been tested", and the carve-out box states that no study has shown MVA or *BUB1B* patient cells to be hypersensitive to any spindle agent. Those are absence claims. An absence claim is only as good as the search behind it, and no search had been recorded.

## Answer

**The claim as written is wrong, and the claim it should have made is right.** MVA patient cells have been challenged with spindle agents at least three times, in two independent laboratories, going back to 2000. What has never been done is the measurement the report actually needs.

### What has been done

| Study | Cells | Agent | Readout | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Matsuura 2000, Am J Hum Genet 67:483, PMID 10877982, PMC1287192 | Skin fibroblasts from two unrelated MVA and total PCS infants | **Colcemid, 1 microgram/ml**, harvested at 6-hour intervals to 24 hours | Mitotic index, BrdU incorporation, flow cytometry, nuclear morphology | No mitotic block. Cells entered G1 and S without segregation or cytokinesis, accumulated a DNA content of 8C, and "cells with multiple small nuclei appeared and accumulated, accounting for 27.0% to 27.9% of the cells after 24 h of treatment" |
| Matsuura 2006, Am J Med Genet A 140:358, PMID 16411201 | Patient cells from seven Japanese PCS families | **Colcemid** | Checkpoint response, restored by microcell-mediated transfer of chromosome 15 | Abnormal response, corrected by restoring *BUB1B* |
| Suijkerbuijk 2010, Cancer Res 70:4891, Figure 1B | Patient fibroblasts, genotypes 753X/R814H and 731X/Y155C | **S-trityl-L-cysteine**, an Eg5 inhibitor | Time to mitotic exit | 50 percent of cells exited within 68 minutes and 114 minutes respectively, against a normal checkpoint arrest |

Three challenges, two laboratories, no author overlap between Hiroshima and Utrecht.

### What has never been done, which is what the report needs

Every one of those readouts is **checkpoint function**: does the cell arrest. None of them is **cytotoxicity**, and none is a **micronucleus dose-response**. None used a clinically administered agent: colcemid and S-trityl-L-cysteine are laboratory tools, not medicines, and no vinca alkaloid or taxane appears anywhere. None used a clinically achievable concentration; 1 microgram per millilitre of colcemid is a saturating checkpoint challenge, not a therapeutic exposure.

So the question the report asks in section 9, whether this child's cells are more or less sensitive to vinca alkaloids, remains genuinely unanswered, and the direction is still open both ways. What changes is that the report can no longer say nobody has looked. Somebody looked three times, at a different question.

**The finding also cuts slightly against the report's own framing.** Matsuura 2000 shows MVA patient cells exposed to a microtubule poison do not arrest and instead accumulate multinucleate cells at 27 percent by 24 hours. That is the mechanistic direction the aneugen avoidance rule assumes, observed in patient material rather than in engineered knockdown lines, and the report was citing only engineered knockdown data (Janssen 2009) for it. It is not evidence of clinical hypersensitivity, because there is no killing measurement, but it is closer to the point than what the report had.

### A second find, which is clinical rather than laboratory

**Nishitani-Isa M, Hiraumi Y, Nishida Y, Usami I, Maihara T. "Rhabdomyosarcoma with premature chromatid separation-mosaic variegated aneuploidy syndrome: Reduced-intensity chemotherapy." Pediatr Int 2019;61(6):613-616, PMID 31184400, DOI 10.1111/ped.13849.**

A treating team faced exactly this situation, an MVA child with rhabdomyosarcoma, and gave reduced-intensity chemotherapy. This project never found it, and it is the only published clinical precedent for the decision the carve-out is written about.

It does not contradict the carve-out, which bars de-escalation **on the basis of this report** and explicitly leaves regimen decisions to the treating oncologist and the tumour board. But a document that tells a team not to reduce chemotherapy should say that at least one team has, in this syndrome, and that its reasoning is not reachable here.

**Access level: title, authors, journal and keywords only.** The case report has no abstract in PubMed, no abstract in Europe PMC, is marked not open access, and the full text sits behind Wiley. What dose was reduced, by how much, on what reasoning, and with what outcome are all unknown to this project. Retrieving it is worth more than most items on the paywalled list in `track2/synthesis/NEXT.md`, because it is the only clinical precedent that exists.

### Corrections applied

Section 9 question 6 no longer says no MVA cell has been tested. It says what was tested, by whom, with which readout, and names the measurement that is missing. The carve-out discussion in section 5 now records the Nishitani-Isa case and that its content could not be reached.
