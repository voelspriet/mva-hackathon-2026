# Q18. Should anything be said to the carrier parents?

**Asked because** the report measures both parents in five of its nine gate items, uses their reproductive history as evidence in the phase calculation, and says nothing about what any of it means for them. A 2026 paper linking heterozygous *BUB1B* variants to recurrent pregnancy loss made the omission worth checking.

## Answer

**The report's consent architecture is better than expected and does not need rewriting. One thing is genuinely missing, and it is not counselling. It is naming, in advance, what the parents' own abnormal results will and will not mean.**

The existing text already treats both parents as separate research subjects with their own consent and "their own plan for what they are told about their own results, carrier and PCS status included", and already requires diagnostic-grade pre-test counselling because a cis result would change "recurrence information for both parents". That is the right structure. What it lacks is any statement of what is actually known, so the plan it demands has nothing to be built from.

### What the parents' results will show

Both parents are obligate carriers, and carrier status is not cellularly silent.

| Measure | Carriers | Controls |
|---|---|---|
| Premature chromatid separation, G-banding | 8.93 and 13.79 percent in two heterozygous *BUB1B* carriers (Wang 2026, Front Endocrinol 17:1838559) | 0.40 ± 1.05 percent, n=20 |
| Premature chromatid separation, carrier parents of MVA children | 3.5 to 41.7 percent (Kajii 2001, PMID 11746029) | no control group in that paper |
| BubR1 protein, parental lines | "consistently in between those of patients and unrelated healthy controls" (Suijkerbuijk 2010) | |

So gate items 3 and 4 will, with high probability, return abnormal numbers on both parents. That is not an incidental finding. It is the expected result.

### What is known about what those numbers mean: almost nothing, and the literature says so

**Cancer risk in carriers is an open question, named in print as a question about MVA parents specifically.** Hahn MM, Vreede L, Bemelmans SA, van der Looij E, van Kessel AG, Schackert HK, Ligtenberg MJ, Hoogerbrugge N, Kuiper RP, de Voer RM. "Prevalence of germline mutations in the spindle assembly checkpoint gene BUB1B in individuals with early-onset colorectal cancer." Genes Chromosomes Cancer 2016;55(11):855-863, PMID 27239782. They sequenced *BUB1B* in 192 people with colorectal cancer at or under 50, found no homozygotes or compound heterozygotes, and two rare heterozygous variants that did not affect BubR1 expression or localisation. Their conclusion, verbatim:

> "Our data suggest that mutations in BUB1B do not occur frequently in the germline of individuals with CRC and that BUB1B unlikely plays a major role in the predisposition to early-onset CRC. Whether carriers of pathogenic BUB1B mutations, such as the parents of MVA syndrome patients, have an increased risk for cancer remains of interest, as studies in mice have suggested that haploinsufficiency of BUB1B may cause an increase in carcinogen-induced tumors."

The mouse basis for the interest is Dai W et al., "Slippage of mitotic arrest and enhanced tumor development in mice with BubR1 haploinsufficiency", Cancer Res 2004;64(2):440-445, PMID 14744753. This report's own discipline, established when a mouse residue number was misapplied to a human variant, says that does not transfer.

**Reproductive risk has one new and preliminary source.** Wang 2026 reports two unrelated families with unexplained recurrent pregnancy loss, random aneuploidies in six miscarriage specimens, and novel heterozygous *BUB1B* variants classified as variants of uncertain significance, with elevated premature chromatid separation and a trend to reduced BubR1. The authors put it no higher than "the first clinical evidence suggesting that heterozygous BUB1B variants may impair SAC fidelity". Two families and two VUS is a hypothesis, not a risk figure.

### What follows, and what does not

**Does not follow:** any surveillance recommendation for the parents. There is no evidence base for one, the only human study looked for colorectal cancer and found nothing, and inventing carrier surveillance from a mouse would repeat the error this report already corrected once.

**Does follow:** that the plan the report already demands should be written against these three states of knowledge, in advance of the sample, in plain terms. A parent told "your cells show ten times the chromosome separation errors of controls" and nothing else has been handed an alarming number with no meaning attached. The honest frame is that the finding is expected in carriers, that whether it carries any cancer risk is unresolved and named as unresolved in the literature, that a possible link to pregnancy loss rests on two families, and that none of it currently changes anything anyone would do.

**Also does follow:** the report uses the parents' reproductive history as evidence. The phase calculation applies a likelihood ratio for parental recurrent miscarriage. Using that as data while saying nothing about what carrier status means to them is the asymmetry worth closing, and closing it costs one paragraph.

## Correction applied

The consent architecture section gains a short passage stating what the parents' own results are expected to show, that carrier cancer risk is an open question named as such in the literature, that the pregnancy-loss link rests on two families, and that no carrier surveillance follows from any of it. No counselling is offered and no contact is proposed; the hackathon rules forbid contact with the family and this report is not a counselling document.
