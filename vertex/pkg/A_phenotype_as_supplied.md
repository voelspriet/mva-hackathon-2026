Notes for Participants

Interpreting the Phenotype

No single feature in isolation is diagnostic. The clinical significance lies in recognising the co-occurrence of cancer predisposition, growth restriction, renal anomalies, adverse perinatal history, and parental reproductive loss as a coherent phenotypic cluster. Participants are encouraged to consider the full constellation of features rather than optimising for individual findings.

Family History as a Signal

The history of recurrent miscarriages is clinically significant and should be treated as part of the phenotypic input, not merely as background. In chromosomal instability disorders, reproductive loss in parents or other family members can reflect heritable or de novo mechanisms that may not be apparent without genomic investigation.
