# I. The pharmacist's layer: can it be given, and what the pharmacy asks for first

From a paediatric oncology and nephrology dispensary. Reference child: about 15 kg, BSA 0.62 m2, nephrocalcinosis, low muscle mass (creatinine eGFR overreads; apply renal rules to cystatin C), rhabdomyosarcoma treated with vincristine, actinomycin D, cyclophosphamide or ifosfamide, possibly vinorelbine plus oral cyclophosphamide maintenance. Efficacy is not judged. Prices approximate, September 2026.

## Potassium citrate

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Formulations | US: potassium citrate/citric acid solution USP, about 2 mmol K/mL (Cytra-K, generics). UK: Potassium Citrate Mixture BP, 28 mmol K/10 mL, licensed (emc 4922). DE: Kalinor effervescent (40 mmol units). NL: kaliumcitraat drank FNA. No compounding. |
| 15 kg dose, renal | 0.3 to 0.6 mmol/kg/day = 4.5 to 9 mmol K/day in three doses, urine pH 6.5 to 7.2, never above 7.5. About 2 to 3 mL/day BP mixture. Contraindicated in hyperkalaemia or severe renal impairment; below eGFR 45, serum K within 48 hours of each change. |
| Interactions | Potassium stacking with spironolactone, amiloride, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, tacrolimus, and trimethoprim (co-trimoxazole PJP prophylaxis). |
| Monitoring | K, bicarbonate, cystatin C at baseline, 1 week, each change; home urine pH twice weekly; urine Ca/creatinine 3-monthly; renal ultrasound 6-monthly (PMC5976810). |
| Cost, status | Generic; 5 to 25 per month. Not on the FDA shortage list early 2026. |

## Hydrochlorothiazide

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Formulations | Tablets only in US, UK, NL, DE. Compounded 5 mg/mL in Ora-Plus/Ora-Sweet: 60 days, at least 91% retained (Allen and Erickson 1996, PMID 8893069); SickKids and CHEO recipes; FNA 1 mg/mL. |
| 15 kg dose, renal | 0.5 to 1 mg/kg/day in two doses = 7.5 to 15 mg/day, start 6.25 mg twice daily (NHS GGC 973). Unreliable below eGFR 30. |
| Interactions | Hypokalaemia feeds the QT stack below; hyponatraemia and volume contraction additive with cyclophosphamide and ifosfamide tubulopathy. Raises calcium: stop vitamin D and calcium supplements. |
| Monitoring | Na, K, Ca, Mg, glucose, cystatin C at baseline, 2 weeks, each increment, then 3-monthly. |
| Cost, status | Generic, under 5 per month; compounded bottle 15 to 40. No shortage. |

## Metformin

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Formulations | Oral solution 500 mg/5 mL licensed in UK (emc 5304) and US (Riomet, generics). DE, NL: tablets plus NRF/FNA recipes. Licensed from age 10; under 10 is off-label by age and indication. |
| 15 kg dose, renal | No approved dose. Paediatric oncology phase I 666 to 2000 mg/m2/day (PMC9972017): about 400 mg/day, 2 mL twice daily with food. SmPC: review below eGFR 45, stop below 30, hold on days of vomiting, dehydration, contrast, ifosfamide or cisplatin. |
| Interactions | No CYP involvement. OCT2/MATE inhibitors raise levels: trimethoprim, cimetidine. Lactic acidosis risk additive with vincristine ileus and thiazide dehydration. |
| Monitoring | Cystatin C and bicarbonate monthly for 3 months then 3-monthly; lactate if unwell; B12 yearly; weight. |
| Cost, status | Generic; tablets 2 to 5, solution 15 to 60 per month. No shortage. |

## Hydroxychloroquine

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Formulations | 200 mg tablets only. Compounded 25 mg/mL: 90 days in Oral Mix (IJPC 2017 abstract 4322; SickKids recipe), 30 days in other vehicles (PMC8129641). |
| 15 kg dose, renal | 5 mg/kg = 75 mg daily = 3 mL. No renal formula in SmPC or BNFC; below eGFR 45 the pharmacy asks for 4 mg/kg and an eye-screening plan. |
| Interactions | QT: hydroxychloroquine, dasatinib and ondansetron are all CredibleMeds-listed (PMC7994840); ondansetron comes with every VAC/IVA cycle, thiazide hypokalaemia underneath. |
| Monitoring | ECG at baseline, 1 week, and before every cycle with ondansetron, with K and Mg; FBC; G6PD once; retinal exam at baseline, yearly from 5 years or earlier with reduced renal function (J AAPOS 2023). |
| Cost, status | Generic; tablets under 10, compounded 20 to 50 per month. Supply normal. |

## Dasatinib

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Formulations | Tablets 20 to 140 mg (EU generics 2023, US 2024); do not crush. Powder for oral suspension 10 mg/mL (Sprycel PFOS, emc 10228; US), licensed from age 1: the only giveable form at 15 kg. No compounding while a licensed liquid exists. |
| 15 kg dose, renal | CML label 10 to <20 kg: 40 mg daily tablets; PFOS has its own weight table. Senolytic pulse (adult 100 mg days 1 to 3, NCT04733534) has no paediatric dose. Under 4% renal excretion, no adjustment. |
| Interactions | CYP3A4 substrate: azoles and clarithromycin raise exposure; label requires reduction or avoidance. Weak P-gp inhibitor: more vincristine and vinorelbine neuropathy possible. Acid suppression: famotidine cut AUC 61%, omeprazole 43% (FDA label); PPIs and H2 blockers not recommended, antacids 2 hours apart. QT as above. |
| Monitoring | FBC weekly for 8 weeks then monthly; ECG, K, Mg, Ca before each course; LFTs monthly; growth velocity 3-monthly (labelled growth warning). |
| Cost, status | Generic tablets 100 to 400 EUR (EU), 300 to 1,500 USD (US) per month; PFOS brand only, several thousand, single-source. |

## Sirolimus

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Formulations | Oral solution 1 mg/mL (Rapamune, generics, EU and US), tablets 0.5 to 2 mg. No compounding. |
| 15 kg dose, renal | 0.8 mg/m2 twice daily to trough 10 to 15 ng/mL (Adams 2016, PMID 26783326): about 0.5 mg twice daily. No renal adjustment; SmPC warns of proteinuria and delayed tubular recovery after injury. |
| Interactions | CYP3A4 and P-gp substrate, narrow window: azoles, macrolides raise trough two to tenfold; carbamazepine, phenytoin lower it. Shares CYP3A4 with vincristine, vinorelbine, cyclophosphamide, ifosfamide without inhibiting it; the overlap is mucositis, myelosuppression, wound healing, pneumonitis. |
| Monitoring | Trough at 5 to 7 days, after every dose or co-medication change, then monthly; FBC, lipids, urine protein, LFTs monthly; PJP prophylaxis decision recorded. |
| Cost, status | Generic; solution 150 to 400 per month. No shortage. |

## Everolimus

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Formulations | Dispersible tablets 2, 3, 5 mg (Afinitor Disperz US, Votubia EU), licensed from age 1 for TSC-SEGA, dispersed in water in an oral syringe. US generic exists. |
| 15 kg dose, renal | 4.5 mg/m2 daily = about 3 mg, trough 5 to 15 ng/mL (EXIST-1, PMID 27351628). No renal adjustment. |
| Interactions | As sirolimus; label halves the dose with moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors. Stomatitis 43% stacks with actinomycin D and vincristine mucositis. |
| Monitoring | Trough at 1 to 2 weeks and after changes, then monthly; FBC, glucose, lipids, LFTs, urine protein monthly. |
| Cost, status | EU 2,500 to 4,000 EUR per month; US generic about 11,000 USD per 28 tablets (Drugs.com 2026). No shortage. |

## Dexrazoxane

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Formulations | Powder for infusion 250 and 500 mg (Cardioxane EU; US generics). IV, 30 minutes before the anthracycline. |
| 15 kg dose, renal | 10:1 to doxorubicin: about 185 mg per 30 mg/m2 dose. FDA label: halve below creatinine clearance 40 mL/min. |
| Interactions | Only with doxorubicin (EpSSG IVADo). Adds myelosuppression. EU: contraindicated under 18 unless cumulative anthracycline exceeds 300 mg/m2 (EMA 2017 referral). US label adult breast cancer; paediatric use off-label but standard in COG. |
| Monitoring | FBC per cycle; echocardiogram and troponin per protocol. |
| Cost, status | Generic; 200 to 800 per 500 mg vial. ASHP: available. |

## Ataluren

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Formulations | Granules 125, 250, 1000 mg (Translarna). EU conditional authorisation not renewed, 28 March 2025 (EMA EPAR; Duchenne UK). Never US-approved. Unlicensed everywhere here: named-patient import in DE (section 73(3) AMG), NL (artsenverklaring, IGJ), UK (MHRA notification); single-patient IND plus IRB in the US. |
| 15 kg dose, renal | DMD regimen 10/10/20 mg/kg = 150/150/300 mg; sachets cannot be split, so 125/125/250. Former SmPC: avoid in severe renal impairment, no data in moderate. Confirm the L737* codon context first (UGA/UAG/UAA). |
| Interactions | Inhibits OATP1B3 and UGT1A9, induces CYP2B6 (former SmPC): cyclophosphamide and ifosfamide are partly activated by CYP2B6, so active-metabolite exposure could rise. Avoid IV aminoglycosides (nephrotoxicity signal in DMD). |
| Monitoring | Lipids, cystatin C, urea 6-monthly; blood pressure. |
| Cost, status | 250,000 to 350,000 EUR per year when reimbursed. Not obtainable through normal channels. |

## Nicotinamide (prescription forms)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Formulations | UK: 50 mg tablets, licensed for pellagra (BNF). DE, FR: Nicobion 500 mg, licensed. US: prescription medical-food tablets (DailyMed), OTC 500 mg. A compounded 50 mg/mL has no stability data: 7 to 14 day beyond-use (USP 795). |
| 15 kg dose, renal | No dose for this indication. Pellagra 100 to 300 mg/day; adult NAD studies 500 to 1,500 mg/day. Accumulates in renal impairment (thrombocytopenia in dialysis trials): cap 100 mg/day below eGFR 45. |
| Interactions | High dose lowers platelets, adding to chemotherapy nadirs. PARP inhibition at high concentration in a DNA-repair-stressed genome: prescriber records the direction. |
| Monitoring | FBC and LFTs monthly above 300 mg/day; phosphate 3-monthly. |
| Cost, status | Under 5 per month. No shortage. |

## Off-label and unlicensed paperwork

**UK.** GMC Good practice in prescribing (2021): sufficient evidence or experience, prescriber responsibility for monitoring, reasons recorded, family informed. Unlicensed medicines need documentation that no licensed product meets the need; MHRA guidance note 14 covers imports. Section 10 compounding needs a worksheet, a stability reference or default expiry, and pharmacist release; outsourced batches need an MHRA specials licence.

**Netherlands.** Geneesmiddelenwet article 68: off-label only within a professional protocol, otherwise after consulting the pharmacist and informing the patient (WGBO, recorded). Preparation follows KNMP/LNA procedures; an unmarketed product needs an artsenverklaring and IGJ notification.

**Germany.** GKV reimbursement follows the Bundessozialgericht criteria (serious disease, no alternative, justified prospect of benefit) via Einzelfallantrag or section 35c SGB V; written Aufklärung. Rezepturen: Apothekenbetriebsordnung section 7, Plausibilitätsprüfung, NRF formula where available; ataluren only as Einzelimport, section 73(3) AMG.

**US.** Off-label prescribing is medical practice; paperwork is P&T non-formulary approval and payer prior authorisation. Compounding follows USP 795 and section 503A: patient-specific prescription, beyond-use dates from published stability, no copying a marketed product. Ataluren needs a single-patient IND (Form 3926), IRB approval and PTC's agreement to supply.

## Three refusals without written justification

1. **Dasatinib while the child takes a PPI or H2 blocker, or without an antiemetic plan for chemotherapy days.** Absorption falls 43 to 61% and the QT stack has no paediatric precedent. Required: acid suppressant replaced by a timed antacid, a non-QT antiemetic named, baseline ECG.
2. **Sirolimus or everolimus started within a month of IV vincristine, vinorelbine, actinomycin D or ifosfamide without a stated purpose, target trough and stop rule.** A new azole for febrile neutropenia moves the trough several fold overnight.
3. **Ataluren, or any unlicensed import, without the L737* codon context sequenced, a stated function for the readthrough product, and a named-patient route with funding.** A drug withdrawn for unconfirmed efficacy is not stocked on a verbal request.

## What the pharmacist would put in front of the team first

1. **Hydrochlorothiazide plus potassium citrate.** Licensed, cheap, child-usable in all four countries, weight-dosed in paediatric guidance, mutually correcting on potassium. Bloods and urine only.
2. **Metformin oral solution.** Licensed liquid in UK and US, a paediatric oncology dose range, no CYP overlap, a stop rule at eGFR 30. Cystatin C before the first bottle.
3. **Hydroxychloroquine suspension.** A 90-day stable compound used daily at this weight in rheumatology. It brings ECG and eye-screening commitments and must precede ondansetron-heavy cycles and dasatinib.
4. **Sirolimus solution over everolimus.** Both giveable liquids; sirolimus has a paediatric dosing base at this weight and costs a tenth as much.
5. **Dasatinib PFOS.** Giveable; acid suppression, QT and growth make it the last oral drug released.
6. **Nicotinamide.** Trivial to give; needs a renal dose ceiling and a platelet check.
7. **Dexrazoxane.** Routine if doxorubicin is in the protocol, otherwise irrelevant.
8. **Ataluren.** Not giveable through any normal channel in September 2026.
