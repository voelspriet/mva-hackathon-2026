# H. Food as intervention: a cook's protocol for nephrocalcinosis and failure to thrive

Written from a paediatric renal and oncology ward. Nothing here replaces the treating team's prescriptions. The diet is one of the few things a family controls three to five times a day, and it can be measured.

## 1. A nephrocalcinosis diet a small child will eat

**Sodium.** Urinary calcium tracks urinary sodium. The paediatric target is under 2 to 3 mEq/kg/day, about 46 to 69 mg sodium per kg: for a 15 kg child roughly 700 to 1,000 mg sodium, or 1.75 to 2.5 g salt ([PMC3426770](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3426770/)). That sits near the UK SACN target of 2 g salt/day at 1 to 3 years, 3 g at 4 to 6 years ([SACN 2003](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a74983de5274a44083b7ef6/SACN_Salt_and_Health_report.pdf)). The salt is not in the salt cellar, it is in bread, cheese, processed meat, stock cubes, crisps and jar sauces. Replace it with acid, fat and browning: lemon, butter, roasted onion and tomato, smoked paprika, cumin, bay, herbs. A tomato sauce reduced 40 minutes and finished with butter and lemon does not read as unsalted. Unsalted home bread with unsalted butter is the biggest single saving.

**Calcium: normal, not restricted.** Restriction raises oxalate absorption and threatens bone; guidance is to curtail excess, not restrict ([PMC3426770](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3426770/); Habbig, Beck, Hoppe, Kidney Int 2011;80:1278-91). Give it inside meals, where it binds gut oxalate: milk in the porridge, yoghurt with the fruit, cheese sauce on the pasta.

**Oxalate: awareness, not a low-oxalate diet,** unless hyperoxaluria is documented; restriction is advised only for stone formers with proven hyperoxaluria ([PMC3426770](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3426770/)). Demote five foods small children eat a lot of: cooked spinach (about 755 mg oxalate per half cup), rhubarb (541 mg), beetroot, almond butter, cocoa (about 117 mg/100 g) ([UCI table](https://ucikidneystonecenter.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Oxalate-Content-of-Foods.pdf)). Swap kale, cabbage, courgette, broccoli for spinach; sunflower seed butter for almond; vanilla desserts for cocoa. If cocoa is the only route by which milk gets drunk, keep both.

**Fluid.** At least maintenance, higher (1.5 to 2 L/m²/day) in high-risk categories ([PMC3426770](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3426770/)). Where a child will not drink to order, fluid arrives as food: chilled fruit soups, jelly from diluted juice, milk-and-fruit lollies, and a bedtime drink, since overnight urine is most concentrated.

**Citrate.** Lemon juice holds 1.44 g citric acid per ounce, lime 1.38, while commercial lemonades run 0.03 to 0.22 g/oz ([Penniston, J Endourol 2008, PMID 18290732](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18290732/)). So it has to be real juice. Palatable and energy-dense at once: lemon posset, lemon curd made with butter and yolk, lime in coconut rice pudding, lemon butter over pasta or white fish. This supports prescribed potassium citrate rather than replacing it, and makes the drug less of a taste outlier.

## 2. Energy and protein density without sodium or oxalate

Fortifiers carrying no sodium and no oxalate load: unsalted butter, cream, rapeseed and olive oil, full-fat milk and yoghurt, egg yolk, skimmed milk powder (2 to 4 tsp into 200 mL milk adds 4 to 8 g protein), mascarpone, ground sunflower seeds, glucose polymer where prescribed. All bland enough to hide.

Dishes that work: risotto on unsalted stock finished with butter and mascarpone; scrambled egg with cream; root vegetable and chicken soup blended with oil and drunk from a cup; pancakes with lemon and sugar; macaroni in a béchamel where cheese supplies flavour, not volume. Small plates, refilled.

**Food-first versus supplements.** NICE NG75 sets a food-first approach for older infants and children ([NG75](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng75/chapter/recommendations)). The supplement evidence: a meta-analysis of 10 RCTs, 1,116 children, 585 on oral nutritional supplements for a mean of 116 days, weight difference 0.4 kg, height 0.3 cm, compliance 98%, plus a pooled 39% reduction in upper respiratory infection across three studies ([Cawood et al., J Child Health Care 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/13674935231185181)). The effects are real, small, and untested in a child on chemotherapy with a renal diagnosis. Order: food first, supplements when a 3-day diary shows a documented energy gap, enteral feeding if weight velocity stays flat under both.

## 3. Food and drug rules, one line each

| Drug | Interaction | Practical rule |
|---|---|---|
| Dasatinib | Grapefruit and Seville orange inhibit CYP3A4 and raise exposure; antacids lower absorption, H2 blockers and PPIs lower it further ([label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/021986s028lbl.pdf)) | No grapefruit, pomelo or Seville marmalade. Antacid 2 hours before or after. Ask the team before any acid suppressant. Sweet orange, lemon and lime are fine |
| Sirolimus | Grapefruit juice inhibits CYP3A4-mediated metabolism; must not be taken with it or used to dilute it ([Rapamune label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2010/021110s058lbl.pdf)) | Same fruit ban. Keep it consistently with or without food as the team sets, never switching |
| Metformin | GI intolerance; label use is divided doses with meals | Give with the largest meal, not before it. If it blunts appetite, that competes with the growth plan; record it in the diary |
| Hydroxychloroquine | Administer with food or milk to cut nausea and vomiting ([label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2022/214581s000lbl.pdf)) | Give with the milk part of a meal, which doubles as the calcium-with-meal slot |
| Potassium citrate | Sour, refused neat | Cold, thick, fatty carrier (yoghurt, posset), never a favourite food, so refusal costs no food |
| Hydrochlorothiazide | Potassium loss, volume depletion poorly tolerated in a small child | Keep fluid steady; keep low-oxalate potassium foods in (banana, potato, milk, apricot); team checks potassium |
| Fortified foods, vitamin D, calcium | Supplementation regarded as unsafe with hypercalciuria and urolithiasis; high-dose intermittent vitamin D has caused nephrocalcinosis (PMID 2386055; PMC7551195) | Total vitamin D and calcium across supplements, growing-up milks, cereals and spreads. None over the counter without nephrology |

## 4. Can food reach NAD+ doses from the SIRT2/BubR1 work? The arithmetic

NMN raised BubR1 in mice at 500 mg/kg/day, injected, for 7 days ([North et al., EMBO J 2014, PMC4194088](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4194088/)). Body-surface scaling (mouse Km 3, human Km 37) gives about 40 mg/kg/day, roughly 800 mg/day for a 20 kg child ([Nair and Jacob, PMC4804402](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4804402/)).

Cow milk holds about 12 µmol/L NAD+ precursor vitamins, 60% nicotinamide, 40% nicotinamide riboside ([Trammell, J Nutr 2016;146:957-63](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022316623005953)): about 4.8 µmol NR/L, at 255 g/mol about 1.2 mg per litre. Half a litre gives 0.6 mg. Reaching 800 mg from milk NR would take roughly 650 to 700 litres daily.

Niacin and tryptophan do not close it. RDA is 6 mg NE/day at 1 to 3 years, 8 mg at 4 to 8, with 60 mg tryptophan counting as 1 mg NE ([NIH ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Niacin-HealthProfessional/)). A 20 kg child eating 22 g protein takes in about 240 mg tryptophan, about 4 mg NE; an ordinary fortified diet supplies perhaps 15 to 25 mg NE. Diet secures niacin sufficiency and cannot approach a pharmacological NAD+ dose. A precursor test is a drug question, not a menu question.

## 5. Feeding as the daily battleground, and a measurable plan

The dossier lists "Nutritional support" as current treatment for short stature alongside growth hormone (M6), and family accounts describe "on-going health issues that need constant care and attention" (S2). Families in comparable cancer-predisposition syndromes weight quality of life above survival and monitor continuously ("It's a constant thought", F1); surveillance burden is accepted when it returns a sense of control (PMID 42303221, PMID 20658357). Feeding is that burden in repeating form, and nobody schedules it.

A monitored feeding plan, all parent-collected:

- **Weight velocity.** Same scale, same time of day, undressed. Weekly for 8 weeks, then fortnightly, plotted on UK-WHO charts as change in weight-for-age z-score, not single points (NICE NG75).
- **MUAC.** Monthly, z-score tape. Tracks lean tissue rather than hydration, which matters with a thiazide and a fluid target running together.
- **Height velocity.** 3-monthly, timed to the existing cancer surveillance visits so no extra trip is created.
- **3-day food diary** (2 weekdays, 1 weekend day) at baseline, week 6, week 12: types, amounts, mealtime problems, refusals, and the clock time of each drug against each meal. NG75 recommends this diary to inform management and assess progress.
- **Fluid and urine record** over the same 3 days, alongside the urine calcium-to-creatinine ratio and renal ultrasound already scheduled (PMC5976810).

Run it for 8 to 12 weeks before any new drug, so a later agent is added to a stable baseline. A drug hypothesis that cannot shift weight velocity or MUAC over 12 weeks has not shifted what the family asked about.
