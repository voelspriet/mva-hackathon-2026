# Directions 1, 2 and 3: allele-directed, expression-directed, segregation-directed

Written without reading archive/panel/. Scope: market-approved medicines, jurisdiction stated. Every claim is tagged human, animal, cell or inference.

## 0. The stop-codon context, determined from the reference sequence

Retrieved ENST00000287598.11 CDS from the Ensembl REST sequence endpoint (https://rest.ensembl.org/sequence/id/ENST00000287598?type=cds). Length 3153 nt, 1050 codons plus stop, consistent with the MANE transcript.

Codons 735 to 740 read CCA GAG **TTA** AGT GCC TCT. Codon 737 is therefore **TTA (Leu)**, and c.2210 is the middle base of that codon. c.2210T>G converts TTA to **TGA**, so p.Leu737Ter is a **UGA** premature termination codon. The tetranucleotide context is **UGA A**: the +4 nucleotide is the A that opens codon 738 (AGT). The -1 nucleotide is the G that closes codon 736 (GAG). Local sequence c.2207 to c.2215 is GGAGTGAAGT.

What this predicts. UGA is the leakiest of the three stops and is the codon on which ataluren and the aminoglycosides act best (Welch 2007, PMID 17450125; Manuvakhova 2000, PMID 10917599). The flanks are less favourable. Floquet 2012 (PMID 22479203) found the base immediately 5' of the stop to be the major determinant of gentamicin response, with U at -1 giving the strongest response; here -1 is G. The +4 A is mid-ranking, below C. Net expectation, **inference**: a readthrough-competent codon in an average-to-poor sequence context, so a low single-digit percentage of readthrough at best.

What a readthrough product would be. UGA is misread by near-cognate tRNA-Trp, tRNA-Cys and tRNA-Arg, so the product is full-length BubR1 carrying p.Leu737Trp, Cys or Arg. Residue 737 lies between the KARD (665 to 682) and the pseudokinase domain (766 to 1050), in a linker with no annotated motif in UniProt O60566. **Inference**: substitution there is more likely tolerated than a substitution inside the kinase fold, but this has never been tested.

## Ranked ideas

**1. Stabilise the nonsense transcript on purpose and accept the truncated protein.** Step: nonsense-mediated decay, the only reason the c.2210T>G allele contributes nothing. Drugs: digoxin or digitoxin, approved US and EU for heart failure and atrial fibrillation. Cardiac glycosides were the top hits in an unbiased NMD screen and act through Na/K-ATPase inhibition and a rise in intracellular calcium (Nickless 2014, PMID 25064126; cell). The rationale is not readthrough. Suijkerbuijk 2012 (PMID 22698286; cell) showed that BUBR1 truncated at 731, lacking only the kinase domain, restores chromosome alignment in BUBR1-depleted cells, and that BUBR1 catalysis is dispensable in vertebrates. A stabilised 736-residue product retains TPR, GLEBS, KEN boxes, ABBA and KARD. Failure mode: the truncate could act as a dominant negative on MCC assembly, and digoxin has a narrow paediatric therapeutic index. Measurement: allele-specific ddPCR on the c.2210 allele in patient fibroblasts plus or minus drug, with anti-BubR1 immunoblot for a band near 82 kDa.

**2. Amlexanox, which does both jobs in one molecule.** Step: simultaneous NMD inhibition (UPF1 phosphorylation) and PTC readthrough. Approved: Japan (Solfa, oral, allergic rhinitis and asthma); US approval was topical oral paste for aphthous ulcers (Aphthasol), so systemic use outside Japan is off-label. Evidence: 25 micromolar restored full-length dystrophin, p53 and CFTR from PTC alleles in patient-derived human cells (Gonzalez-Hilarion 2012, PMID 22938201; cell), with UGA alleles among the responders (Bortolussi 2023, PMID 37513945; cell). Failure mode: micromolar in vitro potency has not been shown to be reachable in plasma at approved doses, and combined NMD inhibition plus readthrough perturbs hundreds of physiological NMD substrates. Measurement: dose-response ddPCR of the c.2210 allele fraction in patient lymphoblastoid cells.

**3. Ataluren, targeted at a UGA allele, in the one jurisdiction where it is still authorised.** Step: readthrough of UGA specifically. Approved: UK (MHRA, nmDMD), after the European Commission declined renewal of the EU conditional authorisation on 28 March 2025 (https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/translarna). Evidence: human, in a different gene, and contested. The codon match is exact: ataluren is most active on UGA, and the L737 allele is UGA. Failure mode: EMA judged efficacy unconfirmed in DMD over a decade of data, so prior probability of a measurable effect here is low; the -1 G context predicts poor response. Measurement: full-length BubR1 by quantitative immunoblot in patient fibroblasts against a heterozygous parent line, powered to detect a 10 percent change.

**4. Caffeine citrate as a low-cost SMG1 brake.** Step: SMG1, the PIKK that phosphorylates UPF1 and licenses NMD, is caffeine-inhibited like ATM and ATR. Drug: caffeine citrate (Cafcit), approved US and EU for apnoea of prematurity, with an established paediatric safety record and a child born at 32 weeks already inside its label population. Evidence: inference from PIKK pharmacology; no NMD or BUB1B data. Failure mode: caffeine inhibits PIKKs only in the high micromolar to millimolar range, above plasma levels at licensed doses, so the effect is likely nil. Measurement: same allele-specific ddPCR readout as idea 1, run as a negative-control arm rather than a therapeutic arm.

**5. Heat-shock co-induction to hold up the p.Asn1002Lys protein.** Step: kinase-domain missense BubR1 is degraded faster in an HSP90-dependent, proteasome-mediated way, with I909T and R727C showing roughly two-fold increased turnover and 5 to 10 fold lower steady-state levels (Suijkerbuijk 2010, PMID 20516114; human patient cells). Drugs: arimoclomol (Miplyffa, FDA 20 September 2024, Niemann-Pick C, from age 2), which amplifies HSF1 and TFEB output and raises HSP70; and geranylgeranylacetone/teprenone, approved in Japan as a gastric mucosal protectant and a documented HSP70 inducer. Failure mode: raising chaperone capacity may extend the half-life of a protein that is misfolded but inactive, and HSF1 activation is a general pro-survival programme in a cancer-predisposed child. Measurement: cycloheximide-chase half-life of BubR1 in patient fibroblasts, plus or minus drug.

**6. Chemical chaperone rather than chaperone induction.** Step: non-selective stabilisation of a destabilised fold. Drug: sodium phenylbutyrate (Buphenyl, Pheburane; approved US and EU for urea cycle disorders, paediatric dosing established). Evidence: inference; no BubR1 data. Failure mode: 4-PBA is also a weak HDAC inhibitor at therapeutic concentrations, which pushes the FOXM1 arm in idea 8 in the wrong direction, so the two mechanisms may cancel. Measurement: BubR1 steady-state immunoblot and BUB1B transcript by RT-qPCR in the same lysate, to separate a protein-stability gain from a transcript loss.

**7. Feed the NAD+ pool with a prescription drug, not a supplement.** Step: SIRT2 deacetylates BubR1 at K668, blocking CBP-primed ubiquitination and APC/C-driven degradation; SIRT2 transgene raised BubR1H/H median lifespan by 58 percent (North 2014, PMID 24825348; animal). SIRT2 is NAD+-dependent. Drug: acipimox (Olbetam), a nicotinic acid analogue approved in several EU states for hyperlipidaemia, with human data showing raised muscle NAD+ and mitochondrial respiration at 250 mg three times daily for two weeks (van de Weijer 2015, PMID 25352640; human). Failure mode: the only BubR1-raising NAD+ data are NMN in mouse testis, never in BubR1 mutants; flushing limits paediatric tolerability. Measurement: whole-blood NAD+ by LC-MS with BubR1 immunoblot in PBMCs, paired baseline and on-treatment.

**8. Avoid list, expression arm: three approved classes predicted to lower BubR1.** (a) High-dose nicotinamide, which inhibits sirtuins by base exchange at concentrations near physiological (Avalos 2005, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2005.03.024; cell) and would oppose the SIRT2 axis, so the NAD+ precursor chosen must be nicotinic acid or acipimox, not nicotinamide. (b) HDAC inhibitors, including valproate, vorinostat and romidepsin, which suppress FOXM1 and its downstream G2/M programme (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8317537/; cell); BUB1B is a canonical FOXM1 target, so the BubR1 consequence is **inference**. (c) HSP90 inhibitors, which would accelerate exactly the turnover measured for kinase-domain BubR1 mutants. Measurement: BubR1 immunoblot before and after any such exposure.

**9. Ultra-low-dose paclitaxel, with the two published directions stated as opposite.** Step: spindle microtubule plus-end assembly rate, upstream of lagging chromosomes. Direction A: 0.2 nM taxol restored assembly rates and **reduced** lagging chromosomes and CIN in colorectal lines (Ertych 2014, PMID 24976383; cell). Direction B: partial BubR1 reduction made cells "remarkably more sensitive to low doses of taxol", which **increased** the number and severity of segregation errors, while Mad2 reduction did not sensitise (Janssen 2009, PMID 19855003; cell). Direction B was measured in the patient's genotype class, direction A was not. Failure mode: the sensitising result is the more relevant one, and paclitaxel has no safe subtherapeutic label. Measurement: anaphase lagging-chromosome frequency in patient fibroblasts across 0.1 to 2 nM, against a parental heterozygous line.

**10. Avoid list, segregation arm: aneugens that are easy to prescribe by accident.** Colchicine (approved US and EU, gout, familial Mediterranean fever, pericarditis) is a reference aneugen in the human lymphocyte micronucleus assay, producing non-disjunction, metaphase arrest and large centromere-positive micronuclei (Vanhauwaert 1998, PMID 9412992; human cells). Benzimidazole anthelmintics, albendazole and mebendazole, bind the same colchicine site. In a child whose baseline defect is kinetochore-microtubule attachment error, these add insult at the identical step. Failure mode of the avoidance itself: none of this is measured in MVA cells, so the recommendation is class-based **inference**. Measurement: cytokinesis-block micronucleus assay on patient lymphocytes with and without each agent, before any elective use.

## Ideas rejected during this round and why

- **Systemic gentamicin or amikacin pulses.** The stop codon is UGA, which favours aminoglycoside readthrough, but the -1 base is G, the weakest class in Floquet 2012 (PMID 22479203). Weighed against ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity in a child who already has nephrocalcinosis, the expected benefit does not justify the renal risk. Kept only as an ex vivo comparator.
- **Bortezomib, carfilzomib or ixazomib to block BubR1 degradation.** Proteasome inhibition would raise BubR1 and would also selectively stress aneuploid and whole-genome-doubled cells (Quinton 2021, PMID 33505027; cell), but it blocks mitotic exit through APC/C substrate stabilisation, which is the wrong intervention in a checkpoint-weak child.
- **17-AAG and other HSP90 inhibitors.** Direction is wrong for the missense allele: the mutant is HSP90-dependent, so inhibition destabilises the only BubR1 the child makes.
- **NMN and nicotinamide riboside.** Supplements, not medicines, in the US and EU, so out of scope; also never given to BubR1 mutant mice.
- **Mps1/TTK and KIF18A inhibitors.** No approved agent in either class, and Mps1 inhibition is directionally harmful in a SAC-weak background.
- **Arsenic trioxide as a SUMO-pathway lever.** Approved, but its SUMO effect is a targeted degradation mechanism, not a stabilisation one, and BubR1 K250 SUMOylation is required for kinetochore localisation.
