# J. Lateral ideas for Track 2

Written without reading the panel files. I am a designer, not a biologist. Everything below is a hypothesis to be checked by someone who can, and several will not survive that check. Facts used: BUB1B compound heterozygous (L737* in exon 17, N1002K in the kinase domain, phase unproven), aneuploidy not detected above about 10% of cells in the sequenced sample, burden in muscle, growth and kidney, rhabdomyosarcoma treated, parents are obligate carriers with the PCS trait, about 40 known patients worldwide, MVA Society target GBP 325k for 2026.

## Ideas

**1. Inversion: make the aneuploid cell the meter, not the target.** Every candidate drug in this space fails for the same reason: no endpoint that moves inside a year. The buccal micronucleus cytome assay reads chromosome missegregation from cells collected with a small toothbrush, has published paediatric reference ranges, and needs no clinic visit ([Thomas 2009, Nat Protoc](https://www.nature.com/articles/nprot.2009.53); [MAPEC_LIFE child cohort](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1438463918301573)). The family swabs weekly and posts slides; a blinded reader scores them. This turns a syndrome with no biomarker into one with a cheap, repeatable, family-held one.

**2. Inversion: the missegregating cell as an immune target rather than a cell to rescue.** Aneuploid senescent cells activate NF-kB and are cleared by NK cells ([Wang 2021, PMC8339690](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8339690/)). If clearance is rate-limiting, the intervention is not fixing mitosis but raising surveillance of the cells that already failed it. Ask which approved drugs raise NK number or cytotoxicity at doses tolerated in children. The failure mode is obvious and must be stated: raising immune clearance in a child whose muscle and fat carry the p16-high load may cost tissue that a growing child cannot spare.

**3. Place: the parents are the experiment.** Both parents carry one BUB1B allele and, by the Kajii data, 3.5 to 41.7 percent premature chromatid separation in lymphocytes. That is a quantifiable cellular phenotype in a consenting adult with an ordinary safety profile. Any drug proposed for the child can first be tested on parental lymphocytes ex vivo, then, for the safest candidates, in a parent in vivo with PCS frequency as readout. The child is not the first human dosed. Two adults per family, about 80 carriers across the society.

**4. Place: treat the tissue, not the child.** Nephrocalcinosis is a kidney-lumen problem driven by urinary calcium concentration and crystal inhibition. Amiloride is approved, generic, and hypocalciuric by a mechanism separate from thiazides, which are already in the standard set. As an add-on it acts where the deposit forms rather than systemically. Check: paediatric dosing exists, potassium must be watched, and the combination with citrate has been used in other hypercalciuric conditions. Evidence tier: human, but in other stone diseases, not in MVA.

**5. Time: dose when cells divide.** A drug that acts on mitosis is inert in tissue that is not dividing. Mitotic index in gut crypt and marrow is under circadian control, and growth in children is not linear: knemometry shows mini growth spurts every 30 to 55 days ([Hermanussen](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00496409)). A mitosis-facing agent given continuously is mostly exposure without target. Given in the spurt window, or at the daily peak, the same total dose buys more effect and less off-target time. Requires knowing the child's own spurt rhythm first, which is idea 6.

**6. Time: use short-term growth as the fast endpoint.** Height measured yearly cannot support an N-of-1 crossover. Lower-leg length by knemometry resolves 0.09 to 0.16 mm and produces a weekly signal ([Urlacher 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26854116/)). A crossover of eight weeks on, eight weeks off becomes interpretable. Knemometers are inexpensive and have been used in field settings by non-clinicians. This is a measurement idea, not a treatment idea, and it is the one that determines whether any growth-facing treatment idea can ever be judged.

**7. Scale: 40 patients, one protocol, one app.** The Dog Aging Project runs a randomised rapamycin trial through owners who collect the data at home ([TRIAD](https://vet.tufts.edu/clinical-trials/dog-aging-project-test-rapamycin-aging-dogs-triad)). MVA has about 40 patients on four continents and one charity that already talks to most of them. An aggregated N-of-1 design with a shared registration, one primary outcome, and one app turns 40 isolated off-label decisions into a dataset. The barrier is not statistics; it is that each family currently prescribes alone and nothing is recorded in a common format.

**8. Scale: the carrier cohort nobody has counted.** About 80 parents, plus grandparents and siblings, carry one allele and decades of natural history. Mouse work links monoallelic BubR1 to sarcopenia via mTORC1 hyperactivity ([Sieben 2020, PMID 31738183](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31738183/)). A questionnaire plus grip strength plus a renal ultrasound in carriers costs little and tests whether the human carrier state has a measurable phenotype. If it does, carriers become both a natural history cohort and a trial population that is easier to enrol than children.

**9. Borrowing from radiation protection.** Amifostine is approved and its metabolite WR-1065 reduces radiation-induced and mitomycin-induced micronuclei, including in Fanconi anaemia lymphocytes ([PMC6245386](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6245386/); [PMC2629584](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2629584/)). Its toxicity profile makes chronic paediatric use unlikely. The transfer is the assay, not the drug: radiation biology has spent fifty years building quantitative chromosome-damage endpoints and knows what a real reduction looks like. Borrow their scoring conventions and their idea of a positive control for the MVA readout.

**10. Borrowing from spaceflight medicine.** Astronauts present the same cluster: chromosome aberrations, muscle loss, calcium leaving bone and appearing in urine as stones. The countermeasure that works there is not a drug; it is loaded resistance exercise on a schedule, with pharmacology as support ([AJP Cell 2026 review](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/ajpcell.00649.2025)). For this child, a supervised loading protocol should be the comparator any muscle-facing drug has to beat, and the calcium logic runs the same way: immobilisation raises urinary calcium, so muscle work and kidney protection are one intervention.

**11. Constraint removal: pick the drug for its side effect.** If only approved medicines count, invert the label. Mirtazapine is approved as an antidepressant and its most reported adverse effects are appetite increase and weight gain; it is used off-label for appetite in paediatric oncology and cystic fibrosis. For failure to thrive that is the wanted effect, with a safety record in children that appetite stimulants like megestrol do not have. Check: sedation, and whether any hormonal appetite route is contraindicated in a cancer-predisposition syndrome.

**12. Constraint removal: read the contraindication list backwards.** Rather than searching for drugs that raise BubR1, search approved labels for agents documented to lower it or to worsen missegregation, and write the avoid-list. For a child facing surgery, infections and future chemotherapy, a one-page list of drugs the treating team should not reach for may change outcomes sooner than any candidate treatment, and it needs no trial. It is also the deliverable a hospital pharmacy can act on this month.

**13. Measurement-first: put the titration in the family's hands.** Thiazide and citrate are already standard, but dose is set at clinic visits months apart while urinary calcium moves with diet, illness and immobility. A home urine calcium-to-creatinine strip photographed into an app gives a weekly curve. The family sees the effect of a bad week; the nephrologist titrates on data instead of on a single spot sample. Small, cheap, and it makes the existing treatment work better without adding a drug.

**14. Measurement-first: grip strength and sit-to-stand.** A GBP 30 dynamometer and a timed five-rise test give a muscle endpoint with paediatric norms that a parent can run monthly at home. Muscle is one of the two earliest tissues affected in the BubR1 mouse. Families in surveillance-heavy syndromes report that appointment-linked worry is cyclical; a stable home number between scans is an outcome they can hold. Any trial in this disease needs a muscle endpoint that does not require a gait lab.

**15. Economics: what GBP 325k buys in 2026.** One line panel from the child and both parents, plus a screen of a few hundred already-approved compounds with a micronucleus or PCS readout, is within a single year's fundraising at CRO rates and produces a ranked list rather than an argument. Cures Within Reach funds repurposing proofs of concept under USD 250k and in three years ([criteria](https://www.cureswithinreach.org/programs/frequently-asked-questions-faqs/)). Sizing the ask to that envelope changes who can say yes.

**16. Incentive: change who pays before choosing what to test.** An off-patent drug given a paediatric formulation can take the EU Paediatric Use Marketing Authorisation route, which carries eight years of data protection, and orphan designation waives agency fees, which matters when the sponsor is a charity ([EMA PUMA](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/marketing-authorisation/paediatric-medicines-marketing-authorisation/paediatric-use-marketing-authorisations); [PMC4237943](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4237943/)). Choosing a candidate that has a formulation gap makes a commercial sponsor possible. Choosing one already available as a paediatric liquid does not, but reaches this child faster. The two goals conflict and the society should decide which it is buying.

## Three deliberately wrong-headed ideas

**A. Continuous senolysis in a growing child.** Clearing p16-high cells looks right from the mouse data, but growth plates and satellite cells are the same compartment, and permanently removing cells from a child whose complaint is that he is not growing risks paying for a cellular endpoint with height.

**B. Chronic aminoglycoside for stop-codon readthrough.** The readthrough logic is sound for L737*, but the drug class is nephrotoxic and this child already has nephrocalcinosis, so the organ you damage is the organ under treatment.

**C. Low-dose taxane to stabilise segregation.** The published direction of effect is inconsistent, and giving a mitotic poison to a checkpoint-weak child with a treated rhabdomyosarcoma bets the cancer risk on a result that has not been shown to hold in his cell type.

## Three to hand to a scientist first

1. **The buccal micronucleus assay as a family-collected primary endpoint.** Ask: is the assay sensitive enough to detect a treatment effect in MVA, what is this child's baseline against paediatric norms, and does posted-slide logistics preserve scorability.
2. **Amiloride as an add-on for nephrocalcinosis.** Ask: does the hypocalciuric effect hold in paediatric nephrocalcinosis, is it additive to thiazide plus citrate, and what is the potassium risk in a child with low muscle mass.
3. **Parental lymphocytes as the ex vivo screening tissue.** Ask: does PCS frequency in carrier cells respond measurably to any approved compound, and is that response predictive of anything in the child's own cells.
