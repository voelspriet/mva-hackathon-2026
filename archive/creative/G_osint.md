# G. Open-source intelligence: what has already been tried, funded, patented or proposed for MVA and BUB1B

Scope: disease level only. No attempt was made to identify, locate or profile any MVA patient or family, nothing is cited except disease-level facts, and no one was contacted. Public, published material only, accessed 5 September 2026.

Evidence tiers: **T1** human clinical; **T2** animal in vivo; **T3** human cells or non-mammalian model; **T4** grant text, patent or organisational statement, that is, an intention rather than a result.

---

## 1. What the MVA Society is actually funding, and what that removes from the candidate list

The Society's project page names four active strands, none with a named PI or an amount:

1. **Drug repurposing, London lead, with collaborators in Scotland and Spain, "testing existing medicines using a worm model of MVA"** (mvasociety.org/our-research-projects). This is the most important correction to the existing dossier: the London project is **not** a mammalian or patient-cell screen but a whole-organism *C. elegans* phenotypic screen.
2. **Single cell RNA profiling of several MVA patients, Spain**, to build genetic maps and identify targets.
3. **Supplements and cell health, USA**, testing whether supplements stabilise human MVA cells, explicitly "following promising mouse study results".
4. **Fibroblast growth from MVA family skin biopsies, a London laboratory**, early stage.

The speaker list for the inaugural **MVA Research Conference, Great Ormond Street Hospital, 17 September 2026** (selectscience.net, 2026) resolves strands 1 and 3: van Deursen, North, Malumbres, Brown, Foulkes, Putoux, McCarthy, Matsuura, Akutsu, Leitch, Dixon. Agenda: disease modelling, drug repurposing, therapeutic strategies, patient registries, clinical management, a **UK MVA National Audit**, international clinical experience.

- **Andre Brown** heads Behavioural Phenomics at the **MRC Laboratory of Medical Sciences, London** (lms.mrc.ac.uk/research/behavioural-phenomics), building *C. elegans* "avatars" of rare diseases and running approved-drug and drug-combination screens at about 10,000 behaviour experiments a day. **T4.** Their cited precedents are epalrestat (worm model to phase III in five years, about $5m) and ravicti. **Harry Leitch**, also MRC LMS, is the plausible worm-to-fibroblast route, matching strand 4.
- **Brian J. North** is at **Creighton University** (creighton.edu directory) and first author of *SIRT2 induces the checkpoint kinase BubR1 to increase lifespan* (EMBO J 2014, PMC4194088), which is the "promising mouse study" behind the USA supplements strand: acetylation of BubR1 **K668** blocks its ubiquitylation and degradation, SIRT2 deacetylates K668 in an NAD+ dependent way, and **NMN 500 mg/kg/day intraperitoneally for 7 days raised NAD+ and restored BubR1 protein in aged mouse tissue**; SIRT2 transgene crossed onto BubR1^H/H gave **+58% median lifespan overall, +123% in males, no effect in females**. **T2.** The paper names MVA but makes no therapeutic recommendation.

**What this adds:** the NAD+ or SIRT2 direction is an active charity-funded programme with a named mechanism (K668), a molecule class, a mouse dose and a sex-specific caveat no write-up should omit. **What it removes:** "NAD+ precursor to raise BubR1" is not a novel idea. The differentiating contribution is the translation gap: which precursor has paediatric human data, at what dose, with what endpoint.

The Society is **charity 1210996, England and Wales, a CIO from 19 November 2024**, reporting up to date; its full-print register page returns 403 to automated fetching and the API needs a subscription key, so no filed accounts were read. Its own site gives **GBP 250k raised in 2025, GBP 325k target for 2026**. No grant agreement or per-project amount is public, and **no funded PhD studentship appears on any public page**, so treat that strand as unconfirmed.

## 2. Grant databases

**NIH RePORTER** (API v2, 5 Sep 2026). "Mosaic variegated aneuploidy" returns 16 records that are all one award: **R01CA096985, "BubR1 in Cancer and Aging", Mayo Clinic Rochester**, PIs van Deursen then Bram then Baker, 2007 to 2022, about USD 220k to 400k a year. "BUB1B" returns 15 records, all oncology or embryo biology (Paddison R01CA295090 at Fred Hutch; Burnstein; Gottesman; Goorha; Brooks). Aneuploidy plus chromosome therapy plus mosaic, 2020 to 2026: **zero**. **There is no NIH-funded MVA therapy programme**; the whole NIH BubR1 investment is one Mayo ageing and cancer grant that ended in 2022.

**UKRI Gateway to Research** and **CORDIS** returned nothing indexable for BUB1B, BubR1 or mosaic variegated aneuploidy through search. Both are listed below as unresolved rather than negative.

## 3. Patents

- **WO2025101836A1**, Dartmouth College (Godek, Deng, Compton), priority 10 Nov 2023. Claims methods to **induce** aneuploidy and chromosomal instability in human and primate pluripotent stem cells, building mixed populations that "mimic that of a particular disease condition, optionally mosaic variegated aneuploidy (MVA)" at about 25% aneuploid cells, for drug studies. **T4.** A modelling platform, not a treatment, but the only patent found naming MVA, and an isogenic screening substrate a Track 2 proposal could cite as a validation step.
- **Senolytic patent family**, Mayo with Buck Institute, Johns Hopkins and Unity Biotechnology, inventors including van Deursen: WO2015116740A1, EP3099380A1, WO2017008060A1, plus US10328073, US10413542, US10517866. **T4.** Named indications are atherosclerosis, IPF, COPD, osteoarthritis and eye and skin disease. **No claim names MVA or BUB1B**, even though BubR1^H/H is the founding model of the field. A freedom-to-operate observation, not a scientific one.
- **No patent was found claiming readthrough or NMD inhibition for BUB1B.** The readthrough patent estate found (for example US9358246B2, and the BMPR2/GJ103 family) names other genes. "BUB1B-PAK6 readthrough" hits in search are a naturally occurring readthrough transcript, an unrelated meaning.

## 4. Orphan drug designations

No FDA or EMA orphan designation for MVA was found, but openFDA has no orphan drug endpoint at the path tried and EMA's list sits behind IRIS, so this is a **search failure, not a confirmed absence** (the prior is against one existing, given zero registered trials). One adjacent precedent is directly usable: **ChromaDex received FDA Orphan Drug and Rare Pediatric Disease designations for nicotinamide riboside chloride in ataxia telangiectasia on 7 June 2024**, on a phase II in 24 AT patients of whom 17 were **under 18**, with fourfold whole-blood NAD+ elevation, improved ataxia scores, eye movement and immunoglobulins, and stable liver and kidney biomarkers (Veenhuis, Mov Disord 2021; Presterud, Mov Disord 2024). **T1.** A Werner syndrome NR study followed in 2025.

**What this adds:** the dossier's "no paediatric NAD+ data" cell is now filled. There is paediatric human NR dosing, a safety readout, an efficacy signal in another chromosomal instability and premature ageing disorder, and a copyable regulatory path. With the North K668 mechanism and the Creighton project behind it, NAD+ precursor supplementation is the best evidenced candidate the panel appears to have underweighted.

## 5. Preprints and recent primary literature the review round can have missed

- **AICAR-mediated selection of karyotypically normal cells in Pallister-Killian syndrome patient fibroblasts.** Salzano, Kaur, Jacko, McEldrew, Raible, Krantz, Izumi (Children's Hospital of Philadelphia), bioRxiv 10.1101/2022.01.27.478025 v2, 28 Jan 2022, still unpublished. AICAR **selectively eliminated isochromosome 12p cells in a time and dose dependent manner**, leaving euploid cells, framed as "chromosome therapy" that "may establish the euploid state in PKS and a broader spectrum of human mosaic disorders". **T3.** The closest precedent for drug-based cell selection in a **human mosaic aneuploidy patient's own cells**, extending Tang 2011 from mouse MEFs to patient fibroblasts. AICAR is not approved; **metformin, the approved AMPK-activating stand-in, was not tested**, which is the experiment a Track 2 submission can name.
- **Proteostasis failure and mitochondrial dysfunction contribute to chromosomal instability-induced microcephaly.** Gonzalez-Blanco, Acuna-Higaki, Boettger, Milan (IRB Barcelona), bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.03.18.643858, now Nat Commun 2026, doi 10.1038/s41467-026-70521-0, PMID 41820377. A *Drosophila* SAC-depletion model of MVA microcephaly: complex aneuploidy destroys neural stem cell stemness through proteostasis failure and mitochondrial dysfunction, and **autophagy activation, TOR depletion, ROS scavengers, mitochondrial chaperones and protein folding factors each dampened the damage**. **T3.** All interventions were genetic, but the directions map onto mTOR inhibitors, autophagy inducers and antioxidants. Note the conflict: Santaguida 2015 has aneuploid cells dying when autophagy is blocked (hydroxychloroquine), this paper has boosted autophagy protecting neural stem cells. **Opposite directions in different tissues; do not merge them.**
- **Recessive SMC5 variants, near-tetraploidy and MVA** (Diagnostics 2025, PMID 41374403): a new gene with a milder phenotype. **MVA as a novel feature of Mulibrey nanism with TRIM37 variants** (PMID 41702694, Feb 2026): mosaic aneuploidy in 7 to 36% of metaphases in amniocytes and neonatal fibroblasts. Both widen the differential and the registry denominator.
- **Modeling and targeting general and chromosome-specific aneuploidy in cancer** (PMID 40721295, 2025) is the current synthesis of aneuploidy-selective pharmacology and the right citation for the segregation-directed question.
- Nothing was found on BubR1 stabilisation by approved chaperone or proteasome drugs, on readthrough in BUB1B, or on senolytics given as drugs to BubR1 hypomorphic mice rather than genetic INK-ATTAC clearance.

## 6. Reviews and citing literature

**Nature Reviews Genetics 2024, PMID 39169218, Malumbres and Villarroya-Beltri.** Europe PMC confirms **isOpenAccess: N**, cited 13 times. No open version, author summary or accepted manuscript exists. The 13 citing papers are model, mechanism and case papers (hepatoblastoma chromosome 18 mosaicism PMID 42019868; the TRIM37 paper; the Milan microcephaly paper; chromosome 8 and lineage PMID 41706839; CIN beyond cancer evolution PMID 40441073; the SMC5 family) and **none quotes a treatment section**. Malumbres leads the Cancer Cell Cycle Group at **VHIO Barcelona**, the Spanish single-cell strand; his lab lists MVA genes BUB1B, TRIP13, CENATAC, BUB1, MAD1L1, MAD2L1BP, and names the obstacle plainly: few samples, continuous cellular evolution, a need for liquid biopsies.

**Frattini 2025** (Am J Med Genet A, doi 10.1002/ajmg.a.63901) could not be read; Wiley returned 403 to automated fetching. Not confirmed open access.

## 7. Drugs actually given to MVA patients for the syndrome itself

Almost nothing exists.

- **Growth hormone:** still only the MVA2 (CEP57) siblings with severe short stature and **no response to GH** (PMID 36588761; ESPE 2022 abstract hrp0095p1-129). **T1, n=2, wrong subtype.** No MVA1 GH case was found.
- **Symptomatic drugs only** in MVA1 case reports: valproate and levetiracetam, nasogastric feeding, physiotherapy, three-monthly ultrasound, fundus and haematology checks (Pavone 2022, PMC9616775). Plus the dactinomycin severe reaction (Ochiai 2019, PMID 31081598) and the single HSCT (PMC6500003) already in the dossier.
- **No case report exists of metformin, nicotinamide, nicotinamide riboside, a senolytic, hydroxychloroquine, sirolimus or any repurposed agent given to an MVA patient for the syndrome.** Every mechanism-directed candidate therefore has **zero T1 human evidence in MVA**, which the submission should state rather than imply.
- **Rio Frio and Foulkes, NEJM 2010** (PMID 21190457): homozygous intronic BUB1B c.2386-11A>G, ampullary adenocarcinoma at 34, later colon and gastric primaries, with PCS and MVA in patient cells. An adult-onset MVA1 phenotype exists, which sets how long any surveillance or treatment plan must run.

## Leads that turned out empty

So nobody repeats them:

1. **FDA and EMA orphan designation for MVA.** openFDA has no orphan drug endpoint at `/other/orphandrug.json` and EMA's list is only in IRIS. Skip generic web searches; go to accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/ and the EMA IRIS opinion list by hand.
2. **UKRI Gateway to Research.** `gtr.ukri.org/search/project?term=BUB1B` returns 404 to automated fetching and web search surfaced no UKRI record. Needs a manual browser session.
3. **CORDIS.** No EU project for BUB1B, BubR1 or MVA surfaced; only the CORDIS homepage.
4. **Charity Commission filings.** Full-print page 403, API needs a subscription key. Accounts unread.
5. **Conference abstracts, ASHG 2023 to 2025, ESHG, AACR, SIOP, ASCB.** Nothing on MVA treatment or BubR1 pharmacology. ASHG publishes abstracts as large PDFs that index poorly; a full-text grep of the downloaded PDFs is the only reliable route and was not done here.
6. **Readthrough or NMD patents naming BUB1B.** None. "BUB1B readthrough" hits are the natural BUB1B-PAK6 transcript, a different phenomenon.
7. **A free copy of the 2024 Nature Reviews Genetics review.** Confirmed not open access, and no citing paper quotes its treatment text. Stop looking.
8. **Frattini 2025.** Wiley returns 403. Open access not confirmed.
9. **Preprints on BubR1 stabilisation by chaperone or proteasome drugs, or drug senolytics in BubR1^H/H mice.** None. The senolytic evidence stays genetic INK-ATTAC clearance, as the dossier already says.
10. **Other participants' Track 2 material.** Nothing public; only the K-Dense Track 1 repo.
11. **A named funded PhD studentship.** Not on any public MVA Society page.
12. **MVA Society news index.** Radio, marathon, podcast, dinner and conference items only, no research announcements or PI names. Do not re-scrape it.
