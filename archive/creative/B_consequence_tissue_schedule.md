# B. Consequence, tissue and schedule directions

Divergent round, directions 4, 5 and 6 of archive/PROMPT_track2_creative.md. Written without reading archive/panel/. Sources are the two research dossiers plus live PubMed and regulatory records. Every idea excludes the ten items the prompt lists as already covered. Evidence tier is stated per idea; **[inference]** marks reasoning that no cited source states.

---

## Ranked ideas

### 1. Score the child for a type I interferon signature before treating anything as senescence

**Step.** Micronuclei formed by mis-segregation rupture and expose genomic DNA to cGAS, generating cGAMP and chronic STING-driven type I interferon (Mackenzie 2017, PMID 28738408; Harding 2017, PMID 28759889; Bakhoum 2018, PMID 29342134). A constitutional aneuploidy disorder is the human setting in which this should be tonic rather than episodic. **Drug.** None at this step; this is the gate for ideas 6 and 7. **Tier.** Cell and mouse for the mechanism, no human MVA measurement. **Failure.** MVA cells may clear micronuclei by autophagy without interferon output, as RNase H2 null cells partly do (PMID 29016854); the score may be flat. **Measurement.** Whole-blood 6-gene interferon score by the validated Aicardi-Goutieres method (Rice 2013, PMID 24183309), paired with the same score in both carrier parents, plus lymphocyte micronucleus frequency.

### 2. Reattribute the nephrocalcinosis to prematurity before attributing it to MVA

**Step.** Not a drug action but a causal reassignment that changes prescribing. The child was born at 32 weeks small for gestational age. Furosemide exposure is the strongest single risk factor for nephrocalcinosis in preterm infants (PMID 19460127), with parenteral nutrition, calcium and vitamin D load contributing (PMID 24625999). No published report establishes nephrocalcinosis as an MVA feature at all. **Drug.** Discontinue and permanently avoid loop diuretics; review any past neonatal exposure. **Tier.** Human, in preterm cohorts, not in MVA. **Failure.** If the calcification is instead distal renal tubular acidosis or ifosfamide tubulopathy from rhabdomyosarcoma therapy, removing a drug already stopped changes nothing. **Measurement.** Fractional excretion of calcium, urine pH after ammonium chloride or furosemide-free acidification testing, tubular phosphate reabsorption, and the neonatal drug record.

### 3. Add amiloride to the thiazide rather than raising the thiazide

**Step.** Amiloride blocks the epithelial sodium channel in the distal nephron, hyperpolarises the cell and increases calcium reabsorption through TRPV5, an effect additive to thiazide-driven hypocalciuria while sparing potassium (PMID 6619267; low-dose thiazide plus amiloride in stone prevention, Am J Med 1981, PII 0002-9343(81)90215-1, https://www.amjmed.com/article/0002-9343(81)90215-1/abstract). **Drug.** Amiloride, approved US and EU, and available as fixed-dose combination with hydrochlorothiazide. **Tier.** Human, adult stone formers; paediatric use is by extrapolation. **Failure.** Hyperkalaemia and metabolic acidosis, both harder to tolerate in a small child already receiving potassium citrate; amiloride is renally cleared, so any decline in filtration compounds it. **Measurement.** Paired 24-hour or spot urine calcium-to-creatinine ratio before and 6 weeks after, with serum potassium and bicarbonate at 1 and 4 weeks, and cystatin C-based eGFR because creatinine overestimates filtration at low muscle mass.

### 4. Cyproheptadine as the appetite agent, and an explicit veto on the hormonal alternatives

**Step.** Cyproheptadine is a 5-HT2 and H1 antagonist that raises intake without engaging any growth-factor or steroid axis. In children with cancer or treatment-related cachexia it produced weight gain in a direct comparison with megestrol (PMID 18989154), and in a paediatric feeding programme it improved growth in young children with feeding difficulty (PMID 24941960). **Drug.** Cyproheptadine, approved US and EU. **Tier.** Human, small uncontrolled paediatric series. **Failure.** Sedation and, rarely, hepatotoxicity; the weight gained may be fat rather than the muscle that is deficient here, which does not address the sarcopenia phenotype of the BubR1 mouse. **Measurement.** Weight-for-height plus a body-composition readout that separates the compartments, bioimpedance or DXA lean mass index, at baseline and 12 weeks, not weight alone.

### 5. A written aneugen-avoidance list, because the ordinary paediatric formulary contains three

**Step.** Approved drugs that raise mis-segregation act on the same axis the genotype has already weakened. Griseofulvin, still first-line for paediatric tinea capitis in several jurisdictions, is a classic spindle aneugen (PMID 25308540). Colchicine is the reference aneugenic positive control in OECD TG 487 validation (PMID 20362070; PMID 20478394). High-dose intermittent vitamin D has itself caused nephrocalcinosis and hypercalciuria (PMID 2386055). **Drug.** All three to be flagged as avoid, with named alternatives: terbinafine for tinea capitis, anakinra or an NSAID rather than colchicine for a recurrent pericarditis or fever indication, daily rather than stoss vitamin D. **Tier.** Human genotoxicity data plus **[inference]** for the transfer to a BUB1B-deficient host. **Failure.** Terbinafine is less effective against Microsporum; the substitutions carry their own costs. **Measurement.** Lymphocyte micronucleus frequency before and 4 weeks after any unavoidable exposure.

### 6. Hit-and-run rather than continuous senolysis, which moves the dasatinib growth warning

**Step.** The dasatinib label warning on growth in children derives from continuous daily dosing in chronic myeloid leukaemia, where tyrosine kinase inhibition disrupts the growth hormone axis over months (PMID 23716272). Senolytic effect does not require occupancy: a single oral dose of dasatinib plus quercetin gave cardiac benefit at 5 days and exercise benefit sustained beyond 7 months (Zhu 2015, PMID 25754370), and bi-weekly dosing extended post-treatment survival (Xu 2018, PMID 29988130). NCT04733534 uses days 1 to 3 and 30 to 32 only. **Drug.** Dasatinib alone, 3 consecutive days per month, quercetin omitted for its renal signal. **Tier.** Mouse for the schedule, human only in adults. **Failure.** No senolytic has been tested in BubR1 mice at all; and 3 days per month may still suffice to suppress the growth plate. **Measurement.** Height velocity and bone age at 6 and 12 months against the child's own pre-treatment trajectory, with p16 transcript in peripheral blood T cells as the target-engagement readout.

### 7. Lamivudine as a non-immunosuppressive way to lower the cytosolic DNA load

**Step.** Reverse transcriptase inhibitors reduce endogenous retroelement-derived cytosolic DNA and lowered the interferon score in Aicardi-Goutieres syndrome (PMID 30566312). Unlike baricitinib (PMID 29649002, EMA-approved from age 2 for juvenile idiopathic arthritis), a nucleoside analogue does not suppress interferon-gamma or JAK-dependent tumour surveillance, which matters when the cancer risk is of the order of 38 to 75 percent. **Drug.** Lamivudine, approved US and EU with paediatric oral solution from 3 months. **Tier.** Human, in a different interferonopathy; **[inference]** for MVA, where the cytosolic DNA is micronuclear rather than retroelement-derived, so the drug may act on the wrong source. **Failure.** Exactly that mismatch; also mitochondrial toxicity and renal dose adjustment. **Measurement.** Interferon score from idea 1 at 0, 4 and 12 weeks.

### 8. Glycerol phenylbutyrate, chosen over sodium phenylbutyrate for the sodium

**Step.** Aneuploidy imposes proteotoxic stress with impaired HSP90-mediated folding and a blunted heat-shock response (Oromendia 2012, PMID 23222101; Donnelly 2014, PMID 25205676). Phenylbutyrate is a chemical chaperone that reduces unfolded-protein-response signalling. **Drug.** Glycerol phenylbutyrate (Ravicti), approved US and EU for urea cycle disorders from 2 months; sodium phenylbutyrate delivers a sodium load that raises calciuria and is the wrong salt with nephrocalcinosis. **Tier.** Cell for the chaperone action, human only for safety in a different disease. **Failure.** Direction of benefit is unresolved. Relieving proteotoxic stress may improve somatic tissue and simultaneously improve the fitness of pre-malignant aneuploid clones, the same tension that makes rapamycin ambiguous in aneuploid yeast. **Measurement.** Fibroblast HSPA5 and DDIT3 transcript levels and thermal-stress survival in patient cells before proposing any exposure.

### 9. Treat ceramide as a direction-of-effect biomarker, not a target

**Step.** In aneuploid yeast, ceramides and long-chain bases are increased, and lowering ceramide genetically improves aneuploid cell fitness while raising it worsens fitness; serine and sphingolipid synthesis are a specific aneuploid liability (Hwang 2017, PMID 29281829). The sign is what matters clinically: for a child whose whole body is aneuploid, the fitness-improving direction is ceramide reduction. **Drug.** None with an approved indication at this step. Note the negative: glucosylceramide synthase inhibitors (miglustat, eliglustat) raise ceramide by blocking its exit, the fitness-worsening direction, so they are an avoid rather than a candidate. **Tier.** Yeast, with mammalian lipidomic support only. **Failure.** Yeast disomes are a poor model of a mosaic human. **Measurement.** Plasma C16:0 and C24:0 ceramide with the C16:0/C24:0 ratio, in the child and both carrier parents.

### 10. If rhabdomyosarcoma recurs, redesign the maintenance schedule rather than the induction

**Step.** RMS 2005 maintenance is vinorelbine on days 1, 8 and 15 plus continuous oral cyclophosphamide 25 mg/m2 for 28 days (Bisogno 2019, PMID 31562043). Vinorelbine is a spindle poison delivered to a host with a constitutionally weak checkpoint; the alkylator is not. Metronomic cyclophosphamide alone preserves the schedule with the aneugenic component removed. **Drug.** Oral cyclophosphamide, approved everywhere. **Tier.** Human for the combination, **[inference]** for the de-escalation, which has never been tested and discards part of a randomised survival benefit (5-year overall survival 85.0 versus 72.4 percent). **Failure.** Losing the vinorelbine contribution may cost more than the checkpoint argument saves; the direction of vinca toxicity in a BUB1B-deficient host is genuinely unknown. **Measurement.** Serial lymphocyte micronucleus frequency during a first vinorelbine cycle, which would give a within-patient answer before the second.

## Ideas rejected during this round and why

**Beta-agonists for muscle mass (formoterol, salbutamol).** BubR1 hypomorphic mice die of cardiac failure even after senescent-cell clearance improved every other measured outcome (Baker 2011, PMID 22048312), and SIRT2 overexpression reversed an ECG J-point defect (North 2014, PMID 24825348). Loading a cardiac phenotype of unknown severity for a modest anabolic effect inverts the risk. Rejected in favour of cardiac characterisation first: echocardiogram and 12-lead ECG with attention to the J point.

**Megestrol acetate.** Causes severe symptomatic adrenal suppression in paediatric oncology patients (PMID 12872362), and is a progestational agent in a child with a tumour predisposition. Cyproheptadine achieves the same endpoint without either liability.

**Oxandrolone.** Androgen exposure advances skeletal maturation and carries hepatic adenoma risk; in a child whose final height is already compromised, accelerating epiphyseal fusion trades adult stature for short-term weight.

**Bortezomib or any proteasome inhibitor.** Whole-genome-doubled and aneuploid cells depend on proteasome function (Quinton 2021, PMID 33505027). That dependency is a tumour vulnerability only when the tumour is the aneuploid compartment. Here the whole child is.

**STING agonists.** None approved, and the mechanistic direction is opposite to idea 1.

**N-acetylcysteine.** Baker 2013 applied paraquat and isoproterenol to BubR1 transgenics with no difference in oxidative-stress resistance, arguing against an antioxidant mechanism in this model (PMID 23242215).

**Fenofibrate or a statin to lower ceramide.** Myopathy risk in a child with low muscle mass, plus fenofibrate's creatinine rise confounding renal monitoring, for a sign-of-effect argument resting on yeast.

**Acetazolamide and topiramate.** Carbonic anhydrase inhibition promotes hypercalciuria and stone formation (PMC5976810). Named here so that a future seizure indication, present in 45 percent of MVA1, does not reach for them by default. Levetiracetam is the alternative without the renal mechanism.
