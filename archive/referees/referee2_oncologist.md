# Referee 2 report

Reviewer: paediatric oncologist and clinical trialist, cancer predisposition programme.
Manuscript: Track 2 report, drug repurposing for PROBAND01 (biallelic *BUB1B* MVA).

## 1. Summary of the claim

No approved medicine is a defensible repurposing candidate for this child, so the outputs are four non-prescribing products: a list of ordinary drugs to avoid as aneugens or hypercalciuric agents, three no-cost surveillance additions, a renal tubular work-up deciding between two causes of nephrocalcinosis, and measurements establishing whether any gene-directed hypothesis has a target in him. Secondary: that a carve-out written by intent rather than drug name protects cancer treatment, and that power arithmetic forces an aggregated crossover across the roughly 40 known patients. The central claim is statable in two sentences.

## 2. Validity

**Supported.** Kajii 2001 (PMID 11746029): total PCS in 3.5 to 41.7 percent of carrier parents' lymphocytes, botryoid rhabdomyosarcoma in two of ten infants, Wilms tumour in seven; the under 2 percent control figure is not in that source and I could not locate it. Nakano 2024 (PMID 39264246) recommends renal ultrasound every three months from birth to age 7 for all MVA conditions, and reading that horizon as a floor is fair. Rio Frio and Foulkes 2010 (PMID 21190457): ampullary adenocarcinoma at 34, later colon and gastric primaries, homozygous intronic c.2386-11A>G, PCS and MVA.

**Overstated.** Schneider 2020 (PMID 33209717) says MVA rhabdomyosarcoma is "highly likely to occur in the GU tract, primarily in the vagina and bladder," embryonal with botryoid features, on five case reports, opening "while data is limited." The manuscript keeps the conclusion and drops the qualifier.

**Contradicted; the one I expected to hold.** The manuscript gives EpSSG RMS 2005 maintenance 5-year overall survival as 85.0 against 72.4 percent, twice, once inside the safety carve-out. Bisogno 2019 (PMID 31562043) reports 86.5 percent (95 percent CI 80.2 to 90.9) against 73.7 percent (65.8 to 80.1), HR 0.52, p=0.0097, intention to treat. No erratum is indexed. Neither figure carries its interval, and the primary endpoint, disease-free survival, which did not reach significance (77.6 against 69.8 percent, p=0.061), goes unmentioned. A wrong number inside the clause meant to stop chemotherapy being withheld is the most consequential error here.

**Overstated.** Pavone 2022 (PMID 35804254) gives 38.7 percent as 12 of 31 published MVA1 cases with malignancy at report: a proportion of an ascertained series, not a lifetime risk. Section 8 uses it as one, naming ascertainment while omitting censoring, which pushes the other way.

**Contradicted in emphasis; the one I expected to fail.** Question 6 rests on "Janssen 2009 predicts amplified errors." Janssen 2009 (PMID 19855003) also reports that untransformed human fibroblasts with reduced Mps1 could not be sensitised to sublethal taxol, the most relevant experiment in that paper for a non-tumour patient, and it points away from the hypersensitivity worry. Sudo 2004 (PMID 15059905) is correctly cited for the opposite direction.

**Supported, one inference reversed.** Hanks 2004, Matsuura 2006, Suijkerbuijk 2010, Baker 2013 and North 2014 are as described. Wu 2023 (PMID 38009412) is correctly bounded to the wild-type background, but its authors' declared interest is in NAD+ precursors as therapeutics, so a null runs against that interest, not toward it as the manuscript argues.

**Unverifiable.** That hypomorphs "died of cardiac failure after senescent-cell clearance improved every other measured outcome" (Baker 2011), and the J-point endpoint attributed jointly to Sieben 2020 and North 2014.

## 3. Logic

The carve-out is not watertight. Section 6 item 6 recommends substituting cyclophosphamide for ifosfamide and adding dexrazoxane in a relapse regimen. The carve-out, printed twice, says do not reduce, omit, delay or substitute any agent in a rhabdomyosarcoma protocol. The document forbids on one page what it ranks on another, and tagging the row "governed by the carve-out" does not resolve it. Section 12 then concedes the panels did not know whether ifosfamide was given. A substitution is proposed for a drug whose administration is unknown, against a tubulopathy whose cause is unknown, in a protocol whose survival data the same row concedes sit downstream of ifosfamide induction.

Radiotherapy is absent from the whole manuscript. Nakano 2024 states that genomic instability disorders show sensitivity to ionizing radiation and that radiation exposure should be avoided, and local therapy in rhabdomyosarcoma is routinely radiotherapy. A clinician skimming a document that names spindle agents, alkylators and anthracyclines and never names radiotherapy may infer the question was considered and settled. Furosemide likewise sits on the avoid list while hyperhydration alongside alkylators is standard supportive care; the acute-indication clause covers it only for a reader already looking.

Rank order is an inference applied inconsistently. Metformin is removed from the ranked list on exposure arithmetic and on the argument that rank 6 of 7 puts a familiar drug in front of families as a candidate. Hydroxychloroquine, whose supporting experiment used a different drug at 25 micromolar against achievable paediatric plasma concentrations one to two orders lower, stays at rank 7 with no exposure arithmetic applied. The paper's best method is used on one drug and withheld from another with the same defect, and the survivor carries retinal, QT and antigen-presentation hazards in a child with a syndromic eye phenotype.

Two conclusions turn on single results: withdraw Rio Frio 2010 and addition (c) has no basis; withdraw Baker 2013 and the section 7 withdrawal of the BubR1-raising bar reverts.

## 4. Criteria

**Scientific rigour 6/10.** Tiering, shown-or-inferred marking and a verification pass that changed four positions are done properly. Against that: a misquoted randomised survival figure inside a safety clause, a break-even threshold resting on a number the paper elsewhere declines to choose, and power figures I cannot reproduce. Correcting the first and publishing the variance assumptions behind the third would take this to 8.

**Potential impact 6/10.** Addition (b) is real, free and correctly grounded; the rest acts on nothing this year. Naming the child's primary tumour site, so a reader can tell whether (b) adds anything to off-therapy follow-up that already exists, and closing the omissions in section 6, would raise it.

**Innovation 7/10.** The measurement gate, exposure arithmetic as a screening step, and a harm screen as a first-class deliverable are transferable and not standard practice. The drug-mapping tool is ordinary and its top hit is wrong for a reason the authors state. Running the promised transfer to a second gene would raise both this and the next score.

**Scalability 6/10.** Transfer to another gene is asserted and never run, and the aggregated protocol is arithmetically out of reach.

## 5. Statistics and quantitative claims

The break-even calculation is internally inconsistent. At s = 0.2 and added harm 5 percent, a required reduction above 60 percent follows only if lifetime risk is 38.7 percent (0.05 / (0.2 x 0.387) = 0.646). With the Orphanet 75 percent the same paragraph declines to discard, 0.05 / 0.15 = 0.33. The headline threshold depends entirely on the choice the authors say they are not making. s = 0.2 is asserted with no source, and section 9 item 12 concedes residual risk for a treated MVA1 child is unpublished.

The metformin arithmetic is correct: 500 to 1000 micromolar against 7.7 to 15.5 micromolar is 32 to 130 fold, and 5 mg/L is 38.7 micromolar, giving the stated 2.5 to 5 fold headroom.

I cannot reproduce the FISH figure. At an aneuploid fraction of 0.10 with 1000 nuclei the binomial standard error is 0.95 percentage points; three periods with three measurements each give about four degrees of freedom, and at 80 percent power, two-sided alpha 0.05, the detectable relative change is near 33 percent, not 22 percent. The three quoted endpoints imply three different unstated biological coefficients of variation, while section 9 item 10 concedes within-patient variance has never been measured in this disease. A power calculation whose variance term is the paper's own open question cannot bind a design.

Seventeen subjects is not inside a population of "roughly 15 to 20"; it exceeds the lower bound, and neither the range nor its derivation from 40 known patients is shown. In a disease where most reported infants died young, a design needing near-total enrolment of survivors, one central scoring laboratory and eleven months per participant is an obstacle, not a solution.

## 6. What is missing

A full blood count. Nakano 2024 and Pavone 2022 both list MDS, AML and ALL among MVA1 malignancies, and no haematological surveillance appears anywhere. It is free and keyed to a visit that already happens.

A statement that radiation-based imaging should be avoided in surveillance, guideline-endorsed and the strongest zero-cost addition available; and any consideration of whether this child tolerates standard-dose cytotoxic therapy and radiotherapy differently from an unaffected child, which for this audience is the first clinical question.

Content for addition (c), which says the age 7 horizon is a floor and stops. Modality, interval, starting age and when endoscopy enters are what a clinician needs, and the counterweight, that most MVA1 children do not reach the ages at which the Rio Frio phenotype appeared, is not stated.

Governance for the question 6 result. An ex vivo finding of vinca hypersensitivity, once published, will be used to dose-reduce whatever this document says.

## 7. Ethics and framing

The lay summary is defensible and the sentence stating that nothing here is expected to reduce cancer risk is the right sentence in the right place. Two problems. The same sentence calls three-monthly surveillance "the only thing that acts on that risk"; MVA surveillance rests on expert consensus and has never been shown to change mortality, so the paper is exacting about drug evidence and credulous about surveillance evidence in the one line families will read. And the renal section is a branching decision tree naming agents, sequence and escalation; a "not a prescription" header does not neutralise a document a clinician can follow, and section 6 item 6 reads as treatment advice on any plain reading. Re-identification is not addressed: a child with MVA, a treated rhabdomyosarcoma, nephrocalcinosis, neonatal furosemide and two named variants, in a population of about 40, is identifiable to anyone in the field.

## 8. Verdict

Major revision. In priority order:

1. Correct the RMS 2005 figures to 86.5 against 73.7 percent with intervals and the non-significant primary endpoint, and remove or restate section 6 item 6, which recommends the substitution its own carve-out forbids, for a drug the authors do not know was given.
2. Add radiotherapy to the carve-out; add a full blood count and a statement on radiation-based imaging to the surveillance additions; give addition (c) a modality, an interval and a starting age.
3. Fix the risk arithmetic: commit to a cancer risk figure or present the threshold across both, source or drop s = 0.2, publish the variance assumptions behind the three power figures, and withdraw the claim that 17 subjects is within reach.
