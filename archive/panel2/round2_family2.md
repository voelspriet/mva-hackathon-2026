# Round 2, patient-family representative 2

Seat: parent of a child with a different ultra-rare cancer-predisposition syndrome, working on trial design, consent and off-label reimbursement for a European rare-disease umbrella body. All seven other round-1 files in panel2 read; archive/panel/ not opened.

## (a) Objections

**CIN biologist.** Metformin at rank 1: section 4 concedes selection subtracts cells from organs with no reserve in a child with failure to thrive, and the PK file supplies the exposure gap, so rank 1 puts an off-label drug into a child at a concentration never shown to select. Second, rule 2 against chaperone inducers and rapalogs: section 5 says it has never been tested in patient cells, so an assertion is ranked as a finding.

**Endocrinologist.** The growth-drug refusal at rank 1: section 4 admits n=2 in the wrong gene. My objection is not the science. Dossier 03 M6 records growth hormone as a treatment the society's leaflet names, so a refusal with no stated re-entry route reads as a door closing. Second, the metformin stop rule at a 0.25 z-score fall is executed by a parent with a home scale, so it belongs in the consent document.

**Immunologist.** No chronic mTOR inhibitor at rank 1: section 4 calls it "a hold pending item 2, not a permanent bar", and rank 1 is read as settled. Second, "It is not evidence of a primary immune defect" is sound reasoning delivered as a verdict on a family's account of their own child (final section).

**Lab geneticist.** Aneugen avoidance at rank 1: section 4 concedes the sign may be wrong (Sudo 2004, PMID 15059905, against Janssen 2009, PMID 19855003) and restricts the rule to elective agents after an ex vivo test nobody has run, so it cannot be both first and conditional. Second, digoxin ex vivo at rank 6: bench work in a list of candidates for a child reads as sixth choice.

**Nephrologist.** Work-up-gated thiazide plus citrate at rank 1, also mine; the objection is packaging. Section 4 records that 61 to 90 percent of nephrocalcinosis of prematurity resolves by 24 months (PMID 16705005), so watchful waiting is first and the drug pair second. Second, rejecting niacin on renal grounds is right and contradicts the charity's own funded NAD+ strand (creative G lead 2), which must be said out loud, since families read silence as agreement.

**PK pharmacologist.** Thiazide plus citrate at rank 1, with the same section 4 apology as mine; two panellists apologising for one rank is a finding for the moderator. Second, the metformin exclusion is applied asymmetrically: hydroxychloroquine is admitted at rank 2 on an unmeasured claim that lysosomal concentration exceeds plasma "by orders of magnitude", while metformin is excluded on a measured plasma gap with no equivalent partitioning allowance.

**Predisposition oncologist.** The avoid-and-substitute rule at rank 1: I support the content and object to the readout, since "compliance" cannot fail visibly, as section 4 says. It needs a counted denominator, avoid-list agents prescribed per patient-year across the UK MVA audit (creative G lead 10). Second, "Four additions cost no extra visit": a longer scan on a small child and extra tests on a scheduled draw are not free, and both require asking.

## (b) Changes to my ranking

The nephrologist's gate splits my old rank 1 into work-up then drug pair. The PK arithmetic, with the CIN biologist's note that metformin was the weaker AMPK activator in Tang 2011, moves metformin from 4 to 6 as a pre-gated hypothesis. The endocrinologist's glucocorticoid sparing and GH re-entry gate fold into my avoidance rule. The immunologist's vaccination and serology item is the one new candidate I accept, because infection days are an outcome the family names. Hydroxychloroquine leaves my active list, on the PK asymmetry and the tissue-direction conflict (creative G lead 6).

Revised: 1, renal work-up with watchful waiting. 2, hydrochlorothiazide plus potassium citrate, amiloride as escalation. 3, the avoidance and substitution rule with the curative carve-out, steroid sparing and the GH gate. 4, cyproheptadine after a food-first run-in. 5, completed vaccination with post-vaccination titres. 6, metformin, only after an ex vivo dose-response at reachable concentrations.

## (c) Factual points to correct

1. The CIN biologist writes that Tang 2011 tested metformin and that it impaired accumulation of trisomy 13 and 16 cells, citing PMC3532042 against dossier 01 section 4.2, while dossier 02 section 5 already carried that statement and dossier 01 section 7 lists metformin substituting for AICAR as not tested. These are different claims, both quoted here as settled, and need one resolving sentence.
2. Five files render the cancer risk as a range, "38.7 to about 75 percent". Dossier 02 section 1 states the figures are unreconciled and probably reflect ascertainment, and creative F section 4 treats them as three scenarios. A range implies a number this family does not have.
3. The immunologist attributes sirolimus at 0.8 mg/m2 twice daily to dossier 02 section 5; it is from creative file I and Adams 2016, PMID 26783326.

## (d) The disagreement the moderator must not paper over

Metformin. Position A, the CIN biologist (rank 1) and the predisposition oncologist (rank 2): the only approved drug with a published direction of effect against aneuploid cells in human patient material, via the AICAR result in Pallister-Killian fibroblasts (creative G lead 5). Position B, the PK pharmacologist (excluded) and the endocrinologist (rank 6): paediatric plasma sits 15 to 60 times below the concentration at which AICAR acted, and the drug removes appetite from a child who is not growing. Settled by the metformin arm of that Pallister-Killian design, or the same design in MVA fibroblasts, at 8, 15 and 50 micromolar, aneuploid fraction by FISH at days 0, 7 and 14, threshold filed before the run (creative C idea 8).

## (e) Cross-checks

**(1) Metformin PK.** The concentration arbitration is not my competence; what follows is. The gap makes metformin a research question, not a prescription, and the Pallister-Killian result does not close it, since that arm was never run. So neither exclusion nor adoption: no child takes it before a dose-response at 8 to 15 micromolar in patient cells clears a pre-filed threshold, and the gap goes in the lay summary in one sentence. A family told only that metformin is cheap, licensed and might select against aneuploid cells will hear a treatment.

**(2) The single RT-PCR.** On assay design I defer; on cost and consequence I do not. It uses a blood sample of the kind already taken, needs a laboratory that can sequence cDNA at single-molecule depth, and settles two facts most lower-ranked candidates here depend on, so it comes first in the plan, ahead of every drug. The governance point is the cis outcome: the drug list is void, as the lab geneticist says, but for the family that is a re-diagnosis, unsettling an explanation they have absorbed and carrying implications for recurrence and for both parents. It therefore needs pre-test counselling of the diagnostic kind, all three answers named in advance, and must not be requested as a research add-on inside a protocol.

**(3) Should a rule outrank a drug.** Yes, on the family's own ordering of outcomes (dossier 03, M3), because it adds no exposure, costs nothing, and reaches every clinician who will meet this child once in a career (M8). Four of eight round-1 files rank a rule first, which should be recorded as a panel position. The carve-out sits at the head of the rule, same size type: "This list applies to elective, non-cancer prescribing only. It does not apply to chemotherapy given with curative or relapse-treatment intent. Vincristine, vinorelbine and dactinomycin remain standard in rhabdomyosarcoma protocols, and nothing in this list is a reason to reduce, delay or omit them. Any change to a cancer regimen is a decision for the treating oncology team alone." With one negative rule: no chemotherapy agent is named anywhere in the barred column, because a page mixing elective aneugens with vinca alkaloids will be read as one list by a clinician skimming it.

**(4) The "no clone above about 10 percent" premise.** It carries no weight in either direction. Creative F section 2 bounds a single clonal trisomy in blood at roughly 4 percent, bounds a variegated burden hardly at all, and has no sensitivity to a clone confined to kidney or muscle. So it is neither evidence the target is absent, which the lab geneticist's rank 4 reasoning edges towards, nor reassurance to offer a family. It also fails the ordinary consent test: no family can be asked to accept a drug's harm to remove cells whose presence in the treated tissue has never been measured. The measurement is interphase FISH or single-cell sequencing on blood, buccal cells and skin, and until it exists every aneuploidy-selective candidate is pre-gate. The report should carry the sentence "we do not know whether the target is present in him" in those words.

## Sentences a family would read as a promise, a prescription or a verdict

**CIN biologist, rank 1:** "the only approved drug with a published direction of effect against aneuploid cells in patient material." Required instead: "In one experiment on another patient's cells, a related unapproved compound reduced the aneuploid population. Metformin itself was not tested in that experiment, and no MVA patient has taken it for this purpose."

**Endocrinologist, rank 1:** "Rule: no somatotropin, no mecasermin, no androgen or progestin for growth." Required instead: "Growth hormone should not be started without a documented growth hormone deficiency. If the tests below show one, the decision returns to the family with the endocrine and oncology teams together."

**Immunologist, opening:** "It is not evidence of a primary immune defect." Required instead: "No immune measurement has been published in any MVA patient, so nothing here can confirm or rule out what families describe. The tests that would answer it are listed below."

**Lab geneticist, T1:** "In cis, the child retains an intact allele and the list below is void." Required instead, out of the table and in plain text: "It has not been shown that the two changes sit on different copies of the gene. One test can show this. If they are on the same copy, the diagnosis would need to be revisited with the clinical team, and that conversation should be prepared before the sample is taken."

**Nephrologist, rank 1, and my own round-1 wording:** "the only candidate with a paediatric licence, a dose, a readout inside six weeks, and an effect on a lesion the child has." Required instead: "and a measured effect on urinary calcium in other conditions. Whether it changes this child's kidney findings is not known."

**PK pharmacologist, exclusions:** "Metformin, on arithmetic." Required instead: "Metformin is excluded as a prescription on present evidence, because the concentration at which the effect was seen is 15 to 60 times higher than a child's blood level. A cell experiment could still change this."

**Predisposition oncologist, section 6:** "Four additions cost no extra visit." Required instead: "Four additions can be attached to visits already scheduled, if the family agrees to a longer scan and to the same blood sample being used for more tests."
