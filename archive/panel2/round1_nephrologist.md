# Round 1, paediatric nephrologist

Lens: nephrocalcinosis in a 32-week SGA child after rhabdomyosarcoma chemotherapy, and the renal ceiling on every other candidate. Sources: track2 dossiers, creative files A to J, PMIDs. Two web checks.

## 0. The premise the panel has to accept first

Nephrocalcinosis is not a described feature of MVA1: Pavone's 31 cases give renal dysfunction at 12.9% with no nephrocalcinosis figure, and dossier 02 §1 found no published report of it. The leaflet phrase "renal support" (dossier 03 §1, [M6]) has no operational content. So it is worked up as in any child, as file B2 says. Two causes compete: nephrocalcinosis of prematurity, where furosemide is the strongest risk factor (PMID 19460127) and 61 to 90% resolve by 12 to 24 months (PMID 16705005; PMC9587072); or ifosfamide proximal tubulopathy, in a quarter to a third of children above about 60 g/m2 cumulative, giving phosphaturia, proximal renal tubular acidosis, hypocitraturia and hypophosphataemic rickets, often permanent (PMID 12868118; PMID 8625085; PMC3246161). They demand opposite prescriptions, and dossier 02 does not say whether ifosfamide was given: EU practice (IVA) uses it, North American VAC does not (§4). Establishing the regimen is the cheapest step here.

## 1. Ranked list

**1. Work-up-gated hydrochlorothiazide plus potassium citrate.** Step: distal tubular calcium reabsorption plus urinary crystal inhibition. Tier: human, in other paediatric hypercalciuric disease, not in MVA; thiazides arrest nephrocalcinosis progression in XLH (PMID 11433085). Approval: both licensed, HCTZ 0.5 to 1 mg/kg/day in two doses from 6.25 mg twice daily, potassium citrate 0.3 to 0.6 mmol/kg/day, urine pH 6.5 to 7.2 and never above 7.5 (dossier 02 §3; file I). They correct each other on potassium. The gate: serum and urine calcium, phosphate, bicarbonate, magnesium, urine pH, tubular phosphate reabsorption, calcium-to-creatinine ratio, low molecular weight proteinuria, cystatin C. Harm: thiazide raises serum calcium, volume-depletes a child with failure to thrive, and its hypokalaemia stacks onto every QT-prolonging drug below. Readout: paired urine calcium-to-creatinine at baseline and 6 weeks, ultrasound at 6 months. Rank: the only candidate with a paediatric licence, a dose, a readout inside six weeks, and an effect on a lesion the child has.

**2. The renal avoidance rule set (a rule, not a drug).** Step: removal of iatrogenic calciuric load. Tier: human. Content: no loop diuretics again (PMID 19460127); no acetazolamide or topiramate, which matters because seizures occur in 45% of MVA1 and levetiracetam has no carbonic anhydrase mechanism (PMC5976810; file B5); no stoss vitamin D or over-the-counter calcium (PMID 2386055; file H §3); no NSAIDs during chemotherapy; sodium under 2 to 3 mEq/kg/day (file H §1). Plus the dosing rule: creatinine-based eGFR overreads at this muscle mass, so every renal threshold here is a cystatin C threshold (dossier 02 §3). Harm: terbinafine replacing griseofulvin is less effective against Microsporum; salt restriction competes with energy density. Readout: the drug chart. Rank second: free, and it blocks the most likely route to a worse kidney.

**3. Amiloride added to the thiazide rather than raising the thiazide.** Step: ENaC blockade, hyperpolarisation, TRPV5-mediated calcium reabsorption, additive to thiazide and potassium sparing (files B3, J4; PMID 6619267). Tier: human, adult stone formers. Approval: US and EU, including fixed-dose combination with HCTZ, no paediatric licence here. Harm: hyperkalaemia and acidosis on top of potassium citrate, with trimethoprim prophylaxis stacking potassium further (file I); amiloride is renally cleared. Readout: potassium and bicarbonate at 1 and 4 weeks, urine calcium-to-creatinine at 6 weeks. Rank third: safer than doubling a thiazide in a child with poor volume reserve.

**4. Phosphate plus alfacalcidol, only if the work-up shows ifosfamide Fanconi.** Step: replacement of renal phosphate and bicarbonate losses. Tier: human, in ifosfamide tubulopathy and XLH. Approval: both licensed paediatrically. Harm: here the correct treatment of the tubule worsens the calcification, since phosphate plus active vitamin D is the standard cause of nephrocalcinosis in XLH, which is why thiazide was studied there (PMID 11433085). It runs with candidate 1, with 6-monthly ultrasound. Readout: tubular phosphate reabsorption, serum phosphate, alkaline phosphatase, wrist radiograph. Rank fourth: conditional on a finding not yet established, and it addresses growth through bone (dossier 03 §1).

**5. Metformin, with a hard renal gate.** Step: AMPK activation; impaired accumulation of trisomic cells in culture (Tang 2011, PMID 21315436; dossier 02 §5). Tier: cell, plus feasibility precedents in Fanconi anaemia and a paediatric oncology phase I (PMC9972017; file C7). Approval: licensed from age 10, so off-label by age, oral solution available, phase I range 666 to 2000 mg/m2/day (file I). Harm: lactic acidosis with falling filtration, additive to thiazide volume depletion and vincristine ileus; stop rule is review below eGFR 45 and stop below 30 on cystatin C, with hold days for vomiting, contrast or ifosfamide (file I). Readout: cystatin C and bicarbonate monthly, micronucleus frequency at 0 and 6 months (file J1). Rank fifth: cell-only mechanism, and the kidney sets the stop rule.

**6. Hydroxychloroquine, last among candidates I would allow.** Step: lysosomal and autophagic dependency of aneuploid cells (Santaguida 2015, PMID 26404941; Tang 2011). Tier: cell. Approval: paediatric rheumatology dosing 5 mg/kg/day actual weight (dossier 02 §5). Harm: no SmPC renal formula, so 4 mg/kg below eGFR 45; QT stacking with ondansetron under thiazide hypokalaemia (file I); retinopathy screening from age 5, earlier with reduced filtration. G6PD is normal (file D §6). Rank sixth: allowable, but it spends ECG, potassium and eye-screening capacity on a cell-level hypothesis.

## 2. Creative-round ideas adopted and rejected

Adopt B2, reattribution before treatment, my rank 1 gate. Adopt B3 and J4, amiloride as add-on. Adopt B5, the aneugen and carbonic anhydrase avoid list. Adopt J13, home urine calcium-to-creatinine sticks, because clinic-interval titration cannot track a value that moves with diet and illness. Adopt H §1 and §3: sodium target, calcium inside meals, no unsupervised vitamin D. Adopt B8's glycerol over sodium phenylbutyrate. Adopt D §6's closure of SLC34A1 p.Ala133Val as likely benign, leaving the nephrocalcinosis genetically unexplained.

Reject A4, caffeine citrate as an SMG1 brake: the salt is trivial and the caffeine dose is not renally neutral. Reject A's aminoglycoside readthrough even though D §6 clears MT-RNR1 of m.1555A>G, since cochlear safety is not renal safety and gentamicin in a calcified kidney is a poor trade for single-digit readthrough. Reject the quercetin arm of D plus Q on the renal signal above 1 g/day (dossier 02 §5), and veto B6's dasatinib pulse on fluid retention, QT with thiazide hypokalaemia and the growth warning. Reject C5 niacin and file I's nicotinamide row at pharmacological doses: nicotinamide accumulates in renal impairment, causes thrombocytopenia in dialysis trials, and raises phosphate questions in a possibly phosphate-wasting child. Reject J9 amifostine. Neutral on C6 fasting minimisation.

## 3. Exclusions on renal grounds

Loop diuretics; acetazolamide and topiramate; stoss vitamin D and unsupervised calcium; systemic aminoglycosides; NSAIDs during chemotherapy; sirolimus, everolimus, temsirolimus and tacrolimus, for proteinuria, delayed tubular recovery and immunosuppression in a cancer-predisposed child (file I; dossier 02 §5); navitoclax, unapproved; mecasermin, contraindicated with a malignancy history; growth hormone, no MVA efficacy signal (dossier 02 §2); colchicine and griseofulvin, aneugenic, colchicine also renally cleared.

## 4. Strongest objection to my own top candidate

If the calcification is residual nephrocalcinosis of prematurity, 61 to 90% resolve within two years without any drug (PMID 16705005), and I would commit a child with failure to thrive to a twice-daily diuretic, a three-times-daily potassium salt, four-weekly bloods and a food-refusal risk (file H §3) for an outcome that was coming anyway. If instead the mechanism is ifosfamide Fanconi, the hypocitraturia comes from proximal acidosis and the thiazide adds volume depletion to a polyuric tubulopathy. Hence a gate, not a prescription.

## 5. Evidence that would change the ranking

The chemotherapy regimen and cumulative ifosfamide dose; one paired serum and urine tubular panel with cystatin C; a spot urine calcium-to-creatinine ratio; the neonatal furosemide record; the ultrasound trajectory over two years. Any of these moves candidate 1 to first line, to conditional, or off the list, and candidate 4 from conditional to mandatory. A measured micronucleus frequency in the child and both parents decides whether candidates 5 and 6 have a readout at all; without it they are untestable inside a year.
