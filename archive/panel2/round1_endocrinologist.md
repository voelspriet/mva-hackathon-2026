# Round 1, paediatric endocrinologist (panel 2)

## What kind of growth failure this is

The deficit starts before birth (SGA, 32 weeks), and prenatal-onset growth failure is not a GH-axis phenotype. Growth retardation is near-universal in MVA1 (29/31) while documented endocrine dysfunction is 4/31 (dossier 02 §1, Pavone 2022 PMID 35804254). The mouse maps the mechanism onto tissue rather than hormone: BubR1^H/H dwarfism, sarcopenia and fat loss track p16^Ink4a in muscle and fat and are attenuated by p16 ablation or senescent-cell clearance (dossier 01 §3, §4.1; Baker 2008 PMID 18516091, Baker 2011 PMID 22048312). No GH or IGF-1 study exists in any Bub1b model (dossier 02 §2), and the MVA2 GH non-response (n=2, *CEP57*, PMID 36588761; creative G §7) is off-gene but is the only human growth-treatment datum here.

Hypothesis [inference]: GH-sufficient, partly nutritional, partly cell-intrinsic growth failure with a senescent growth plate. It predicts an IGF-1 normal for nutritional state (GH resistance would give a low IGF-1 with normal or high GH) and a bone age tracking nutrition.

## 1. Ranked candidates

**1. Rule: no somatotropin, no mecasermin, no androgen or progestin for growth, with a diagnostic gate instead.** Step: GH/IGF-1 axis. Tier: human guideline and label. All are approved paediatric drugs, which is why a rule is needed; the MVA Society leaflet lists growth hormones as current treatment for short stature (dossier 03, M6). Phenotype: mecasermin is contraindicated with a malignancy history (FDA 021839s021); GH is treated as contraindicated in cancer-predisposition syndromes, with evidence called insufficient in higher-risk patients (Raman 2015 PMID 25839904; Sklar 2018 PMID 29982476), against an MVA1 cancer risk of 38.7% to about 75% (dossier 02 §1); oxandrolone advances bone age; megestrol causes adrenal suppression in paediatric oncology (creative B). Harm: a subsequent primary neoplasm, or fusion bought with weight. Readout: the gate itself, IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 SDS on stable intake, bone age, TSH and free T4, morning cortisol, pituitary imaging only if IGF-1 is low on adequate intake. Rank: the only item here with human evidence, and it blocks the intervention this family is most likely to be offered.

**2. Cyproheptadine as the single appetite agent, with a body-composition readout.** Step: 5-HT2 and H1 antagonism, engaging no growth-factor or steroid axis. Tier: human, small uncontrolled paediatric series (creative B4; PMID 18989154, PMID 24941960). Approved US and EU, syrup. Phenotype: no renal clearance problem, no growth-signalling rationale to trouble the cancer risk. Harm: sedation, rare hepatotoxicity, weight gained as fat rather than the deficient lean mass. Readout: monthly MUAC z-score plus lean mass index at 0 and 12 weeks, never weight alone. Rank: the nutritional arm is the largest modifiable component and this reaches it without the excluded axes.

**3. Rule: glucocorticoid sparing, in every route.** Step: growth plate directly, plus calciuria. Tier: human, class-level. The exposures here are antiemetic dexamethasone each chemotherapy cycle, steroid or ACTH for the infantile spasms seen in 45% of MVA1 (dossier 02 §1), and inhaled or intranasal courses. Harm: height lost where there is no reserve, urinary calcium raised into an established nephrocalcinosis, immunosuppression in a cancer-predisposed child. Readout: knemometry around any unavoidable course. Rank: same evidence class and cost as item 1, fewer exposures.

**4. Hydrochlorothiazide plus potassium citrate, escalated with amiloride rather than more thiazide, and vitamin D given to a measured level.** Step: renal calcium handling and bone. Tier: human, in other paediatric nephrocalcinosis populations, with paediatric dosing (dossier 02 §3; creative B3, J4; HCTZ 0.5 to 1 mg/kg/day, potassium citrate 0.3 to 0.6 mmol/kg/day). Phenotype: thiazide raises serum calcium, so calcium and vitamin D supplements stop and 25-OH-D is targeted rather than loaded, since high-dose intermittent vitamin D has itself caused nephrocalcinosis (PMID 2386055); cystatin C replaces creatinine for eGFR at low muscle mass (creative I). Harm: hypercalcaemia, hypokalaemia, a bone-mineral cost if calcium intake is cut. Readout: urine Ca/creatinine at 6 weeks, K and bicarbonate at 1 and 4 weeks. Rank: standard care, but this is where the endocrine detail is got wrong.

**5. Levothyroxine, only on documented hypothyroidism.** Tier: human, standard. Endocrine dysfunction is reported in 12.9% of MVA1 with no stated type, and unrecognised hypothyroidism is a reversible cause of growth arrest that would otherwise be charged to the syndrome. Harm: over-replacement advances bone age. Readout: TSH and free T4 yearly. Rank: low prior, cheapest correctable cause here.

**6. Metformin, deferred, and only with a growth guard.** Step: AMPK, the approved stand-in for the AICAR aneuploidy-selective result (dossier 01 §5, Tang 2011 PMID 21315436; creative G §5 names the untested metformin arm in Pallister-Killian fibroblasts). Licensed from age 10, off-label by age here; solution exists (creative I). Phenotype, from this lens: it reduces appetite and weight, the direction this child cannot afford, and lactic acidosis risk rises with thiazide, vomiting and renal decline. Readout: a stop rule if weight-for-age z-score falls 0.25 over 12 weeks. Rank: last, a net negative from the growth side until feeding is stable.

## 2. Creative ideas adopted or rejected

- **B2 / B3 / J4**, reattribute the nephrocalcinosis to prematurity and neonatal furosemide first, and use amiloride as the additive hypocalciuric step ahead of higher thiazide doses: adopt both.
- **B4**, cyproheptadine with a veto on the hormonal alternatives: adopt, it is my item 2, along with B's rejections of megestrol, oxandrolone and beta-agonists.
- **B5 / J12**, a written avoid list: adopt, with item 1's entries added.
- **B6**, hit-and-run dasatinib to move the growth warning: reject as a growth-safety argument. TKI growth deceleration is prepubertal and comes with normal IGF-1 and IGFBP-3, so the action is on cartilage, not the axis (imatinib PMID 21592517, PMID 38497150; dasatinib CA180-226, Patterson 2019 HemaSphere PF416), and cartilage effects are not cleared by lowering occupancy.
- **J6**, knemometry, 0.09 to 0.16 mm resolution (PMID 26854116): adopt as the primary short-interval growth endpoint, the only measure that makes an 8-week crossover readable. **J5**, dosing into the spurt window: mapping only.
- **J14 / J10 / C11**, home grip strength and sit-to-stand, and loaded exercise as the comparator any muscle drug must beat: adopt, with the mTORC1 caveat. **J11**, mirtazapine: second line behind cyproheptadine.
- **C12**, a pre-specified negative-control outcome: adopt, height velocity, for every non-growth candidate. **H**, food-first for 8 to 12 weeks before any new drug: adopt as the run-in for item 2.
- **C5**, niacin as the NAD+ route: reject, no growth data and the SIRT2 result is a transgene. **A1 to A4** (digoxin, amlexanox, ataluren, caffeine): no adoption here, none carries a growth readout.

## 3. Excluded outright

Somatotropin, mecasermin, oxandrolone and testosterone, megestrol (item 1). Aromatase inhibitors and GnRH analogues for height: no indication, and puberty timing is undocumented. Continuous daily dasatinib. Sirolimus and everolimus for the ageing hypothesis: mTORC1 is the pathway through which chondrocytes and satellite cells grow, carrier sarcopenia correlates with mTORC1 hyperactivity so the direction is contested (Sieben 2020 PMID 31738183), and immunosuppression with stomatitis in an underfed child buys no growth endpoint. Topiramate and acetazolamide for any future seizure indication (hypercalciuria); levetiracetam instead.

## 4. Strongest objection to my top candidate

The refusal rests on inference. The only human growth-treatment evidence in MVA is two *CEP57* siblings, wrong gene, n=2, and posterior fossa malformation occurs in a quarter of MVA1 patients, so a structural hypothalamic-pituitary lesion with true GH deficiency is possible and treatable. Refusing a licensed therapy on mechanism costs a family that names growth as a priority, while the cancer harm is a probability on an already high baseline. The rule is a gate, not a ban: documented deficiency with a matching MRI reopens it, under the Sklar 2018 one-disease-free-year condition. But I do rank a refusal first on evidence that would not support a prescription.

## 5. What would change the ranking

An IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 with a bone age: a low IGF-1 on adequate intake moves GH from excluded to gated, a normal or high value closes it. A pituitary MRI. A knemometry baseline showing whether mini growth spurts occur at all, since their absence would demote every growth-directed candidate. Growth-plate p16 in the BubR1 mouse, unpublished so far: chondrocyte senescence would lift a senolytic arm above cyproheptadine, its absence would sink it. A senolytic given as a drug to those mice with bone length as the outcome. Any MVA1 GH case, published or registry. And p16 or micronucleus frequency in this child's own cells, before any drug is chosen on the senescence model.
