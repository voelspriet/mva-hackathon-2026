# Q22. The second published clinical precedent: transplantation in MVA1

**Asked because** the de Wolf thesis reference list contained a second clinical management report in this exact syndrome that this project had never found, and because the first one, a rhabdomyosarcoma treated with reduced-intensity chemotherapy, remains paywalled. This one is open access.

**Laberko A, Balashov D, Deripapa E, Soldatkina O, Raikina E, Maschan A, Novichkova G, Shcherbina A. "Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation in a patient with type 1 mosaic variegated aneuploidy syndrome." Orphanet J Rare Dis 2019;14(1):97, PMID 31053147, PMC6500003.** Dmitriy Rogachev National Center for Pediatric Hematology, Oncology and Immunology, Moscow. Full abstract reached.

## The case, in the authors' own words

- **Genotype.** "The BUB1B gene mutations c.498_505delAAACTTTA and c.1288 + 5G > A were detected" — a frameshift and a splice-region allele. Structurally the same shape as this child's genotype, one clearly truncating allele plus one whose consequence is less certain.
- **Natural history, which is the useful part.** "The patient presented with cytopenia soon after birth, but remained stable until 9 years of age, when he developed myelodysplastic syndrome associated with monosomy of chromosome 7."
- **The intervention.** "Due to severe dependence on blood transfusions, a TCRαβ+/CD19+ depleted HSCT was performed from a matched unrelated donor (MUD) using a **treosulfan-based reduced intensity conditioning (RIC) regimen**."
- **The outcome.** "The engraftment occurred, and no severe toxicity was observed soon after the HSCT, but on day + 47, graft rejection was detected. It was followed by prolonged pancytopenia and sepsis with multi-organ Enterococcus faecium infection, which led to the patient's death on day + 156 after HSCT."
- **Their conclusion.** "RIC HSCT with TCRαβ+/CD19+ depletion was well tolerated and resulted in complete hematologic recovery in our MVA1 patient, but, unfortunately, it was followed by rapid graft rejection. This fact needs to be taken into consideration for HSCT in other MVA patients."

## What this changes

### 1. The haematological surveillance addition gains a timeline and a target

The report already added a full blood count and already names myelodysplastic syndrome, acute myeloid leukaemia and acute lymphoblastic leukaemia as being in the MVA1 spectrum. What it did not have is a natural history. This case supplies one: cytopenia from birth, clinically stable for nine years, then myelodysplastic syndrome with **monosomy 7**.

That is directly actionable. Monosomy 7 is a specific cytogenetic lesion, and it is detectable on the same karyotype and interphase FISH the gate already asks for. A blood count that falls is the trigger; monosomy 7 is what to look for when it does. The report can now say what the count is watching for rather than only that it is free.

### 2. A second instance of clinicians reducing cytotoxic intensity in this syndrome

The carve-out in section 5 tells a treating team not to reduce, omit, delay or substitute chemotherapy on the basis of this report. Two published cases now exist in which treating teams did reduce intensity in MVA: reduced-intensity chemotherapy for rhabdomyosarcoma (Nishitani-Isa 2019) and reduced-intensity conditioning for transplant (this case). Neither contradicts the carve-out, which bars de-escalation **on the basis of this report** and leaves regimen decisions to the treating team. But the pattern is now two of two, and a document written about this decision should say so.

### 3. A hard datum for a decision the report does not discuss

If this child ever needs a transplant, this is the entire published experience in MVA1: engraftment achieved, rejection at day 47, death at day 156. The authors themselves flag rejection as the finding to carry forward. The report does not discuss transplantation and should not start, since nothing in this child's presentation calls for it. But the fact belongs in the record rather than being rediscovered by the next reader.

## What it does not change

Nothing in the drug-avoidance rule, the ranked hypotheses or the measurement gate. This is one patient with a different allele pair, and a single case cannot establish tolerance or intolerance of conditioning in a syndrome.

## Corrections applied

The full blood count addition in section 6 gains the natural history and names monosomy 7 as the lesion to look for. The carve-out discussion in section 5 records that two published cases now exist of reduced intensity in this syndrome rather than one.
