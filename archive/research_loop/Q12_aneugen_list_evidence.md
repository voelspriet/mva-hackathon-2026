# Q12. How much of the aneugen avoidance list rests on human evidence at clinical exposures?

**Asked because** this is the first thing a clinical pharmacologist reviewing the report would attack. A rule that bars drugs from a child on the strength of in vitro assays at concentrations nobody achieves is a rule that does harm for no benefit. The audit had already flagged one link in the chain, that "benzimidazoles bind the same tubulin site", as inferred and unsourced.

## Answer

**The list is better supported than the report claims, and it is supported in the opposite direction to the report's own argument.** The drug the report was least confident about has direct paediatric human evidence. The drug it built the whole justification on has the weakest human evidence of any of them.

### The evidence, drug by drug

| Drug | Human in vivo evidence at clinical exposure | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Albendazole** | **Yes, and in children.** Sister chromatid exchange and micronucleus frequencies measured in lymphocytes of paediatric patients aged 6 to 13 before and after treatment for hepatic hydatid disease: "MN frequencies in all the patients increased significantly following albendazole treatment" (PMID 17331781) | Strongest member of the list |
| **Mebendazole and the benzimidazole class** | **Class evidence, and it is exposure-gated.** For flubendazole, "a bone marrow micronucleus test, using a formulation that limited systemic absorption, was negative", while "the in vivo micronucleus test of the new formulation of flubendazole also showed evidence of induced aneugenicity" (PMID 26443851) | Real, but proportional to systemic exposure |
| **Colchicine** | **No.** It is the reference aneugen of the in vitro human lymphocyte micronucleus assay (Ramirez 1997), which is what the report cites. The only human in vivo cytogenetic study of therapeutic colchicine found in this search examined 38 patients on colchicine for familial Mediterranean fever for up to three years and concluded "no untoward effects on long-term colchicine treatment in FMF with respect to fertility, teratogenicity and chromosomal damage" (PMID 602955) | Weakest human support of the four |
| **Griseofulvin** | Not located. In vitro and rodent evidence only from the searches run | Inferred |

### Why the colchicine result does not simply refute the list

The familial Mediterranean fever study measured **mitotic rate, percentage tetraploidy and chromosome breakage**. Breakage is a clastogen endpoint. Colchicine is an aneugen, not a clastogen: it acts on the spindle, so it produces whole-chromosome missegregation and micronuclei, not broken chromosomes. **A study measuring breakage would be expected to find nothing even if the drug were strongly aneugenic in that population.** Tetraploidy is spindle-related and was also negative, which carries some weight, but micronucleus frequency and aneuploidy, the endpoints that would actually detect the effect, were not measured.

So this is not a refutation. It is a **missing measurement**, and no modern human micronucleus study of therapeutic colchicine was located. The honest position is that colchicine's aneugenicity in humans at therapeutic dose is untested rather than absent.

### What this changes about the rule

**Three things, and the first is the one that matters.**

1. **The report's inference chain runs backwards.** Section 5 justifies the list by naming colchicine as a reference aneugen and then extending to benzimidazoles by an unsourced claim that they bind the same site. The evidence is the other way round: albendazole has direct paediatric human micronucleus data, and colchicine has none at therapeutic exposure. The rule should be argued from albendazole outward, not from colchicine outward.

2. **Risk within the class is graded by absorption, and that is actionable.** The flubendazole result shows benzimidazole aneugenicity in mammals appears when systemic absorption is improved and disappears when the formulation limits it. Mebendazole is poorly absorbed and albendazole substantially better absorbed as its sulfoxide, which is standard pharmacology rather than a finding here. So within the class the concern ranks albendazole above mebendazole, and the report currently treats them identically.

3. **It sharpens rather than softens the substitution problem.** These are the drugs a child may genuinely need. The report already says the bar is a reason to ask the treating team rather than to leave a helminth infection untreated. That stands, and is now better founded: the caution is not theoretical, it was measured in children of about this age.

### What could not be established

No study measures micronucleus frequency or aneuploidy in humans taking therapeutic colchicine, and none was located for griseofulvin either. The calciuric limb of the list, acetazolamide, topiramate, loop diuretics and high-dose vitamin D, is not an aneugen question and was out of scope here; it rests on stone and nephrocalcinosis evidence assessed elsewhere in the report.

## Correction applied

Section 5 now cites the paediatric albendazole data rather than asserting that benzimidazoles bind the same site, records that the class effect tracks systemic absorption so albendazole and mebendazole are not equivalent, and states plainly that colchicine's human in vivo aneugenicity at therapeutic dose is untested rather than demonstrated.

## Access levels reached

Abstracts via NCBI efetch for all four papers; the figures and quoted conclusions are in the abstracts. Searches run across four formulations covering benzimidazole genotoxicity in humans, benzimidazole tubulin selectivity, colchicine cytogenetics in treated patients, and griseofulvin aneugenicity.
