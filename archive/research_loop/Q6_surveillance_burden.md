# Q6. What does surveillance cost a family in burden rather than money?

**Asked because** the report justifies four surveillance additions on the ground that they are free, and a referee or a parent would immediately ask free to whom. Free in money is not free in consequence.

## Answer

**The report's own additions really are close to zero marginal burden, and it was right about that. But "free" is the wrong word, and the report's weakest surveillance argument, its rejection of whole-body MRI, turns out to be defensible on much stronger grounds than the one it used.**

### Separate marginal burden from total burden, because the report only controls one

The four additions are a genotype call read from whole genome sequence already in hand, a full blood count keyed to a draw that already happens, a reading of an electrocardiogram already indicated, and a rule about which imaging modality to choose rather than an extra scan. None adds a visit. That is a real property and the report should keep claiming it.

**What it does not control is the existing protocol**, and that is where the burden sits: renal ultrasound every three months from birth to age seven (Nakano 2024). Twenty-eight scans before school age is the load this family already carries, and nothing in this report changes it either way.

### The rejection of whole-body MRI is better supported than the report says

Section 8 rejects whole-body MRI because "no validated imaging surveillance protocol for rhabdomyosarcoma in MVA exists". True, but weak: absence of validation is not evidence of harm. There is now measured performance.

**Utility of Whole-Body Magnetic Resonance Imaging Surveillance in Children and Adults With Cancer Predisposition Syndromes: A Retrospective Study, PMID 40138602:**

> "The sensitivity and specificity of WB-MRI in our cohort were 64% and 92%, respectively, with a false-positive rate (FPR) of 8% and a false-negative rate of 36%."

**Medical guidelines for Li-Fraumeni syndrome 2019, version 1.1, PMID 34633580**, on the syndrome where whole-body MRI surveillance is most established:

> "the effectiveness of this surveillance is unknown, and there are problems, such as adverse events associated with a high rate of false positives, overdiagnosis, and sedation used during imaging as well as negative psychological impact."

A false-negative rate of 36 percent is the number that matters most for a family, because a normal scan is what they are being sold. And a young child needs sedation or general anaesthesia for whole-body MRI, which is a repeated anaesthetic exposure with nothing to show for it two thirds of the time a tumour is present.

**This also explains why the consensus chooses renal ultrasound.** Ultrasound needs no sedation, no contrast and no radiation, which is why a three-monthly schedule is tolerable at all. The modality choice is doing the work, not the interval.

### Where the report should stop saying "free"

A full blood count on an existing draw costs nothing in money and nothing in visits. It is not consequence-free: a count that comes back abnormal starts a pathway, and in this syndrome, where cytopenia from birth is on record and myelodysplastic syndrome appeared at age nine in a published case, that pathway leads to a marrow examination. That is the right pathway and the addition is still correct. But the honest claim is **no marginal cost, not no consequence**, and the difference is exactly what a parent is entitled to be told before consenting.

### What could not be quantified

No study measures surveillance burden in mosaic variegated aneuploidy specifically. Everything above is borrowed from Li-Fraumeni syndrome and from mixed cancer-predisposition cohorts, where whole-body MRI is the modality under study. The three-monthly renal ultrasound schedule this child actually follows has no published false-positive rate that this search could reach, which is itself worth stating: the burden of the surveillance that is actually happening is unmeasured.

## Corrections applied

Section 8's rejection of whole-body MRI now carries the measured performance and the guideline's own statement about sedation and psychological impact, rather than resting on absence of validation. The surveillance additions section replaces "free" with a statement of what is and is not free, and names where the real burden sits.
