## 1. Bakhoum SF et al., Nature 2018 (PMID 29342134) — direction of the cGAS-STING finding

**Answer: the paper says chronic cGAS-STING signalling in chromosomally unstable cells PROMOTES metastasis.** It is not an immune-clearance paper.

Abstract conclusion, verbatim (PubMed / Nature):

> "Here we show that chromosomal instability promotes metastasis by sustaining a tumour cell-autonomous response to cytosolic DNA. Errors in chromosome segregation create a preponderance of micronuclei whose rupture spills genomic DNA into the cytosol. This leads to the activation of the cGAS-STING (cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes) cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway and downstream noncanonical NF-κB signalling. Genetic suppression of chromosomal instability markedly delays metastasis even in highly aneuploid tumour models, whereas continuous chromosome segregation errors promote cellular invasion and metastasis in a STING-dependent manner. By subverting lethal epithelial responses to cytosolic DNA, chromosomally unstable tumour cells co-opt chronic activation of innate immune pathways to spread to distant organs."

The discussion (full text, PMC5785464) makes the escape-from-clearance logic explicit:

> "Given that chromosomally unstable cells are awash with cytosolic DNA, we raise the interesting possibility that by suppressing downstream type I interferon signaling and instead upregulating the alternative NF-κB pathway, they have substituted a lethal epithelial response to inflammation with that of myeloid-derived cells, thereby engaging in some form of immune mimicry."

Note what the metastasis experiments used, because it matters for the disagreement: orthotopic injections were performed "into the mammary fat pad of athymic or immune competent BALB/c mice" (MDA-MB-231 and 4T1 respectively), and suppressing CIN reduced spontaneous metastasis in both.

*Evidence level: full text, open access (PMC5785464). No paywall barrier.*

### 1b. The separate immune-clearance literature

These are real and they support the other expert's *phenomenon*, just not as a reading of Bakhoum 2018.

**Santaguida S et al., Dev Cell 2017 (PMID 28633018), "Chromosome Mis-segregation Generates Cell-Cycle-Arrested Cells with Complex Karyotypes that Are Eliminated by the Immune System."** Abstract:

> "We find that chromosome mis-segregation leads to further genomic instability that ultimately causes cell-cycle arrest. We further show that cells with complex karyotypes exhibit features of senescence and produce pro-inflammatory signals that promote their clearance by the immune system. We propose that cells with abnormal karyotypes generate a signal for their own elimination that may serve as a means for cancer cell immunosurveillance."

**Wang RW, Viganò S, Ben-David U, Amon A, Santaguida S. EMBO Rep 2021 (PMID 34105235), "Aneuploid senescent cells activate NF-κB to promote their immune clearance by NK cells."** Abstract — note the cancer-cell caveat in the last sentence, which is the bridge to Bakhoum:

> "Our data indicate that in untransformed aneuploid cells, NF-κB signaling upregulation is central to elicit this immune response. Inactivating NF-κB abolishes NK cell-mediated clearance of untransformed aneuploid cells. In cancer cell lines, NF-κB upregulation also correlates with the degree of aneuploidy. However, such upregulation in cancer cells is not sufficient to trigger NK cell-mediated clearance, suggesting that additional mechanisms might be at play during cancer evolution to counteract NF-κB-mediated immunogenicity."

**Joo YK et al., Mol Cell 2023 (PMID 37788673), "ATR promotes clearance of damaged DNA and damaged cells by rupturing micronuclei."** Abstract:

> "Furthermore, ATRi reduces cGAS-mediated senescence and killing of MN-bearing cancer cells by natural killer cells."

**Reconciliation for the report.** Both experts are pointing at documented results. Bakhoum 2018 is about chromosomally unstable *tumour* cells, where chronic cytosolic-DNA sensing is rewired toward non-canonical NF-kB and is pro-metastatic. Santaguida 2017 and Wang 2021 are about *untransformed* aneuploid cells that senesce and are cleared by NK cells, and Wang 2021 states in its own abstract that the same NF-kB upregulation is *not* sufficient to trigger NK clearance in cancer cell lines. So the direction of Bakhoum 2018 is unambiguous, and the clearance literature does not contradict it; it describes the earlier, pre-transformation stage that tumour cells have escaped.

*Evidence level: PubMed abstracts for all three (all also open access in PMC: PMC5536848, PMC8339690, PMC10599252).*

## 2. Baker DJ et al., Nat Cell Biol 2013 (PMID 23242215)

**Answer: yes on all three counts. The premise used to reject NAD+/SIRT2 agents runs the wrong way.**

Abstract, verbatim:

> "Using a transgenic approach in mice, we show that sustained high-level expression of BubR1 preserves genomic integrity and reduces tumorigenesis, even in the presence of genetic alterations that strongly promote aneuplodization and cancer, such as oncogenic Ras. We find that BubR1 overabundance exerts its protective effect by correcting mitotic checkpoint impairment and microtubule-kinetochore attachment defects. Furthermore, sustained high-level expression of BubR1 extends lifespan and delays age-related deterioration and aneuploidy in several tissues. Collectively, these data uncover a generalized function for BubR1 in counteracting defects that cause whole-chromosome instability and suggest that modulating BubR1 provides a unique opportunity to extend healthy lifespan."

So: reduced tumorigenesis including against oncogenic Ras, reduced aneuploidy in several tissues, extended lifespan. The typo "aneuplodization" is in the published abstract; quote it as printed or paraphrase.

**Caveat to state in the report.** This is a whole-body transgene raising BubR1 above normal, not a NAD+ precursor. It rebuts the specific mechanistic premise ("raising BubR1 in every cell would raise aneuploid-cell fitness") but it does not by itself validate NAD+/SIRT2 agents, which raise BubR1 indirectly and were not tested for tumour or aneuploidy endpoints in North 2014.

*Evidence level: PubMed abstract, verbatim. Nat Cell Biol full text is paywalled; PMC3707109 exists but I did not need it for the claim as asked. Body-of-paper effect sizes were not retrieved.*

## 3. North BJ et al., EMBO J 2014 (PMID 24825348 / PMC4194088)

Open access. All three sub-claims verified from full text.

### (a) BubR1 K668 acetylation mechanism — confirmed

> "We purified BubR1 acetylated in vivo by CBP and incubated it in vitro with or without rSIRT2, to determine sites specifically acetylated by CBP and deacetylated by SIRT2 (Supplementary Fig S3A and B). We detected acetylation on lysine-668 (K668; Fig 3 A), a residue that lies in a highly conserved region among vertebrates, between the amino-terminal domain, which is involved in inhibition of the APC, and the kinase domain of BubR1"

Causal direction, and the mimic mutants:

> "To determine whether the acetylation status of the single site, K668, was sufficient to regulate BubR1 degradation, we replaced K668 with glutamine (K668Q) to mimic a constitutively acetylated state or with arginine (K668R) to mimic the non-acetylated state. The K668Q enhanced ubiquitinated BubR1, and, conversely, the K668R mutation reduced it"

> "These results indicate that CBP-mediated acetylation at K668 drives BubR1 toward proteasome-mediated protein degradation, and this pathway is counteracted by SIRT2"

### (b) NMN dose, route, duration, animals, and whether BubR1 protein rose — confirmed, with a tissue caveat

Methods:

> "3-month- and 30-month-old C57BL/6 mice were obtained from the NIA Aged Rodent Colony. Mice were injected with 500 mg/kg/day nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN; Sigma Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) diluted in PBS or vehicle for 7 days. Injections were performed prior to dark cycle. On the final day, mice were injected at beginning of light cycle and sacrificed 5 h following injection."

Results:

> "Intraperitoneal injection of NMN for 7 days (500 mg/kg/day) lead to an increase in NAD+ within the heart and testes in young and in aged mice (Fig 6 B and C). Although BubR1 protein levels were difficult to determine in the heart tissue of aged animals, BubR1 protein levels were clearly increased in testes following NMN treatment (Fig 6 D). This increase in BubR1 was observed in both young and aged mice, and more remarkable, BubR1 levels in aged NMN-treated animals were brought back to the levels of the 3-month-old animals (Fig 6 E)."

So: **500 mg/kg/day, intraperitoneal, 7 days, in 3-month-old and 30-month-old wild-type C57BL/6 mice.** BubR1 protein rose — but the clean readout is testes; heart was not resolvable in aged animals. Group sizes for the NAD+ measurements were n = 3-4. There is no lifespan or aneuploidy endpoint in the NMN arm at all: NMN was a 7-day biomarker experiment.

SIRT2-dependence was shown in cells, not mice:

> "NMN treatment of wild-type MEFs induced BubR1 protein abundance, and this induction was almost completely absent in the Sirt2 −/− MEFs (Fig 6 G)."

### (c) SIRT2 transgene lifespan effect on BubR1 H/H mice — confirmed, numbers exact

> "Interestingly, although the SIRT2tg/BubR1 H/H mice remained small (Supplementary Fig S4C–E), they experienced a 58% increase in median lifespan and 21% increase in maximal lifespan compared to the BubR1 H/H control mice (Fig 4 A). Lifespan extension was observed preferentially in male mice, with a 123% increase in median lifespan (Fig 4 B). There was no change in median lifespan for females (Fig 4 C). These results indicate that increasing SIRT2 activity can extend the lifespan of male BubR1 H/H mice."

Figure 4 legend, with the statistics:

> "A Lifespan of BubR1 H/H (n = 31) and SIRT2tg/BubR1 H/H (n = 33) mice. P = 0.0384 determined by log-rank test.
> B Lifespan of males BubR1 H/H (NT, n = 16) and SIRT2tg/BubR1 H/H (SIRT2tg, n = 17) mice, P = 0.004.
> C Lifespan of females BubR1 H/H (NT, n = 15) and SIRT2tg/BubR1 H/H (SIRT2tg, n = 16) mice, N.S. (P = 0.7915)."

The +58%/+123%/none figures reported to me are exactly right. Two things worth carrying into the report: the overall P is 0.0384, which is marginal and is driven entirely by males; and the baseline is a progeroid, short-lived hypomorph, so this is rescue of a deficit, not lifespan extension from a normal baseline.

The authors themselves flag the sex effect and its precedent:

> "We were initially surprised that the ability of SIRT2 to extend the lifespan of BubR1 H/H mice was gender specific, with a bias toward male animals. However, the recently published lifespan extension of BubR1 transgenic mice also showed a bias toward male mice (Baker et al, 2013), suggesting a shared etiology"

### Wu LE et al., Aging Cell 2023 (PMID 38009412) — does it contradict?

**No. It bounds the claim.** The title is "SIRT2 transgenic over-expression does not impact lifespan in mice", but the abstract concedes the BubR1 result explicitly and then tests a different question:

> "Transgenic overexpression of SIRT2 in the BubR1 hypomorph model of progeria can rescue many aspects of health and increase overall lifespan, due to a specific interaction between SIRT2 and BubR1 that improves the stability of this protein. It is less clear whether SIRT2 is relevant to biological ageing outside of a model where BubR1 is under-expressed. Here, we sought to test whether SIRT2 over-expression would impact the overall health and lifespan of mice on a nonprogeroid, wild-type background."

Result:

> "While we previously found that SIRT2 transgenic overexpression prolonged female fertility, here, we did not observe any additional impact on health or lifespan, which was measured in both male and female mice on standard chow diets, and in males challenged with a high-fat diet."

Conclusion:

> "Overall, we conclude that strategies to enhance SIRT2 protein levels may not lead to increased longevity."

**How to state it exactly:** Wu 2023 does not dispute that SIRT2 overexpression extends lifespan in BubR1 H/H progeroid mice; it accepts that in its own opening. What it removes is the extrapolation to normal animals. If a report leans on North 2014 to argue that raising SIRT2 or NAD+ extends healthy lifespan generally, Wu 2023 is the direct counterweight and must be cited. Note also that David Sinclair is a co-author on both papers, and Wu 2023 carries a competing-interest declaration that LEW and DAS "are advisors and shareholders in EdenRoc Sciences, the parent company of Metro Biotech NSW and Metro Biotech which are developing NAD+ precursors as therapeutics" — that is, the negative result comes from the same group with a commercial interest in the positive one, which strengthens rather than weakens it.

*Evidence level: North 2014 full text (PMC4194088, open access, including figure legends and methods). Wu 2023 abstract plus conflict-of-interest statement (open access, PMC10726910); I did not read the Wu 2023 survival curves themselves.*

## 4. Ataluren / Translarna — EU non-renewal and UK status

### EU: not renewed, 28 March 2025 — confirmed

The EMA medicine page for Translarna is now flagged **"Expired — This medicine's authorisation has expired"** and states, verbatim:

> "On 28 March 2025, the European Commission issued a decision to not renew the conditional marketing authorisation for Translarna (ataluren) in the European Union (EU). The marketing authorisation holder for the medicine was PTC Therapeutics International Limited.
> The non-renewal of the marketing authorisation follows the opinion issued by EMA's human medicines committee, CHMP, which recommended to not renew the authorisation because the effectiveness of the medicine has not been confirmed."

And for the original grant:

> "Translarna was granted marketing authorisation in the EU on 31 July 2014 for the treatment of Duchenne muscular dystrophy."

The date reported to me (28 March 2025) is exact, and the mechanism is correct: this was a **non-renewal of a conditional marketing authorisation**, not a withdrawal or a suspension. Use "not renewed", not "revoked".

Source: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/translarna

### UK: still authorised — confirmed, with a Great Britain caveat

The current UK SmPC on the electronic medicines compendium lists three Translarna products (125 mg, 250 mg and 1000 mg granules for oral suspension), MA holder **PTC Therapeutics International Limited**, with:

> "8. Marketing authorisation number(s)
> PLGB 44221/0004
> 9. Date of first authorisation/renewal of the authorisation
> 24/02/2026
> 10. Date of revision of the text
> 24/02/2026"

Indication unchanged from the former EU label:

> "Translarna is indicated for the treatment of Duchenne muscular dystrophy resulting from a nonsense mutation in the dystrophin gene, in ambulatory patients aged 2 years and older"

**Caveat that should be in the report.** The number is a **PLGB**, a Great Britain marketing authorisation. Northern Ireland continues to follow EU pharmaceutical law, so "authorised in the UK" is imprecise: it is authorised in Great Britain. The renewal date of 24 February 2026 also means the MHRA licence was actively renewed *after* the EU non-renewal, which is the sharper point: the two regulators reached opposite conclusions on the same evidence base.

Sources: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/10430/smpc (250 mg; 125 mg and 1000 mg are products 10431 and 2576). MHRA's own products.mhra.gov.uk search is a client-side app and did not return server-rendered results to a fetch, so the emc SmPC (the MHRA-approved label text) is the primary record I could reach.

*Evidence level: EMA medicine page, live text, quoted verbatim. UK SmPC, live text, sections 4.1 / 7 / 8 / 9 / 10 quoted verbatim. I could not retrieve an MHRA press statement explaining the divergence.*

## 5. Dasatinib paediatric formulations — powder for oral suspension

**The two experts are each right about one jurisdiction. The suspension is authorised in the EU and not in the US.**

### EU: powder for oral suspension is authorised

The Sprycel EPAR product information contains a **second, complete SmPC** for the suspension:

> "1. NAME OF THE MEDICINAL PRODUCT
> SPRYCEL 10 mg/mL powder for oral suspension
> 2. QUALITATIVE AND QUANTITATIVE COMPOSITION
> One bottle of powder for oral suspension contains 990 mg of dasatinib (as monohydrate). After constitution, one bottle contains 99 mL of oral suspension. Each mL of oral suspension contains 10 mg of dasatinib (as monohydrate)."

The tablet SmPC cross-refers to it and makes it the required form below 10 kg:

> "The tablet is not recommended for patients weighing less than 10 kg; the powder for oral suspension should be used for these patients."

> "SPRYCEL film-coated tablets and SPRYCEL powder for oral suspension are not bioequivalent. Patients who are able to swallow tablets and who desire to switch from SPRYCEL powder for oral suspension to SPRYCEL tablets or patients who are not able to swallow tablets and who desire to switch from tablets to oral suspension, may do so, provided that the correct dosing recommendations for the dosage form are followed."

**Approval date.** From the EPAR "procedural steps taken and scientific information after the authorisation (archive)", procedure **X/0056/G**, a grouped extension application whose scope includes "Annex I_2.(d) Change or addition of a new pharmaceutical form" alongside "C.I.6.a — Change(s) to therapeutic indication(s)":

> "X/0056/G — This was an application for a group of variations. ... Opinion issued 26/04/2018 — Commission Decision 02/07/2018 — SmPC, Annex II, Labelling and PL"

So: **CHMP opinion 26 April 2018, European Commission decision 2 July 2018.** The paediatric Ph+ ALL indication came later, procedure II/0059, opinion 13/12/2018, Commission Decision 06/02/2019. The Sprycel MA itself dates from 20 November 2006 (latest renewal 15 July 2016).

### US: no powder for oral suspension approved

- The current FDA label for SPRYCEL (openFDA, effective 21 August 2026) contains **zero occurrences of the word "suspension"** anywhere in the label, and lists only "SPRYCEL (dasatinib) Tablets ... 20-mg, 50-mg, 70-mg, 80-mg, 100-mg, and 140-mg".
- Drugs@FDA: all 12 dasatinib applications on record (including SPRYCEL NDA 021986 and NDA 022072, and every generic) have dosage form **TABLET**. There is no suspension product record, active or discontinued.
- DailyMed returns 18 dasatinib SPLs, none an oral suspension.
- The FDA approval letter for the paediatric CML indication, **NDA 021986/S-020, approved 9 November 2017**, is explicit about what was approved:

> "Please refer to your Supplemental New Drug Application (sNDA) dated May 9, 2017 ... for Sprycel® (dasatinib) 20 mg, 50 mg, 70 mg, 80 mg, 100 mg, and 140 mg tablets.
> This Prior Approval supplemental new drug application provides for a new indication for the treatment of pediatric patients with Philadelphia chromosome-positive (Ph+) chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) in chronic phase."

The word "suspension" does not appear in that letter. The paediatric Ph+ ALL indication followed as NDA 021986/S-021, approved 21 December 2018 — again tablets.

The tell in the US label is that it carries the same sub-10-kg exclusion as the EU label but with **no alternative offered**:

> "Tablet dosing is not recommended for patients weighing less than 10 kg."

In the EU that sentence points the prescriber to the suspension. In the US it is a dead end. That asymmetry is the finding worth reporting.

### Weight-band dosing, both forms

**EU / US tablets** (identical bands and doses in both labels):

| Body weight | Daily dose |
|---|---|
| 10 to less than 20 kg | 40 mg |
| 20 to less than 30 kg | 60 mg |
| 30 to less than 45 kg | 70 mg |
| at least 45 kg | 100 mg |

US footnote: "For pediatric patients with Ph+ ALL, begin SPRYCEL therapy on or before day 15 of induction chemotherapy, when diagnosis is confirmed and continue for 2 years." Both labels require recalculating the dose every 3 months on body-weight change.

**EU powder for oral suspension, 10 mg/mL upon constitution** — note the doses are *not* the same as the tablet, because the two forms are not bioequivalent:

| Body weight | Daily dose, mL (mg) |
|---|---|
| 5 to less than 10 kg | 4 mL (40 mg) |
| 10 to less than 20 kg | 6 mL (60 mg) |
| 20 to less than 30 kg | 9 mL (90 mg) |
| 30 to less than 45 kg | 10.5 mL (105 mg) |
| at least 45 kg | 12 mL (120 mg) |

The suspension SmPC also covers "adult patients with Ph+ CML-CP who cannot swallow tablets", and warns:

> "The dose for the use of powder for oral suspension in adult patients with accelerated, myeloid or lymphoid blast phase (advanced phase) CML or Ph+ ALL has not been determined."

Anyone comparing the two tables should notice that at the same weight band the suspension delivers a *higher* milligram dose than the tablet (60 vs 40 mg at 10-20 kg; 120 vs 100 mg at 45 kg+). That is the non-bioequivalence, not an error.

Sources: EMA Sprycel EPAR product information PDF (https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/product-information/sprycel-epar-product-information_en.pdf), EPAR procedural steps archive PDF, FDA SPRYCEL label via openFDA, Drugs@FDA (openFDA drugsfda), FDA approval letter https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2017/021986Orig1s020ltr.pdf

*Evidence level: full primary label text on both sides (EU SmPC PDF, US SPL) plus the FDA approval letter and the EMA procedural-steps record. No paywall anywhere in item 5.*
