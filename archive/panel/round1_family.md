# Round 1, patient-family representative

Position: parent-advocate from an ultra-rare paediatric cancer-predisposition community, EURORDIS summer school level in evidence appraisal. I speak only from published family and patient-organisation text (03_family_perspective.md), and will not contact this family.

## How I rank

The MVA Society's order is symptom management first, cancer risk second, cure third (03 §1, M3). Families in comparable syndromes add quality of life over survival alone (03 §3, F1), gentler scans, less toxic cancer treatment (03 §3, PMC5880269), and a monitoring load that does not fall entirely on parents (03 §3, PMID 36929011). Three-monthly renal ultrasound to age seven is already recommended (02 §2, Nakano 2024). So I weight: does it touch something the child lives with today, does it fit inside existing monitoring, does it leave growth, muscle, immunity and feeding alone, does it plausibly lower cancer risk. Innovation counts for nothing here.

## Ranked list

**1. Hydrochlorothiazide plus potassium citrate (nephrocalcinosis).**
Target: renal calcium handling and urine citrate, not BubR1; this treats a consequence. Evidence: human other (thiazides arrested nephrocalcinosis progression in XLH, PMID 11433085; 02 §3). Paediatric dosing published: HCTZ 0.5 to 1 mg/kg/day, potassium citrate 0.1 to 0.2 g/kg/day, with potassium monitoring (02 §3). Phenotype: nephrocalcinosis is not a documented MVA feature, so the cause needs its own work-up first (02 §1, inferred). Harm: hypokalaemia and volume depletion in a child with failure to thrive. Readout: urine calcium/creatinine ratio and ultrasound grade at the scans already booked. Why rank 1: the only entry addressing a present symptom with paediatric dosing, at zero extra visits.

**2. Metformin.**
Target: AMPK, the pathway AICAR used to kill aneuploid mouse cells (01 §5, Tang 2011). Metformin itself was never tested for aneuploidy-selective killing (01 §4.2, §7: inference). Evidence: cell (AICAR), inference (metformin). Paediatric approval from age 10 for T2DM; paediatric oncology phase I exists (02 §5). Phenotype: lactic acidosis risk with renal impairment; gastrointestinal intolerance is not minor in failure to thrive. Harm: appetite and weight loss, cutting against the growth outcome families name. Readout: premature chromatid separation fraction or micronucleus frequency in lymphocytes at routine draws. Why rank 2: cheap, oral, decades of paediatric safety, monitoring fits existing bloods; the mechanism is the weakest part.

**3. Hydroxychloroquine.**
Target: autophagy and lysosomal stress that aneuploid cells depend on (01 §5, Tang 2011; Santaguida 2015). Evidence: cell, with chloroquine not HCQ, and "not as dramatic" as the other two compounds. Paediatric rheumatology dosing 5 mg/kg/day (02 §5). Phenotype: no renal or growth conflict at that dose; retinopathy risk is low under age five but needs an eye exam, and MVA already carries ocular anomalies, so that visit may exist (02 §1, §5). Harm: retinal toxicity, QT effects. Readout: as entry 2. Why rank 3: long paediatric record and one added examination, against thin mechanistic evidence.

**4. Dasatinib (senolytic hypothesis; quercetin is a supplement and out of scope).**
Target: senescent cell clearance, which improved muscle fibre diameter and treadmill distance in BubR1 hypomorphic mice by genetic means (01 §4.1, Baker 2011). No senolytic drug was ever given to BubR1 mice (01 §7, correction 1). Evidence: BubR1 mouse for the target, inference for the drug. Paediatric approval for Ph+ CML with weight-band dosing (02 §5). Phenotype: the label warns on effects on growth, plus myelosuppression and fluid retention (02 §5). Harm: it hits growth, and demands frequent blood counts. Readout: p16 in T cells, plus a walking or grip measure the family can see. Why rank 4: the best mouse rationale for muscle, paid for in the currency families least want to spend.

**5. Sirolimus or everolimus.**
Target: mTORC1, hyperactive in sarcopenia-prone BubR1 heterozygous mice (01 §3, Sieben 2020). Evidence: BubR1 mouse correlation only, no intervention. Paediatric use from age one in TSC (02 §5). Phenotype: immunosuppression where a family reports "weakened immunity" (03 §2, S2); stomatitis in 43 percent, against a child needing nutritional support; aneuploid yeast grow better under rapamycin (02 §5). Harm: infection, mouth pain, monthly troughs. Readout: muscle mass by DXA, infection count. Why rank 5: the burdens land on daily life and the cancer direction is unclear.

## Excluded

- Recombinant GH and mecasermin: cancer history is a contraindication on the mecasermin label; no MVA1 efficacy data, no response in MVA2 (02 §2, §5).
- Hydroxyurea: raises micronucleus frequency, the very readout we would use (02 §5).
- Taxanes and vinca alkaloids outside oncology, colchicine, griseofulvin: aneugenic in SAC-weakened cells or by class (01 §4.3).
- Acetazolamide, topiramate, high-dose vitamin D: all promote nephrocalcinosis (02 §3).
- Navitoclax, fisetin, NMN, quercetin, AICAR, HSP90 inhibitors: not approved medicines (01 §4.2, §5).
- Aspirin, statins: no link to the biology; statin myopathy conflicts with low muscle mass (02 §5).

## Strongest objection to my top candidate

It is not repurposing. Thiazide plus citrate is standard care for hypercalciuric nephrocalcinosis, does nothing for cancer risk, and the nephrocalcinosis may have a treatable cause not named publicly (ifosfamide tubulopathy, vitamin D, RTA; 02 §1, §3). A panel could say I ranked a routine prescription first to avoid choosing.

## Acceptable-risk position

Parents here are "about as risk tolerant for their children as adult patients are for themselves" (03 §3, PMID 18826414), and Bloom families were "cautiously willing" (03 §3, PMC5880269). Off-label use without evidence carried a 50 percent higher adverse reaction rate (03 §5, G1). My line: accept reversible harms visible on tests the child already has; refuse any label carrying growth, immunity or neoplasm language; refuse anything aneugenic; refuse anything whose monitoring adds a new department to the calendar.

## What a family needs to hear before a monitored n-of-1

- Duration: fixed in advance, for example six months, with readouts at the three-monthly scans already booked.
- Stopping rules: written down before the first dose; laboratory thresholds stop it, any new mass stops everything, and the family may stop at any time without giving a reason.
- Who decides: the treating team prescribes and documents the off-label justification and monitoring plan (03 §5, G1); the family consents and the child assents where possible (03 §5, FDA1); ethics approval and registration as in the aggregated n-of-1 model (03 §5, PMID 26185179).
- Data: de-identified, shared with the MVA Society patient database (03 §1, M6), published whether it works or not (03 §5, N1), shown to the family first.
- Time: told "some considerable time in advance" (03 §5, N1), not at the clinic door.

## What the scientists are likely to miss

1. The family will read the report. "Hypothesis for follow-up" must appear next to every drug name, or a parent takes a ranked list to a paediatrician as a prescription.
2. The child has had cancer. Families of survivors fear recurrence more than they hope for centimetres; a small "subsequent neoplasm" number in a label ends the conversation.
3. Feeding is the daily battleground in failure to thrive. Nausea, mouth ulcers and appetite loss are not grade 1 events at home.
4. The parents are carriers. Carrier parents show PCS in 3.5 to 41.7 percent of lymphocytes (01 §3, Kajii 2001) and heterozygous mice are prone to sarcopenia (01 §3). No drug panel will mention that the parents may be wondering about themselves, and about the pregnancies they lost.
5. Nothing proposed may weaken adherence to three-monthly surveillance, the only tool families are told protects their child today (03 §1, M4).
6. The readout must include something a parent can observe: weight, walking, days in hospital. A p16 value alone will not hold a family through six months.
