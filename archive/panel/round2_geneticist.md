# Round 2: clinical geneticist

## (a) Objections

**Mitosis cell biologist.**
Top candidate, dasatinib. The INK-ATTAC result is keyed to a mouse whose BubR1 level is known (~10% of wild type, Sieben 2020, PMID 31738183), and the same paper shows allelic identity rather than total level drives outcome: H/L1002P median survival 343 days against 196 days for H/H at comparable total reduction (01 §2.3). This child's residual BubR1 is unmeasured and one allele is an uncharacterised VUS, so the p16 burden of the H/H mouse cannot be assumed.
Second claim, his rank 5. Pimitespib is approved only in Japan and only for advanced GIST (01 §5), and he notes that HSP90-dependent turnover governs destabilised BubR1 missense protein (Suijkerbuijk 2010, PMID 20516114). A drug that could lower residual BubR1 further, in a child whose level is unknown, belongs in his exclusion list.

**Paediatric oncologist.**
Top candidate, hydrochlorothiazide plus potassium citrate. He concedes in his §3 that the urine chemistry work-up has not been done, then ranks the thiazide co-equal with alkali. If the lesion is distal renal tubular acidosis or post-ifosfamide proximal tubulopathy (02 §3), alkali is the treatment and the thiazide contributes hypokalaemia and volume depletion in a child with failure to thrive. Order the pair: citrate now, thiazide contingent on documented hypercalciuria.
Second claim, his rank 5. He argues that substituting cyclophosphamide for ifosfamide "preserves" the vinorelbine maintenance that gave 85.0% against 72.4% overall survival. RMS 2005 randomised patients in remission after nine IVA-based cycles (02 §4), so that estimate sits downstream of ifosfamide induction; a VAC induction changes the population it came from.

**Clinical pharmacologist.**
Top candidate, hydroxychloroquine. Aneuploidy-selective agents require an aneuploid target, and mosaic aneuploidy was not detected above about 10% of cells (archive/PROMPT_track2.md, Facts). His n-of-1 primary endpoint is a 30% fall in micronuclei from a baseline never measured in this child. If that baseline is near normal the protocol can neither succeed nor fail.
Second claim, in his exclusions. He discounts everolimus partly on enzyme-inducing antiepileptics, "which 45% of MVA1 children may need". The 45.1% in Pavone 2022 (PMID 35804254) is seizure frequency, not enzyme-inducer use, and no seizure term appears in this child's HPO list.

**Family representative.**
Top candidate, the same nephrology pair, and she raises the non-repurposing objection herself. The remaining problem is "at zero extra visits": 02 §3 requires serum potassium after initiation and each increment, so the thiazide adds draws. Citrate alone comes closer to her own criterion.
Second claim, her rank 2. Metformin is described as having "decades of paediatric safety". Approval is from age 10 in type 2 diabetes (02 §5); below that it is off-label with one paediatric oncology phase I as precedent (02 §6). Her stated line refuses reversible harms that touch feeding, and metformin's gastrointestinal intolerance and weight effect engage that line directly.

## (b) What changes my ranking

Three arguments move me. The oncologist and the family representative both place the hydroxychloroquine ocular collision above metformin's inferential weakness, and the pharmacologist adds that fields and OCT may be unobtainable with developmental delay: against MVA's own cataract, corneal opacity and glaucoma (Orphanet ORPHA 1052), unmonitorable retinal toxicity outweighs a two-step inference. The oncologist's dexrazoxane entry rests on a BubR1-model organ finding (H/H mice die of cardiac failure despite senescent-cell clearance, Baker 2011, PMID 22048312; low cardiac stress tolerance and the J-point defect in H/L1002P, Sieben 2020). Everolimus loses its slot: three colleagues exclude it and the cell biologist supplies two wrong-direction arguments (aneuploid growth under rapamycin, immunosuppression against clearance of SASP-positive cells).

Revised list:
1. Potassium citrate, thiazide added only on documented hypercalciuria. Change: the pair is now sequenced, not co-prescribed.
2. Deferred pharmacotherapy pending BubR1 immunoblot, PCS/micronucleus baseline, parental phasing. Change: the micronucleus baseline is now also the precondition for any n-of-1 endpoint.
3. Metformin. Change: up from 4, on the ocular argument against hydroxychloroquine, with cystatin C dosing and sick-day rules as conditions.
4. Hydroxychloroquine. Change: down from 3, unmonitorable retinal risk against a syndromic eye phenotype.
5. Dexrazoxane, conditional on anthracycline exposure. Change: new entry, adopted from the oncologist.
6. Dasatinib. Change: rank held, now also carrying a parent-observable readout (weight-for-age, walking, hospital days) per the family representative.

Everolimus removed from the list. Every entry keeps "hypothesis for follow-up" against its name.

## (c) Factual error

The cell biologist's framing states that "both patient alleles act by lowering protein amount, not by making a poison (Suijkerbuijk 2010, PMID 20516114; 01 §1.3, §2.2)". 01 §7 lists "that p.(Asn1002Lys) belongs to the destabilising class" under Inferred, not shown, adding that Q921H, also inside the kinase domain, was indistinguishable from wild type in Suijkerbuijk 2010. NMD of p.Leu737Ter is likewise inference from transcript geometry (01 §2.1). Neither allele here has been shown to lower protein amount, and his rank 1 and rank 5 both rest on that premise.

## (d) The disagreement the moderator must not paper over

Whether senescence in this child is the lesion or a brake. The cell biologist ranks dasatinib first and then argues in his own objection that clearing p16-positive cells may remove a restraint on karyotypically complex cells, citing Santaguida 2017 (PMID 28633018) and Thompson and Compton 2010 (PMID 20123995), with Baker 2011 not testing tumour incidence and p19^Arf ablation worsening the mouse (Baker 2008, PMID 18516091). The oncologist and the family representative rank the same drug last on growth and marrow harm; I rank it last on genotype. A summary reading "mechanistic front-runner with paediatric harms" would conceal a contradiction about direction of effect in a child whose reported cancer risk runs from 38.7% to about 75% (02 §1). One experiment resolves it: a senolytic given to BubR1^H/H or ^H/L1002P mice with tumour incidence as a co-primary endpoint.
