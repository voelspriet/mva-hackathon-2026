# Round 2: paediatric oncologist, objections and revision

## (a) Objections

**Clinical geneticist.** Top candidate, potassium citrate with hydrochlorothiazide gated on documented hypercalciuria: rank 1 contradicts rank 2, which is "deferred pharmacotherapy pending three assays". He starts alkali before the work-up he demands of every other entry. If the lesion is a post-ifosfamide proximal tubulopathy with bicarbonate wasting (02 §3), citrate at 0.1-0.2 g/kg/day will not hold the bicarbonate; if it is hypercalciuria with normal acidification, the thiazide he defers is the active component. The gate belongs before both drugs. Second claim: his exclusion of "vinca and dactinomycin inside RMS treatment" as "an oncology decision, not a repurposing candidate" removes from scope the drug class this child is most likely to receive next, and the only one where the checkpoint defect has direct mechanistic bearing (Sudo 2004, PMID 15059905; Janssen 2009, PMID 19855003). That is a scope ruling presented as an evidence judgement.

**Mitosis cell biologist.** Top candidate, intermittent low-dose dasatinib: the claimed "dosing scaffold" from the paediatric Ph+ CML approval is continuous daily weight-banded dosing (02 §5). It supplies a tolerated continuous dose and nothing about the hit-and-run exposure of the Zhu 2015 and Xu 2018 mouse designs (01 §4.2). His own strongest objection decides it for me: mis-segregating cells arrest with senescence features and pro-inflammatory signalling that recruits immune clearance (Santaguida 2017, PMID 28633018), p19^Arf ablation worsened the mouse (Baker 2008, PMID 18516091), and Baker 2011 never measured tumour incidence. In a child who has already had one embryonal tumour, that is not a residual doubt. Second claim: he sets "aneuploid fraction by single-cell DNA-seq in blood" as the hydroxychloroquine readout, then notes at the end of his file that no clone exceeds about 10% in blood (archive/PROMPT_track2.md, Facts). That readout has no target in the only accessible compartment, which should move the candidate down rather than appear as an afterthought.

**Clinical pharmacologist.** Top candidate, hydroxychloroquine with a prespecified 30% fall in micronuclei per 1,000 binucleated lymphocytes: no baseline micronucleus or PCS rate exists for this child, and if the baseline sits near the normal range that threshold falls inside OECD TG 487 scoring variance. The endpoint has to be set after his three baselines, not before. Second claim: he excludes everolimus partly on "lost exposure with enzyme-inducing antiepileptics, which 45% of MVA1 children may need". The 45% is the seizure frequency (Pavone 2022, PMID 35804254; 02 §1), not the proportion on inducers, and paediatric first-line practice is largely non-inducing. The stomatitis and immunosuppression arguments carry that exclusion on their own.

**Family representative.** Top candidate, thiazide plus citrate, ranked first for "zero extra visits": initiation requires serum potassium and bicarbonate after starting and after each increment (02 §3), which is phlebotomy outside the three-monthly ultrasound, and volume depletion in a child with failure to thrive is a daily-life harm of the kind her own criteria weight heavily. The entry is right; the stated reason for ranking it first is not. Second claim: her refusal rule, "refuse any label carrying growth, immunity or neoplasm language", excludes vincristine, dactinomycin, cyclophosphamide and dexrazoxane, that is, the regimen that treated this child's rhabdomyosarcoma and the agents that would treat a relapse. A rule that cannot be applied to curative therapy is too blunt to rank repurposing candidates against it.

## (b) What changes my ranking, and the revised list

Two arguments move me. The unphased genotype plus the absence of any aneuploid clone above 10% in blood leave the aneuploidy-selective candidates without a demonstrated target in the compartment where I could measure response. And the cell biologist's protective-senescence objection, made against his own rank 1, defeats dasatinib here.

1. **Potassium citrate, with hydrochlorothiazide added only after urine chemistry.** Changed: gated on a work-up (urine calcium/creatinine, citrate, pH, tubular phosphate and bicarbonate) rather than started empirically.
2. **Cyclophosphamide with mesna in place of ifosfamide, if therapy is needed again.** Up from 5: three colleagues put RMS chemotherapy out of scope, which leaves the decision with the highest probability of changing this child's outcome unowned by the panel.
3. **Dexrazoxane, conditional on an anthracycline.** Down from 2, on the family representative's test: it buys nothing unless relapse occurs.
4. **Metformin.** Rank held, now conditional on a measured baseline micronucleus or PCS fraction; without one it has neither target nor readout.
5. **Hydroxychloroquine.** Rank held, conditional on the child being able to cooperate with fields and OCT, given developmental delay in 77% of MVA1 (02 §1).
6. **Dasatinib: removed to the excluded list.** Growth-suppression label plus unestablished paediatric senolytic dose plus the possibility that p16-positive arrest is restraining karyotypically complex cells.

## (c) Factual error in a round-1 file

The geneticist excludes sirolimus as "same mechanism as everolimus without the paediatric formulation". Sirolimus has an oral solution and an established paediatric evidence base, including a prospective paediatric trial in complicated vascular anomalies (Adams 2016, PMID 26783326; 02 §5 and §7). The correct grounds for excluding it are mechanistic and toxicological, not formulation. Separately, the pharmacologist's statement that the dasatinib powder for oral suspension was "approved alongside the tablet in November 2017" is not supported by dossier 02 §5, which records only the 9 Nov 2017 weight-banded tablet approval; I did not run a web check and flag it as unverified rather than wrong.

## (d) The disagreement the moderator must not paper over

Whether this child's next course of chemotherapy is inside the panel's remit, and whether the checkpoint defect should change it. Three colleagues ruled vinca alkaloids and dactinomycin out of scope; I rank a regimen modification second. The substance is directional: SAC suppression produces taxane resistance in tumour cells (Sudo 2004), while partial BubR1 reduction amplifies segregation errors under low-dose taxol (Janssen 2009), so the same defect predicts both reduced tumour killing and increased damage to normal tissue, and neither has been measured in patient material (01 §7). RMS 2005 maintenance is six months of vinorelbine in a child with a constitutional spindle defect, against a 12.6-point overall survival difference (Bisogno 2019, PMID 31562043). Recording this as outside scope hides the question on which this child's survival most plausibly turns.
