## 2. Overreach

Section 6(a)2: "Decides phase, allele-specific expression and NMD efficiency together." The same paragraph states the failure mode in which the assay silently becomes a one-answer test. Required: "is designed to return phase, allele-specific expression and NMD efficiency together; without a working NMD-block arm or long-read genomic phase it returns phase only".

Section 6(a) prints eight numbered investigations with no line saying who is asked, who consents, and which need tissue not otherwise being taken. Items 3 and 8 need skin or tissue; items 1, 3, 5 and 7 make both parents research subjects. Required preamble: "These are proposed investigations, not scheduled ones. Items 3 and 8 need a sample taken for this purpose. Items 1, 3, 5 and 7 ask both parents to give samples as research participants, with their own consent and their own plan for what they are told."

Section 10: "It transfers to another chromosomal instability disorder." The transfer has not been run. Required: "is designed to transfer".

## 3. Omission

**The consent architecture is gone.** My round 1 section "What a family needs to hear before a monitored n-of-1" survives nowhere: off-label disclosure, information far enough ahead to weigh (N1), the withdrawal period consented to before it starts, the pre-filed negative control, publication of negatives, who prescribes, who pays, what happens to access if something appears to help. It matters because 6(a) will be read as a schedule and 6(c) as a shortlist, and neither says who decides.

**The cis result is a re-diagnosis.** Round4 6(a)2 carries the assay's technical failure mode and drops the governance consequence from my round 2 (e)(2): a cis result unsettles an explanation the family has absorbed and changes recurrence information for both parents, so it needs pre-test counselling of the diagnostic kind with all three answers named in advance, not a research consent line.

**Visit load.** Section 7 keeps the family-collected endpoints but nowhere states that the eight-item gate plus a crossover of about 11 months is a burden to be counted, or that draws should be keyed to the surveillance visits already scheduled.

## 4. The verified facts

**Metformin.** V1 moves me against it. I held it at rank 6 as a pre-gated hypothesis on the Pallister-Killian precedent. V1 shows it was tested at 0.5 and 1.0 mM only, two trisomies, 72 hours, called "subtle" by its authors, with no IC50, no human cell line and no in vivo arm; against plasma of 7.7 to 15.5 micromolar the gap is 32 to 130 fold, wider than the round-2 argument I recorded. It stays a named experiment and leaves my ranked list.

**731X.** V1 §2a corrects me too, not only the four files named. My round 1 rejected creative A1 on two grounds, therapeutic index and "the truncate drops the kinase domain anyway". The second is wrong: 731X rescues checkpoint, alignment and segregation and runs at 1.44 times wild type once NMD is bypassed. Round4 6(d) states the surviving reason correctly. My ranking does not move, since narrow index was always the operative reason, but the record should show the correction reaches my file.

## 5. Sign-off

Section 6 fairly represents my position on the rule, the carve-out, the work-up gate and the endpoints. Three fixes and one dissent.

**Section 8 against what I wrote.** Two of my seven replacements are missing and one is truncated. Missing: the nephrology and my own round-1 wording, "the only candidate with a paediatric licence, a dose, a readout inside six weeks, and an effect on a lesion the child has", which must be printed with its replacement "and a measured effect on urinary calcium in other conditions. Whether it changes this child's kidney findings is not known." That one matters most, because the item it describes is ranked 2 in section 6(c). Also missing: "Four additions cost no extra visit", replacement "Four additions can be attached to visits already scheduled, if the family agrees to a longer scan and to the same blood sample being used for more tests." And the metformin replacement now prints "far higher than a child's blood level" where I wrote a number; after V1 the number has changed, so print "32 to 130 times higher than a child's blood level".

**New in round4 and needing rewording.** Section 6(c)2, "Tubular work-up, then potassium citrate; thiazide only on documented hypercalciuria; amiloride as escalation", is a sequenced prescribing instruction. The "hypothesis for follow-up" label in the preamble does not reach it, because a reader takes the line, not the preamble. Add at the end of the line: "Sequence and agents for the renal team to consider; not a prescription."

**The lay summary requirement is not strong enough.** Section 8 forbids writing the cancer risk as a range and section 10 records that no approved drug has a measured effect on cancer incidence in this disease, but nothing requires the lay summary to say so where a family will read it. Required, printed in the lay summary and not in a later section: "Nothing in this report is expected to reduce this child's risk of cancer, and no test here can show whether it has. Three-monthly surveillance remains the only thing that acts on that risk."

**Dissent, to be printed in these words.** "Metformin should not appear in the ranked list. The only concentrations at which it did anything are 32 to 130 times a child's blood level, and its own authors called the effect subtle; printing a cheap, licensed, familiar drug at rank 6 of 7 puts it in front of families as a candidate when it is an experiment, and it belongs in the named-experiments list with the others."
