# Round 2, patient-family representative

## (a) Objections

**Clinical geneticist.** Top candidate, potassium citrate plus conditional HCTZ: I agree with the entry and object to the reasoning attached to it. He calls it "standard paediatric nephrology any competent team would already have started". A parent reads that as a finding about their own team. If the work-up has not been done, say what is missing and to whom the question goes. Second claim, his entry 2, "Deferred pharmacotherapy pending three assays", with harm listed as "delay, plus a blood draw or biopsy". Open-ended deferral is what the diagnostic odyssey already was, described publicly as years in which paediatricians "were unable to diagnose" (03 §2, S2). A skin biopsy in a small child is not a line item. Adopt the deferral with a date and a named person responsible for each assay.

**Mitosis cell biologist.** Top candidate, dasatinib at rank 1, justified "because the target, not the drug, has model-level causal support". He then writes his own refutation: clearing p16-positive cells "may remove a brake on the karyotypically complex cells that matter", and "Baker 2011 did not test tumour incidence". A family whose child has had cancer cannot accept an agent whose principal proponent states it may raise cancer risk by an untested mechanism. That objection belongs in the rank, not below it. Second claim, pimitespib at rank 5: approved only in Japan, only for advanced GIST, no paediatric data. A candidate a family cannot obtain is not a candidate, and listing it consumes attention.

**Paediatric oncologist.** Top candidate, HCTZ plus citrate, ranked for "a direct effect on whether this child can be treated if the sarcoma returns". This makes the kidney an instrument for future chemotherapy. Nephrocalcinosis and renal support are what the leaflet lists as present care (03 §1, M6); treat it because it is happening. Second claim, the maintenance note: "I would not de-escalate on theory against a 12.6-point overall survival gap, but I would document toxicity prospectively and treat this child as the index case." I accept the first half. Index-case status is a research role and requires consent, not a line in a panel document.

**Clinical pharmacologist.** Top candidate, hydroxychloroquine first, while stating that retinopathy screening "needs cooperation with fields and OCT, which developmental delay can make impossible". An irreversible harm that cannot be monitored fails the acceptable-risk line I set in round 1. Second claim, the n-of-1 protocol: 8 weeks of baseline, 24 weeks of dosing, withdrawal, then a 12-week re-challenge, with micronuclei as the primary endpoint. That is close to a year, re-challenge needs fresh consent, and no endpoint in it is visible to a parent.

## (b) What changes my ranking

Three arguments move me. The cell biologist's protective-senescence objection removes dasatinib from my list rather than demoting it. The observation that no aneuploid clone was detected above about 10 percent of cells means the aneuploidy-selective candidates may have no target in the compartment being measured, so metformin and hydroxychloroquine both fall. The oncologist's relapse-conditional entries touch outcomes families name, less toxic treatment and organ preservation, so they enter.

Revised list:
1. Hydrochlorothiazide plus potassium citrate, after a urine chemistry work-up. Unchanged; all four scientists rank it in the top six.
2. Measurement before prescribing: BubR1 immunoblot, PCS and micronucleus rate, parental phasing, with a fixed date. Adopted from the geneticist, with a deadline attached.
3. Cyclophosphamide with mesna in place of ifosfamide, if relapse. New; it protects the kidney and keeps the maintenance that carried overall survival from 72.4 to 85.0 percent.
4. Dexrazoxane, if an anthracycline is used. New; BubR1 mice die of cardiac failure after every other outcome improves.
5. Metformin. Down from 2, on the missing aneuploid target.
6. Hydroxychloroquine. Down from 3, and conditional on eye monitoring being achievable in this child.
Dasatinib and the mTOR inhibitors are removed.

## (c) Wording a family would read as a promise or a prescription

- Pharmacologist: "Hydroxychloroquine 5 mg/kg/day actual body weight, once daily, extemporaneous suspension, no titration, 24 weeks". This is a complete prescription. Require: "Illustrative design only. Not to be initiated outside an approved, registered protocol under the treating team."
- Cell biologist: "Rank 1 because the target, not the drug, has model-level causal support in *Bub1b* itself." Require the qualifier in the same sentence: no senolytic drug has been given to a BubR1 mouse, and tumour incidence was never measured.
- Oncologist: "treat this child as the index case". Require: "with the family's consent to prospective toxicity documentation".
- Geneticist: "any competent team would already have started". Require: "if this work-up has not yet been done, it is the first question for the renal team".

## (d) The disagreement the moderator must not paper over

Senolytic therapy. The four scientists place dasatinib at ranks 1, 4, 5 and 6, and I remove it. The spread is not a difference of confidence in one direction; it is a split over whether senescence in this child is the disease or a brake on it, and no experiment in the dossiers settles it, since Baker 2011 improved muscle and fat without measuring tumours. Averaging four ranks into moderate support would produce a middling recommendation that neither camp holds. Record it as unresolved, and record the experiment that would resolve it: a senolytic given to BubR1 mice with tumour incidence as a co-primary endpoint.
