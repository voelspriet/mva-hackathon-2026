## 1. Lay summary

This child has two changes in one gene, *BUB1B*. It makes BubR1, the protein that checks chromosomes are shared out correctly when a cell divides. Too little BubR1 and cells end up with the wrong number of chromosomes (Baker 2004, PMID 15208629). That is mosaic variegated aneuploidy, and it is why children with this diagnosis are watched for cancer (Kajii 2001, PMID 11746029).

No approved medicine acts on BubR1. Every medicine below acts on something downstream. No repurposed medicine has ever been given to a person with this syndrome for the syndrome, and no case report of one exists (creative G §7).

**Nothing in this report is expected to reduce this child's risk of cancer, and no test here can show whether it has. Three-monthly surveillance is the only part of current care that is aimed at that risk, although surveillance in this syndrome rests on expert consensus and has not been shown to change how many children die (Nakano 2024, PMID 39264246, which states that the rarity of the syndrome hampers accurate risk assessment and reports no mortality outcome for surveillance).**

What the work produces, most useful first: a written list of ordinary medicines to avoid or substitute, because they damage chromosome separation at the step the gene already fails, with a carve-out so it can never be read as a reason to change cancer treatment; four additions to existing surveillance that cost nothing; a kidney work-up before any kidney medicine, because two causes are possible and need opposite treatments; and measurements that would show whether the gene-directed ideas have a target in him at all. It has not been shown that the two changes sit on different copies of the gene, and nobody has ever measured how much BubR1 a person with this syndrome has. The second of the two changes is a variant of uncertain significance, so the diagnosis itself is provisional on that ground as well.

We do not know whether the target is present in him.

## 2. Mechanism: shown and inferred

BubR1 is a spindle assembly checkpoint protein; biallelic *BUB1B* mutation causes MVA1 (shown; Hanks 2004, PMID 15475955; OMIM 257300). Patient cells show impaired checkpoint, misalignment and low BubR1, rescued by ectopic BubR1; the model is quantitative, instability following low BubR1 abundance (shown; Suijkerbuijk 2010, PMID 20516114).

**Allele 1, p.Leu737Ter.** Codon 737 is TTA at c.2209 to c.2211, so c.2210T>G gives **TGA**, with **G at minus 1 and A at plus 4** (computed from ENST00000287598). Two separate statements, which the earlier draft ran together as if they were cumulative (corrected per V3 §6):

- **Readthrough level.** UGA gives the highest readthrough of the three nonsense codons. Floquet 2012 (PMID 22479203) establishes this statistically across 66 contexts, UGA greater than UAG greater than UAA for both basal and gentamicin-induced readthrough; Welch 2007 (PMID 17450125) observes the same ranking for PTC124-induced readthrough and attributes it to previously established termination efficiencies. The Floquet full text contains no word beginning "leak"; the Welch full text is paywalled and was not reached, so that half is unchecked. The word is dropped here either way.
- **Drug response.** Stop-codon identity does not carry over to responsiveness. Floquet states it directly: the identity of the stop codon did not affect the response to gentamicin treatment, with the factor of increase equal across UGA, UAG and UAA. The determinant is nucleotide context, U at minus 1 and C at plus 4, and this allele has G at minus 1 and A at plus 4.

Inference: a codon that supports readthrough at the level end, in a context that predicts a poor drug response, single-digit percent at best. Welch's compound also came out of a screen that was UGA-based from the outset, so a UGA bias in that molecule is partly built into its discovery.

The stop sits in exon 17 of 23, 747 nucleotides upstream of the last exon junction, with six downstream junctions (computed from NM_001211.6), so the 50 to 55 nucleotide rule **predicts** nonsense-mediated decay (shown for the rule; Nagy and Maquat 1998, PMID 9644970). That is a prediction, not a measurement on this allele. Indirect support: the 386X patient transcript was undetectable, and in a 731X/wild-type heterozygous parent total *BUB1B* mRNA sat at about half of healthy control, which the paper states as a two-fold reduction and which is read as 0.51 off its Figure 3A, one allele's worth and is what the authors read as complete absence of the mutant transcript rather than as partial survival of it. Both truncated proteins are expressed once NMD is bypassed by plasmid-driven expression (Suijkerbuijk 2010). No rare variant in the NMD machinery or EJC core exists in this genome (creative D §6), so NMD efficiency should be assumed normal. That last step is an assumption from absence of rare coding variation, not a functional measurement, and is marked as such.

**A correction, and a further correction found in revision.**

The truncation 731X was first characterised in Cancer Res 2010 Figures 5A to 5C (V1 §2a, upheld at V3 §1a): it rescues checkpoint, alignment and unperturbed segregation. Dev Cell 2012 Figures 3A to 3C independently retest 731X and report the same rescue, so 2012 is a second characterisation rather than no source at all. Figure 4 of the same paper covers the low-abundance mutants, 194X, 753X and the five kinase-domain substitutions, and 731X is not among them. Four panel files had rejected transcript stabilisation because a stabilised transcript "still stops at 737 and yields no kinase domain"; that ground is wrong.

**Expression level, citation corrected.** The figure **1.44 times wild type** is from **Suijkerbuijk 2012, Dev Cell, Figure 3D, U2OS cells**, not from Cancer Res 2010. The 2010 paper independently quantifies the same construct at **2.05 (U2OS) and 3.70 (HeLa)** in its Figure 2D. All three values sit above wild type, which is the point being made, but they are not interchangeable. Access caveat, stated because it bears on checkability: the printed Dev Cell article is behind a bot wall, and Figure 3D was read from the author's open-access Utrecht thesis chapter of the same paper, not from the printed version.

**The 731X allele is not a nonsense allele. Found in revision, by neither panel nor any referee (V3 §1d, Appendix A2).** 731X is the splice-acceptor mutation **IVS16-2A>G, p.Ser714fsTer731**: 713 residues of genuine BUBR1 sequence followed by 17 frameshift-derived residues, 730 residues in total, since under HGVS p.Ser714fsTer731 makes Ser714 the first changed residue, about 81 kDa. It is not in Hanks 2004, and it is in neither ClinVar nor public LOVD; Suijkerbuijk 2010's supplement is its original description. Two things follow. It **still supports** the proposition that a BUBR1 protein lacking the kinase domain is functional when it is expressed, because that is what Figures 5A to 5C measure. It **does not support** any stop-codon readthrough argument for p.Leu737Ter, because a splice-acceptor frameshift allele has no premature stop codon to read through in the first place. Any use of 731X as a readthrough precedent is withdrawn here.

Remaining boundaries: no construct terminating at 737 was ever tested, the residue count is 714 rather than the paper's own parenthetical 715, and every result is transient replacement **in HeLa and U2OS, never in an organism**. So for allele 1 the therapeutic object is the transcript, not the fold, and no approved agent stabilises it at an acceptable therapeutic index.

**Allele 2, p.Asn1002Lys: class unknown.** Final exon, kinase domain, gnomAD 1 of 1,461,878. **ClinVar holds no record for this nucleotide, but a VUS exists for the same protein change by a different nucleotide**: NM_001211.6(BUB1B):c.3006T>A (p.Asn1002Lys), **VCV004600147**, submitted by Ambry Genetics, uncertain significance, last evaluated **19 September 2025**. The only other allele deposited at that base is the synonymous c.3006T>C, likely benign. No record exists for c.3006T>G. Absence from ClinVar is absence of a submission, not evidence of absence in the population. Position does not predict class here: Q921H sits in the kinase domain and was indistinguishable from wild type on protein level (Figure 2D) and on function (Figure 4D) (Suijkerbuijk 2010). Known kinase-domain MVA missense alleles act by destabilisation: I909T and R727C turn over about two-fold faster in patient lymphoblastoid lines (Figure 3B), while the HSP90-dependence and proteasome-dependence were measured for I909T and L1012P in U2OS (Figure 3C), and R727C lies near rather than inside the annotated kinase domain at 766 to 1050; forced overexpression of I909T and L1012P restored checkpoint function fully. N1002 is predicted partially exposed, 9.14 A from T1008 (creative D §4). Inference, untested: more likely a stability allele than a catalytic one, since human BubR1 catalysis is dispensable for error-free segregation (Suijkerbuijk 2012, PMID 22698286).

**A numbering coincidence, corrected (V3 §3).** Sieben 2020 states in its own text that "human L1012 corresponds to mouse L1002". The mouse BubR1-L1002P allele therefore models the **human p.Leu1012Pro** substitution. It is **not** a model of this child's p.Asn1002Lys, and the shared number 1002 is arithmetic, not homology: human N1002 is mouse N992. Every claim in earlier drafts that the BubR1-H/L1002P mouse gives functional evidence about a human N1002K variant is deleted rather than softened.

**ACMG codes for both alleles, stated so the classification is auditable.** Allele 1, c.2210T>G p.Leu737Ter: **PVS1** on the nonsense mechanism with NMD predicted, plus **PM2_Supporting**, written at supporting strength because ClinGen caps PM2 there and the project's own Exomiser output assigns PM2_Supporting. Allele 2, c.3006T>G p.Asn1002Lys: **PM2** and **at most PP3**, with **PM3 unavailable** precisely because phase is unproven. Against allele 1 sit 115 gnomAD v4 heterozygotes at AF 7.87e-5, which is worth stating alongside the code rather than instead of it.

**The number nobody has.** Residual BubR1 has never been published as a percentage of control for **any** MVA patient: only fold-changes (2 to 6 fold decreased) and ">50% decrease" (Matsuura 2006, PMID 16411201). The quoted 6 and 13 percent thresholds are a **HeLa shRNA titration**, not patient data; the best-quantified figure is murine, about 10 percent in BubR1^H/H (Sieben 2020, PMID 31738183). Every claim here about remaining function is inferred.

**Consequences measured in models.** BubR1 hypomorphs show aneuploidy, dwarfism, cataracts, fat loss and a short lifespan (Baker 2004, PMID 15208629, whose reachable record gives no median; published medians for BubR1^H/H from the same laboratory are 196 days in Sieben 2020 and 30 weeks in Wijshake 2012); p16^Ink4a and p19^Arf rise in muscle, fat and eye, p16 inactivation attenuating and p19Arf inactivation worsening (Baker 2008, PMID 18516091); genetic clearance of p16-positive cells attenuates sarcopenia, fat loss and cataract (Baker 2011). Sustained high BubR1 reduces aneuploidy and tumorigenesis "even in the presence of genetic alterations that strongly promote aneuplodization and cancer, such as oncogenic Ras", and extends lifespan (Baker 2013, PMID 23242215, verified V2 §2).

**Reading rule for the Track 1 mosaicism result.** It bounds a single clonal trisomy near 4 percent, a burden spread over five chromosomes hardly at all (about 21 percent total would be needed), and a clone confined to kidney or muscle not at all (creative F §2). The CIN biologist's sentence on this is printed in §10.

## 3. Method

Five dossiers were compiled first (mechanism and models, clinical care and the approved-drug landscape, family perspective from published text only, variant context, phenotype-driven ranking), each statement tagged shown or inferred. Two panels then worked on them blind to each other.

- **Team 1, five seats**: clinical geneticist, mitosis cell biologist, paediatric oncologist, clinical pharmacologist, family representative. Three rounds: ranked lists, cross-objections with revision, moderator's record.
- **A divergent round, ten files (creative A to J)**, written without access to the panel directory: allele/expression/segregation; consequence/tissue/schedule; analogies and trial design; computation on the VCF; a target-to-drug mapping tool; a quantitative appendix; open-source intelligence; nutrition; pharmacy; lateral and measurement-first.
- **Team 2, eight seats**: laboratory geneticist, CIN biologist, predisposition oncologist, nephrologist, endocrinologist, immunologist, PK pharmacologist, second family representative. Two rounds, the second with four compulsory cross-checks.
- **Two verification passes** on seven contested claims: full text for Tang 2011, Suijkerbuijk 2010, North 2014, Bakhoum 2018 and the EMA, MHRA and FDA records; abstracts for Suijkerbuijk 2012, Baker 2013, Wu 2023. Four settled positions changed.
- **A final objection round**: all eight remaining seats wrote against the integrated record, then signed off or dissented.
- **Three external referee reports** (clinical and molecular geneticist; paediatric oncologist and clinical trialist; clinical pharmacologist and methodologist), then **a third verification pass adjudicating every contested referee point against primary sources**. That pass overrides both the manuscript and any referee where they conflict. Every correction is applied here, and §13 records where a referee was mistaken and where this round found an error nobody else had.

**On agreement between the teams.** Both reached the same harm screen, the same gate-first procedure, the same rejection of micronucleus frequency as a primary endpoint, and the same wording rule. This is not independent convergence: **both teams read the same five dossiers**, so agreement on a point traceable to a dossier measures dossier agreement as much as expert agreement, and only team 2 saw the divergent round.

**Automated candidate identification.** `pipeline/track2_drugmap/drugmap.py` takes a gene and a mechanism list and returns a ranked, harm-screened table of approved drugs from Open Targets GraphQL, ChEMBL REST and the openFDA label API. For *BUB1B* it queried 110 targets (gene, 33 consequence genes, 76 interaction partners at STRING/IntAct 0.9 or above), returned 148 molecules and passed 29 through a three-way approval test, in 350 seconds. It surfaced six approved drugs the manual route missed (palbociclib, ribociclib, abemaciclib, trilaciclib, venetoclax, temsirolimus) with label evidence, including palbociclib's juvenile-rat cataracts, lens degeneration, kidney tubule vacuolation and abnormal growing incisor teeth. Its top hit is its own limitation: trilaciclib ranks first because the direction check compares drug action against a protein rather than against the phenotype, and a CDK4/6 inhibitor mimics p16, the effector in this gene's own mouse model. It also missed the taxane hazard, because TUBB is not a STRING partner at 0.9. It is a shortlist for a human.

**Mechanism characterisation.** Loss of function, quantitative not qualitative: allele 1 by predicted transcript loss, allele 2 by an unknown mechanism most plausibly destabilisation. Pathway: spindle assembly checkpoint and kinetochore-microtubule attachment. Consequences treated as separate druggable objects: mis-segregation rate, aneuploidy tolerance, senescence and p16, innate sensing and clearance, growth signalling.

**Effort.** One machine, about two days wall-clock.

**Generative AI.** Anthropic, Claude Code, Max plan, model-improvement sharing disabled, no output rating. Used to search public sources, write and run the tool and the quantitative scripts, write the panel and verification files, and draft this report. No patient-level data left the machine.

**Sources, all public**, no proprietary data, no contact with the family or the MVA Society: PubMed and PMC, OMIM, Orphanet, ClinVar, gnomAD v4.1, ClinicalTrials.gov API v2, EU CTR/CTIS, Open Targets, ChEMBL, openFDA and DailyMed, EMA EPARs, MHRA-approved SmPC text, Ensembl REST, published MVA Society and family text.

## 4. The measurement gate

**These are proposed investigations, not scheduled ones.** Items 3 and 9 need a sample taken for this purpose. Items 1, 2, 3, 5 and 8 make both parents research subjects. Ordered by yield per cost.

**1. Tubular panel, one visit.** The nephrologist's panel, as written:

> **Serum:** sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate (venous gas acceptable), albumin-corrected or ionised calcium, phosphate, magnesium, creatinine, cystatin C, alkaline phosphatase, urate, PTH, 25-OH-D.
> **Paired second-void urine:** calcium, citrate, protein, retinol binding protein or alpha-1-microglobulin, sodium, magnesium, urate and glucose, each as a ratio to urine creatinine, plus amino acids and pH on a fresh sample.
> **Derived:** tubular phosphate reabsorption (TmP/GFR), fractional excretion of calcium, phosphate, magnesium, urate and sodium, cystatin C eGFR.
> **Records:** neonatal furosemide and parenteral nutrition calcium and vitamin D exposure; cumulative ifosfamide in g/m2 and any platinum; every current product containing vitamin D or calcium, including growing-up milk.
> **Imaging:** baseline ultrasound reported for medullary versus cortical pattern and stone burden, anchoring the two-year trajectory.
> Acid loading is not part of this panel; if bicarbonate is low and urine pH will not fall below 5.3, the renal team decides, not this page.

It decides which cause operates, which alkali is right, whether a thiazide is indicated at all, and gives the baseline cystatin C eGFR from which renal thresholds are set, repeated before and during any renally cleared drug. Creatinine-based eGFR overestimates filtration at low muscle mass, so cystatin C, not creatinine, sets every threshold here (inferred).

**2. One long-amplicon RT-PCR with an NMD-block arm, and parental genotyping alongside.** Illustrative design: forward primer in exon 16 or the 5' half of exon 17, reverse in exon 23; cDNA product 950 to 1,150 bp across six junctions so genomic template cannot amplify; 500 consensus reads by single-molecule sequencing; both parents, three controls, a synthetic 1:1 template mix for amplification bias; block arm a paired lymphoblastoid culture, vehicle against cycloheximide 100 micrograms per mL for 4 to 6 hours. Under 1,000 EUR, 3 to 5 working days.

**The failure mode, stated correctly.** Efficient decay does **not** make trans look like cis. In trans the surviving transcript carries c.3006G, so cDNA shows c.3006G near 100 percent. In cis both changes sit on one molecule, decay removes both, and c.3006G falls near zero: opposite signatures. The real limit is that under efficient decay phase cannot be read off a single molecule and becomes an inference from allele fraction, which is why the block arm is mandatory. **Only the block arm needs living cells;** phase and allele-specific expression run from stabilised RNA in a PAXgene tube at a scheduled visit. **Parental genotyping at the two sites 10.9 kb apart runs alongside, not instead**: it is a segregation test immune to the artefact and detects a de novo allele. Run this before item 4. It decides whether the allele-directed hypotheses have a premise.

**3. Aneuploid fraction per tissue.** FISH or single-cell sequencing on blood, buccal cells and skin, plus a PCS karyotype, child and both parents. Carrier parents are an intermediate phenotype, not a normal reference: PCS runs 3.5 to 41.7 percent in carrier-parent lymphocytes (Kajii 2001, PMID 11746029). That paper reports no control group, so no control comparator is quoted here; an earlier draft carried an "under 2 percent in controls" figure that is not in it.

**4. BubR1 immunoblot** as a percentage of control against both parents and three controls: a figure that exists for no MVA patient.

**5. Cycloheximide chase and forced-expression rescue on p.Asn1002Lys**, Suijkerbuijk 2010 design. Decides its functional class.

**6. Cardiac baseline: ECG read for the J point, and echocardiography.** The gate item stands; its justification is corrected (V3 §4). The **J-point depression is North 2014 (PMID 24825348), in male 3-month-old BubR1^H/H mice, Figures 5E to 5G**, alongside a lengthened QTc and QRS, and it is the endpoint the SIRT2 transgene reversed. **Sieben 2020 contains no electrocardiography at all**; its cardiac finding is an isoproterenol challenge showing reduced stress tolerance in BubR1^H/L1002P mice, and that mouse models human L1012P, not this child's variant. Baker 2011 separately reports that senescent-cell clearance did not attenuate cardiac arrhythmias or arterial wall stiffening and did not substantially extend survival, and that cardiac failure is "presumably the main cause of death in BubR1 H/H mice (data not shown)", so the rescue reached the p16-dependent phenotypes only and the cause of death is presumed on data not shown. What the baseline is for: it sets the ceiling on any healthspan claim and it is the baseline for the QT problem in §6. What it is not: evidence that this child's genotype carries a cardiac phenotype.

**7. Endocrine gate.** IGF-1 and IGFBP-3 SDS on stable intake, bone age, thyroid function, morning cortisol, imaging if IGF-1 is low.

**8. Immune panel.** Interferon score (PMID 24183309), immunophenotype, immunoglobulins, vaccine titres, child and both parents. No immune measurement has been published in any MVA patient, so nothing here can confirm or rule out what families describe. The tests that would answer it are listed here.

**9. p16 in patient tissue.** Decides whether the senescence hypothesis has a human baseline.

**Already in hand, at zero cost** (creative D §6): *MT-RNR1* clean, *G6PD* normal (which is what permits the hydroxychloroquine hypothesis to be discussed at all), *CYP3A5* \*3/\*3 non-expressor, *DPYD* rs1801160 normal function. Add **UGT1A1 \*28 and \*6** before any irinotecan, **with a stated limitation: \*28 is a promoter TA-repeat, which short-read WGS calls unreliably**, so a negative call from the existing file is not a result and a targeted fragment-length or long-read assay is what actually settles it. The "free" description applies to scanner and visit time, not to this genotype.

### Consent architecture

Parents consent for the child. Both parents are separately **research subjects** for items 1, 2, 3, 5 and 8, with their own consent and their own plan for what they are told about their own results, carrier and PCS status included. **Items 3 and 9 need tissue taken for this purpose**; items 2, 4 and 5 can largely be attached to visits already scheduled, if the family agrees to a longer scan and to the same blood sample being used for more tests. **A cis result is a re-diagnosis, not a research finding**: the child would retain an intact allele, the explanation the family has absorbed would be unsettled, and recurrence information for both parents would change, so it needs pre-test counselling of the diagnostic kind with all three answers named in advance, prepared before the sample is taken. Before any monitored n-of-1: off-label status disclosed in writing, information far enough ahead to weigh, the withdrawal period consented to before it starts, the negative-control outcome pre-filed, publication of negatives committed in advance, and named answers to who prescribes, who pays, and what happens to access if something appears to help. Visit load is a cost to be counted, and draws should be keyed to visits already scheduled.

## 5. The avoidance rule with its carve-out

The first-ranked output is a rule, not a drug. **Hypothesis for follow-up.**

> **SCOPE.** Elective, non-oncological prescribing only.
>
> **DOES NOT APPLY TO any antineoplastic agent given with curative, maintenance, relapse, disease-control or palliative intent, nor to radiotherapy given as local therapy with any of those intents.** Do not reduce, omit, delay or substitute any agent in a rhabdomyosarcoma protocol, or in any other cancer treatment plan, and do not omit, delay or reduce planned radiotherapy, on the basis of this page. EpSSG RMS 2005 maintenance gives **5-year overall survival 86.5 percent (95 percent CI 80.2 to 90.9) against 73.7 percent (65.8 to 80.1), HR 0.52 (0.32 to 0.86), p=0.0097** (Bisogno 2019, PMID 31562043). That is the **secondary** endpoint. The **primary endpoint was disease-free survival, and it did not reach significance: 77.6 percent (70.6 to 83.2) against 69.8 percent (62.2 to 76.2), HR 0.68 (0.45 to 1.02), p=0.061.** No study has shown MVA or *BUB1B* patient cells to be hypersensitive to any spindle agent: the sensitisation data come from engineered knockdown tumour cells (Janssen 2009, PMID 19855003), which also reports that untransformed human fibroblasts with reduced Mps1 could not be sensitised to sublethal taxol, and the opposing data show *BUB1B* suppression producing taxane resistance (Sudo 2004, PMID 15059905). It also does not withhold a loop diuretic, corticosteroid, antibiotic or antiemetic given for an acute indication where no equivalent alternative exists, hyperhydration alongside alkylators included. Decisions on a cancer regimen, radiotherapy included, belong to the treating oncologist, the radiation oncologist and the tumour board.
>
> **APPLIES TO,** *for the treating team to consider,* **and to elective prescribing only:** colchicine, griseofulvin, albendazole, mebendazole, acetazolamide, topiramate, elective loop diuretics, high-dose intermittent vitamin D, somatotropin, mecasermin. Substitutes for the treating team to consider: for griseofulvin, terbinafine; for colchicine, an NSAID (**anakinra is withdrawn as a substitute here: it is a daily subcutaneous biologic for a different indication, not a colchicine equivalent**); for topiramate, levetiracetam; for an elective loop diuretic, a thiazide; for high-dose intermittent vitamin D, daily vitamin D to a measured 25-OH-D; for growth, the endocrine gate. **For albendazole and mebendazole the substitute is pyrantel embonate where it is licensed for the age and weight, a licensing status this report does not source and which has to be checked locally, with household hygiene measures; where no licensed equivalent exists for a young child, the bar is a reason to ask the treating team, not a reason to leave a treatable helminth infection untreated.**

The protecting clause states intent rather than listing drug names on purpose: a closed list protects only what it names and would leave vinblastine, topotecan, doxorubicin and etoposide unprotected by omission. It is repeated verbatim in §7. Basis: colchicine is a reference aneugen in the human lymphocyte micronucleus assay (**Ramirez, Mutagenesis 1997, PMID 9412992**; the earlier draft attributed this PMID to "Vanhauwaert 1998", which is the wrong source for the right content) and benzimidazoles bind the same tubulin site (inferred here, not sourced); carbonic anhydrase inhibition promotes hypercalciuria and stones, documented for topiramate in children with resolution on withdrawal (PMC5976810) and inferred for acetazolamide; high-dose intermittent vitamin D is associated with later nephrocalcinosis and hypercalciuria in a ten-patient series whose authors put it no higher than a suggestion (PMID 2386055), which is an association and not a demonstration of cause.

## 6. Ranked hypotheses for follow-up

**1. The avoidance rule with its carve-out (§5). Hypothesis for follow-up.** Tier: human genotoxicity and label data per agent, inference for transfer to this genotype. Condition: the carve-out printed in the same type size everywhere. Harm: over-application, meaning curative chemotherapy withheld. Readout: avoid-list agents prescribed per patient-year through the UK MVA National Audit, not individual compliance.

**2. Four surveillance additions, at no or near-zero cost. Hypothesis for follow-up.** Tier: human. This is the part of the answer that is not a refusal.

(a) ***UGT1A1* \*28 and \*6 before any irinotecan.** Irinotecan is a component of standard salvage regimens after prior alkylator exposure; no primary citation for that regimen statement is attached here. The caveat from §4 travels with the recommendation: **\*28 is a promoter TA-repeat and short-read WGS calls it unreliably**, so this is free only if a repeat-capable assay is not needed, and if it is, it is cheap rather than free. Rhabdomyosarcoma salvage also gives irinotecan at 20 to 50 mg/m2/day, the nearest reachable record in this project's source set being irinotecan 50 mg/m2/day on days 1 to 5 in a paediatric regimen (PMC9972017); the exposure threshold above which \*28 guidance ordinarily changes dosing carries no citation here, so the value of the call is a safety flag rather than a dose change.

(b) **Extend the three-monthly renal ultrasound to bladder wall and, in girls, vagina.** Schneider 2020 (PMID 33209717) states MVA rhabdomyosarcoma is "highly likely to occur in the GU tract, primarily in the vagina and bladder", embryonal with botryoid features, **on five case reports and opening "while data is limited"**; that qualifier was dropped in the earlier draft and is restored. No extra visit, no extra cost, and it is the tumour this child has already had.

(c) **Surveillance should not be assumed to stop at age 7, and here is what that means in practice.** The guideline horizon of renal ultrasound every three months from birth to age 7 (Nakano 2024, PMID 39264246) is a floor, not a ceiling. Proposed content, for the treating team to consider:
> **Modality:** abdominal and pelvic ultrasound, continuing the existing scan rather than adding one, extended as in (b).
> **Interval:** three-monthly to age 7 as per guideline; **six-monthly from 7 to about 18**; annually thereafter while the person remains in follow-up.
> **Starting age for a gastrointestinal arm:** not in childhood. Upper gastrointestinal endoscopy with duodenal and ampullary views, and colonoscopy, are the modalities that would address the adult phenotype, and the earliest defensible entry point is **early adult life, around age 25**, roughly a decade below the age at which the only described case presented, decided by an adult predisposition service rather than here.
> **The counterweight, stated in the same box:** this arm rests on a single patient, **no pooled survival analysis for MVA1 has been published, so how many MVA1 children reach the ages at which the adult phenotype appeared is not known**, and adding endoscopy to a childhood plan on that basis would be adding burden against an unquantified benefit.

The evidence and its limit: an adult-onset MVA1 phenotype exists, with ampullary adenocarcinoma at 34, later colon and gastric primaries, and PCS and MVA in patient cells (Rio Frio and Foulkes 2010, PMID 21190457). **That patient was homozygous for a leaky intronic allele, c.2386-11A>G, with adult gastrointestinal neoplasia; the reachable record does not state whether childhood features of the syndrome were present, so their absence is not asserted here. It is not this child's genotype**, and §12 criticises this report for reasoning across allele classes from a mouse hypomorph, so the same discipline is applied here: this is a reason not to write an end date into the plan, not a prediction about this child.

(d) **A full blood count, keyed to a visit that already happens.** MDS, AML and ALL are in the MVA1 malignancy spectrum (Nakano 2024; Pavone 2022, PMID 35804254), and no haematological surveillance appeared anywhere in the earlier draft. A count on an existing draw is free and is the cheapest addition in this report. Interval: with each three-monthly surveillance visit while venepuncture is already occurring, otherwise at whatever draws are already scheduled.

**(e) A rule about the imaging itself, not an addition to it.** Nakano 2024 states in its MVA surveillance section that "radiation exposure should be avoided". Its sensitivity statement is generic to the genomic instability disorders as a group and it reports no radiosensitivity measurement in MVA, so the avoidance line is the part that carries this rule. **Where a surveillance question can be answered by ultrasound or by non-contrast MRI, the radiation-based alternative should not be chosen by default, and cumulative diagnostic radiation should be recorded.** This does not touch radiotherapy given as local therapy for a tumour, which is inside the carve-out and belongs to the radiation oncologist; local control in rhabdomyosarcoma uses surgery, radiotherapy, or both (COG 2023 Blueprint, PMC10519430), and nothing here is a reason to omit or reduce it. Whether this child tolerates standard-dose cytotoxic therapy or radiotherapy differently from an unaffected child is not known and is not answered by this report.

**3. Tubular work-up, then the renal agents the panel indicates. Hypothesis for follow-up.** *Sequence and agents for the renal team to consider; not a prescription.* Tier: human, from other paediatric hypercalciuric disease (Seikaly 2001, PMID 11433085). This is the only entry whose lesion is documented in this child, although its mechanism is not, and it tests no *BUB1B* hypothesis; its proponents in both teams conceded that. It has a paediatric licence, a dose, a readout inside six weeks, and a measured effect on urinary calcium in other conditions. Whether it changes this child's kidney findings is not known.

> Hypercalciuria for age **with normocalcaemia** and normal TmP/GFR: thiazide plus potassium citrate, amiloride as escalation (the amiloride step is inferred; no source for it is cited anywhere in this project).
> Low TmP/GFR with low molecular weight proteinuria, glycosuria, aminoaciduria or bicarbonate wasting: alkali with phosphate and alfacalcidol first, thiazide as adjunct, by analogy with X-linked hypophosphataemia (PMID 11433085).
> Normal urine calcium with a resolving ultrasound: no drug.

Normocalcaemia matters because a thiazide raises serum calcium, which is standard pharmacology carried here without a primary citation, so hypercalciuria driven by hypercalcaemia is where it does harm. The second branch matters because ifosfamide proximal tubulopathy is one of the two competing causes; the avoid-list bar on high-dose intermittent vitamin D must not be read as barring alfacalcidol for a tubulopathy. **Dietary calcium continues, inside meals**, because restriction raises oxalate absorption and threatens bone (PMC3426770; Habbig, Beck and Hoppe, Kidney Int 2011;80:1278-91): milk in the porridge, yoghurt with the fruit, cheese sauce on the pasta. A family reading "no calcium" removes milk. Sodium is the lever instead, since urinary calcium tracks urinary sodium.

Citations for the other cause, corrected: **PMID 19460127 is the preterm risk-factor study identifying furosemide and carries no resolution figure.** Resolution comes from two cohorts: **75 percent of 14 very low birthweight preterm infants followed to age 5 to 7 years (PMID 16705005, which carries no 12-month or 24-month figure; the earlier draft's 70 percent and 90 percent are not in it)**, and **61 percent at 12 to 18 months (PMC9587072)**. **Neither reports an untreated arm; both are observational follow-up.** Harm: hypokalaemia, volume depletion, hypercalcaemia. Readout: urine calcium to creatinine at baseline and six weeks, with citrate, potassium and bicarbonate, and the ultrasound trajectory over two years as the outcome.

## 7. Explicitly not recommended, and why

**The carve-out is repeated here verbatim, because this is the section that will be photocopied:**

> **DOES NOT APPLY TO any antineoplastic agent given with curative, maintenance, relapse, disease-control or palliative intent, nor to radiotherapy given as local therapy with any of those intents.** Do not reduce, omit, delay or substitute any agent in a rhabdomyosarcoma protocol, or in any other cancer treatment plan, and do not omit, delay or reduce planned radiotherapy, on the basis of this page. Decisions on a cancer regimen, radiotherapy included, belong to the treating oncologist, the radiation oncologist and the tumour board.

**Metformin, as a prescription.** Tang 2011 tested it at **0.5 and 1.0 mM** in trisomy 13 and 16 MEFs at 72 hours: Ts13 73 plus or minus 2.9 against wild type 86 plus or minus 2.4, Ts16 72 plus or minus 2.4 against 82 plus or minus 2.1, both P<0.005, an effect the authors call "not as dramatic" and "subtle" (verified V1 §1b). **The access route, stated so a reader can check it**, because a referee reported the metformin numbers unreachable. Metformin is named in the main text twice, in Results ("also impaired the accumulation of trisomy 13 and 16 cells in culture, although the effects were not as dramatic") and in Discussion ("the effects of metformin on trisomic MEFs were subtle"), and is absent from the abstract. What the main text does not carry is the concentrations and the percentages, which sit in the supplement: **Table S1** and **Figure S1A**, whose x-axis reads "Metformin (mM)" with ticks at 0, 0.5 and 1.0. In the publisher supplement these are `1-s2.0-S0092867411000560-mmc1.pdf` page 1 and `mmc2.pdf` page 18; in the PMC author manuscript PMC3532042 they are `NIHMS276601-supplement-02.pdf` with the legend in `-supplement-01.pdf`, whose page positions were not re-checked. The panel lettering differs between the two versions, so any citation to a Tang 2011 supplemental panel should state which version it reads.

Paediatric plasma metformin is **7.7 to 15.5 micromolar**, converted from a 1 to 2 mg/L steady-state range that is an unsourced label-level estimate rather than a cited figure: a gap of **32 to 130 fold**. The RIOMET label puts steady-state plasma generally below 1 mg/L and reports paediatric Cmax and AUC within 5 percent of weight-matched adults, so the range used here is a peak rather than a steady state and, if anything, understates the gap. Metformin is not appreciably protein bound, distribution is transporter-driven, extended release lowers Cmax rather than raising it (uncited), and the ceiling is toxicological, since plasma above roughly 5 mg/L, about **39 micromolar** (5 mg/L over a molar mass of 129.16 g/mol is 38.7 micromolar), is the range associated with lactic acidosis, a threshold that carries no citation in this project, allowing at most a further 2.5 to 5 fold. **The number the earlier draft never printed: even at that toxic ceiling the residual gap is still 12.9 to 25.8 fold.** Two scope corrections to the earlier wording. "Cannot be closed by dose, salt, formulation or tissue" overreached on the last word: metformin is transporter-concentrated, and enterocyte and hepatic exposures are not plasma. **The claim is therefore scoped to the target that matters here, a fibroblast or lymphocyte, where no such concentrating transport is in evidence.** Renal cortical accumulation is a few fold rather than two orders, a magnitude for which no source is given and which does load-bearing work in scoping this exclusion. The paper reports no IC50, no dose above 1 mM, no human cell line and no in vivo arm. Metformin moves to §9 as an experiment. Two seats asked that it not appear in a ranked list at all; that is followed, and both dissents are printed in §10.

**Dasatinib is not a candidate at any rank.** The senolytic rationale rests on genetic clearance of p16-positive cells in a mouse, which is not a drug. Three objections fail **independently**. The tumour direction of removing senescent cells is not merely open: Baker 2008 reports that BubR1^H/H;p16^Ink4a-/- mice had significantly more tumours than BubR1^H/H mice (Figure 8a), eight of nine being lung adenocarcinomas, so genetic p16 removal raised tumour burden in this very mouse; what remains unresolved is whether a senolytic drug reproduces that (§9 question 8). TKI growth deceleration is prepubertal, on a retrospective height-SDS study of 48 children taking **imatinib**, not dasatinib, which reports no IGF-1 or IGFBP-3 measurement (PMID 21592517), so the normal-IGF-1 premise and the cartilage inference are not carried by that source and are withdrawn. And dasatinib blocks TCR and NK activating signalling within hours, suppressing the same clearance arm that may be restraining the aneuploid compartment, a claim that traces to working files and carries no primary citation here. There is no paediatric senolytic dose in any condition. A formulation asymmetry compounds it: the powder for oral suspension is authorised in the EU (X/0056/G, Commission Decision 2 July 2018) and **not** in the US, where every product on record is a tablet and the sub-10 kg exclusion has no alternative offered (verified V2 §5).

**Growth hormone and mecasermin outside the endocrine gate.** No MVA1 efficacy data; one MVA2 patient treated from age 6 with "only poor response", the paper's general statement being that MVA2 patients probably do not benefit (PMID 36588761); the earlier draft's "two patients, no response" is not what it reports; mecasermin carries a labelled contraindication with a malignancy history. Growth hormone should not be started without a documented growth hormone deficiency. If the tests in §4 show one, the decision returns to the family with the endocrine and oncology teams together, with the one-disease-free-year condition (Sklar 2018, PMID 29982476; the recommendation is not in the abstract and the full guideline is paywalled, so it is not verified here) in the discussion.

**Chaperone inducers, chemical chaperones including phenylbutyrate, and rapalogs.** This bar stands, on a distinction stated explicitly: **correction versus tolerance.** Raising BubR1 lowers the production of new mis-segregation events at source. Raising chaperone or HSF1 capacity is not shown to touch the mis-segregation rate, and no source cited here measures that rate under chaperone or HSF1 induction; what is documented is that HSF1 overexpression makes aneuploidy tolerable in human cells (Donnelly 2014, PMID 25205676). Sheltzer 2017 (PMID 28089890) reports that nearly all trisomic lines grew poorly in vitro and as xenografts and that prolonged growth brought further chromosomal alterations correlating with improved fitness, which the authors put no higher than a possible contribution to aggressive growth in complex karyotypes; no identified tolerance mechanism is claimed there. Rapalogs add immunosuppression against clearance, and proteinuria; the claim that aneuploid cells grow better under rapamycin has not been shown in human cells, but it is shown in mouse cells in a source already cited here, Tang 2011 Table S1, where rapamycin at 10 nM and torin1 at 250 nM both improved growth of trisomic MEFs relative to wild type.

**The BubR1-raising bar is withdrawn.** One seat's rule barred agents that raise BubR1, on the premise that raising it in every cell raises aneuploid-cell fitness. Verification defeats that premise: sustained high BubR1 **reduces** aneuploidy and tumorigenesis even against oncogenic Ras and extends lifespan (Baker 2013, verified V2 §2). Withdrawn on that half; standing on the tolerance half above.

**The expression-directed arm (niacin, acipimox), with its surviving reason for exclusion.** With the bar withdrawn this arm must be restored or excluded for a stated reason. It is excluded, for weaker but real reasons. SIRT2 deacetylates BubR1 at K668, blocking CBP-primed ubiquitylation and degradation (North 2014); a SIRT2 transgene on BubR1^H/H gave **+58 percent median and +21 percent maximal lifespan, P=0.0384, n=31 against 33, driven entirely by males at +123 percent (P=0.004), with no effect in females (P=0.7915)** (verified V2 §3c). The link from an approved NAD+ precursor to BubR1 in this genotype is untested: the NMN experiment was 500 mg/kg/day intraperitoneally for **seven days** in wild-type mice, clean only in testes, with no aneuploidy or lifespan endpoint. Wu 2023 (PMID 38009412) does not refute North 2014, it bounds it to the hypomorph, and its authors hold a commercial interest in the opposite result. Baker 2013 is a germline transgene at supra-normal level, which no approved drug reproduces. Acipimox raised ex vivo skeletal-muscle mitochondrial respiration and nuclear-encoded mitochondrial gene expression in 21 adults with type 2 diabetes over two weeks (PMID 25352640); the direct NAD+ effect in that work is in C2C12 myotubes, not in human muscle, and the no-paediatric-indication and flushing statements carry no citation here. Net: a marginal, male-driven rescue of a deficit in one progeroid model, joined to an approved drug by an untested link. Not a candidate; retained as the immunoblot experiment in §9. High-dose **nicotinamide** opposes this axis, inhibiting sirtuins by base exchange, a mechanism stated here without a citation.

## 8. Endpoints and their power limits

Arithmetic from creative F; script at `pipeline/track2_math/f_math.py`.

**Rejected as a primary endpoint: micronucleus frequency** for aneuploidy-selective agents, on two grounds both teams reached. It scores ongoing mis-segregation, while the published activity of the chloroquine class was selective killing of cells already aneuploid, so a fall is not predicted and its absence would falsify nothing. And with no baseline in this child, a pre-specified 30 percent fall may sit inside ordinary between-occasion variance, for which no human within-subject figure is cited here; OECD TG 487 is an in vitro guideline for chemical testing and is not a source for it. It stays as a baseline and a stopping rule.

**Power, with the variance assumption stated for each figure rather than left implicit.** Three referees independently could not reproduce these numbers, and they were right that the three did not share a variance model. The model behind all three is the same formula, a two-sided 5 percent test at 80 percent power on two period means of three measurements, detectable relative change = 2.8 x CV x sqrt(2/3). What differs, and what was never stated, is where the CV comes from.

| Endpoint | CV used | Where that CV comes from | Figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interphase FISH, 1,000 nuclei at fraction 0.10 | 10.7 percent | 9.49 percent binomial counting error combined with a 5 percent hybridisation and scorer term. **Biological variance set to zero.** On counting error alone the figure would be 20.0 percent. | 22 percent |
| Micronuclei, 2,000 binucleated cells | 22.9 percent | An 11.2 percent counting term at an **assumed** elevated MVA baseline of 40 per 1,000, which this child has no measurement for, combined with an **assumed biological CV of 20 percent**. Not a counting-error-only bound. | 41 percent |
| Micronucleated reticulocytes, 1e6 cells scored | 20.1 percent | **Not counting error.** The scoring CV of 0.022 alone gives 5.2 percent. The 37.5 percent figure carries the same **assumed biological CV of 20 percent** as the row above, a term the FISH row omits entirely. | 37.5 percent |

**What follows, stated plainly.** Only the FISH row is a measurement-error bound, and even it carries a technical term on top of the counting error; it is the best case an assay can achieve if nothing in the child varies between measurements, and it should be read as a floor rather than as a detection limit. The other two rows are not on the same footing, because each carries an assumed biological CV of 20 percent. Putting that same 20 percent biological term into the FISH row takes its total CV to 22.7 percent and moves 22 percent to 41 percent. An earlier draft said 43 percent, and said that switching from a normal approximation to t critical values would move the FISH row into a 29 to 42 percent band; both are withdrawn. The model already uses t critical values at six degrees of freedom, and replacing them with normal quantiles moves the figure down to 19 percent, so the t correction is worth about three percentage points, not a band.

**And the constraint that binds everything above: within-patient variance of these endpoints has never been measured in this disease** (it is §9 question 10, this report's own open question). A power calculation whose variance term is unmeasured cannot bind a design. **No design in this report is bound by these numbers**; they are order-of-magnitude bounds that say which endpoints are plausibly in reach, and question 10 has to be answered before any of them becomes a sample size. On the same footing, the earlier claim that "the three-per-period design, not the assay, binds" is withdrawn: on this model the comparison turns on whether the 5 percent technical term is kept. With it, 4,000 nuclei gives 15.0 percent and six measurements give 14.8 percent, which is a tie. Without it, 4,000 nuclei gives 10.6 percent against 13.2 percent for six measurements, and the assay does bind harder. The earlier figures of 10.9 and 15.4 percent mixed the two models and are withdrawn.

Periods follow pool turnover: about 12 weeks plus a 4-week washout for lymphocyte endpoints, roughly 11 months in total, or 4-week periods on a reticulocyte readout. A parallel randomised design is out of reach below about a 50 percent effect. **The aggregated crossover figure is withdrawn as a claim of feasibility.** The arithmetic gives 17 subjects for a 20 percent change only under an assumed within-subject CV of 22.9 percent, which is the value the appendix uses, where the FISH row assumes a total CV of 10.7 percent; both cannot stand. Seventeen is also not "inside" an enrollable population of roughly 15 to 20, it exceeds the lower bound, and it would require near-complete enrolment of survivors with no dropout, one central scoring laboratory and eleven months per participant. It is retained in §9 as a direction, not as a sample size. Also unstated in the earlier draft and stated now: the mosaicism reading of "10 percent of cells" to "near 4 percent" rests on a model not shown, and "about 21 percent total across five chromosomes" assumes five independent equal clones. A later bin-by-bin re-run adds a third qualification: these floors are genome averages and do not hold in heterochromatin, acrocentric short arms, centromeres or the MHC, where the statistic returns implied cell fractions above 1.0 and has therefore left its domain (`track2/reanalysis/R6_segmental_bins.md`).

**Decision thresholds, presented across both cancer-risk estimates instead of silently adopting one.** The timing correction dominates: a child already treated for rhabdomyosarcoma faces a residual, not a lifetime, risk, and every threshold multiplies by 1/s where s is the residual share. The two published risk figures are unreconciled and probably ascertainment-driven: **38.7 percent** (Pavone 2022, PMID 35804254) and **about 75 percent** (Orphanet ORPHA 1052, whose record could not be retrieved on re-checking, so the second row rests on a figure this report has not verified). The earlier draft declined to choose between them and then printed a threshold that depended silently on the choice. Both are printed here, at s = 0.2 and added harm 5 percent:

| Cancer-risk figure used | Required relative reduction |
|---|---|
| 38.7 percent (Pavone 2022) | 0.05 / (0.2 x 0.387) = **64.6 percent** |
| about 75 percent (Orphanet) | 0.05 / (0.2 x 0.75) = **33.3 percent** |

The earlier "exceeds 60 percent" was the first row only. **Two caveats on the inputs, not on the arithmetic.** Pavone's 38.7 percent is 12 of 31 published MVA1 cases with malignancy at the time of report, a proportion of an ascertained series and not a lifetime risk; the earlier draft named ascertainment while omitting censoring, which pushes the other way. And **s = 0.2 has no source.** It was asserted, it is not published anywhere for this disease, and §9 question 12 concedes that residual risk for a treated MVA1 child is unpublished. It is retained here **only as a declared placeholder to show the shape of the calculation**, and every threshold in the table is void until question 12 is answered. Since no approved drug has a measured effect on cancer incidence in MVA, every figure here is a requirement, not an estimate.

**Endpoints by entry.** Renal as in §6; aneuploidy-selective entries FISH or single-cell sequencing per tissue; nutrition MUAC z-score and lean mass index; immunology titres and infection days; relapse entries tubular handling, strain and troponin. Growth safety, if any agent with a growth signal is ever considered: knemometry rather than height velocity and bone age at 6 and 12 months. The resolution and interval figures the earlier draft attached to PMID 26854116 are not in that paper, which is a field study reporting a technical error of 0.18 mm, so they are withdrawn pending a correct source. Family-collected endpoints kept: buccal micronucleus cytome posted and blind-scored, home urine calcium to creatinine strips, grip strength and a timed five-rise test, a three-day diary. Rejected: parents dosed in vivo as a comparison arm, and whole-body MRI, since no validated imaging surveillance protocol for rhabdomyosarcoma in MVA exists; Nakano 2024 recommends renal ultrasound every three months from birth to age 7 and clinical review of systems rather than a defined imaging interval for rhabdomyosarcoma.

## 9. Unresolved questions, each with the experiment that settles it

1. **Phase, allele-specific expression, NMD efficiency.** The paired long-amplicon RT-PCR with cycloheximide arm **plus parental genotyping**. Under 1,000 EUR, 3 to 5 working days. Holder: the referring clinical genetics laboratory.
2. **Residual BubR1 as a percentage of control.** Immunoblot against both parents and three controls. It would also test whether a normal transcript level at c.2210 puts the pathogenicity of the nonsense allele itself in question, given 115 gnomAD heterozygotes and no published MVA patient carrying it.
3. **The functional class of p.Asn1002Lys.** Cycloheximide chase and forced-expression rescue. Holder: a mitosis laboratory with the shRNA replacement system.
4. **Whether an aneuploidy-selective target exists in him.** FISH or single-cell sequencing per tissue, child and both parents.
5. **Metformin: does it select against aneuploid cells at a reachable exposure?** Concentration-response in patient or Pallister-Killian fibroblasts over three to four passages. **The ladder must include a 1.0 mM metformin arm as an internal positive control alongside AICAR**, because the only concentrations at which anything was seen are 0.5 and 1.0 mM, and a ladder topping out below them cannot separate "does not work at reachable exposure" from "the assay failed". **Pre-declare before the run: a null below 500 micromolar is uninformative, does not refute the mechanism, and must not be reported as reassurance.** If the 500 micromolar and control arms do not clear a pre-filed threshold, the arm closes.
6. **Are this child's cells more or less sensitive to vinca alkaloids?** Micronucleus frequency and apoptotic fraction under vincristine and vinorelbine at clinically achievable concentrations, in stimulated lymphocytes and fibroblasts, against both parents and three controls, blind, threshold pre-declared. Janssen 2009 predicts amplified errors, Sudo 2004 reduced killing, and no MVA cell has been tested. Whichever way it comes out, the carve-out stands until a clinical trial says otherwise.
7. **Tolerance or correction?** Patient fibroblasts with and without a chaperone inducer, immunoblot and FISH at days 0 and 14. Tolerance predicts BubR1 unchanged with aneuploid fraction stable or rising; correction predicts BubR1 up and aneuploid fraction down. Precondition: question 3 first.
8. **Is senescence the lesion or the brake?** A senolytic given **as a drug** to BubR1^H/H or BubR1^H/L1002P mice, tumour incidence co-primary, bone length secondary. Never done. It is why dasatinib appears here rather than not at all, and settling it in favour of senolysis would still not make dasatinib a candidate (§10).
9. **Is immune clearance rate-limiting?** Interferon score and NK cytotoxicity in patients and carriers. The literature was misread in both directions and corrected: Bakhoum 2018 (PMID 29342134) shows chronic cGAS-STING signalling in chromosomally unstable **tumour** cells is pro-metastatic and is not an immune-clearance paper; clearance of **untransformed** aneuploid cells stands on Santaguida 2017 (PMID 28633018) and Wang 2021 (PMID 34105235).
10. **Within-patient variance of every endpoint over 12 untreated months.** Without it no endpoint is known to be measurable in this disease.
11. **A shared aggregated N-of-1 protocol across the roughly 40 published cases (Scott 2006, PMID 16690728; Orphanet ORPHA 1052), which is a count of cases published by those dates rather than of living patients.** The earlier "17 subjects for a 20 percent change" is withdrawn as a sample size for the reasons in §8, and the size cannot be recomputed until question 10 supplies a measured within-patient variance. The direction stands, the number does not. A cell-line panel plus a few-hundred-compound screen sizes inside one year of the MVA Society's fundraising (GBP 325k target for 2026). Holders: the Society and the GOSH conference network.
12. **A per-genotype embryonal-tumour fraction, and a residual risk for a treated MVA1 child.** Neither is published, and §8's threshold table cannot be closed without the second.
13. **Any approved drug tested in a BubR1 model with a healthspan endpoint, and any paediatric senolytic dose in any condition.** Neither exists.

## 11. Scalability

**To another gene.** The drug-mapping tool changes two things and keeps the rest: the gene symbol and the mechanism-to-gene mapping change, the code does not. For a single-mechanism gene the command line suffices (`--gene TP53 --targets "MDM2 axis:MDM2,MDM4;p21:CDKN1A"`). Where the useful targets are consequences rather than the gene, a JSON config carries the biologist's decisions in reviewable form: which genes stand for a mechanism, which direction is wanted, which hits are excluded and why, and which drugs must be seeded by name because the databases file them elsewhere (rapalogs under FKBP1A, metformin under complex I, the chloroquine class under TLR7/9). Those decisions are the scientific content; the tool makes them explicit and attaches evidence links. It **is designed to transfer** to another chromosomal instability disorder with the gene and partner set swapped. **The transfer has not been run.** What does not transfer is judgement about direction at the phenotype level (the trilaciclib result) and hazards that live in the literature rather than in an interaction graph (taxanes).

**To another N-of-1 case, independent of gene.** Seven transferable parts: a measurement gate before any mechanism-directed prescribing, which both teams reached separately; a verification pass against primary sources, which changed four settled positions here and moved a drug each time; exposure arithmetic as a gate, which removed one candidate outright and is one division; a harm screen as a first-class output, deliverable without a trial; an endpoint power calculation before the endpoint is chosen, which rejected the intuitive endpoint in favour of FISH and turned the design question into a shared crossover protocol; a consent architecture; and a family representative with a veto over wording, a constraint that changed the report and not only its tone.

## 12. Limitations, and what a critic will attack first

**What this can claim.** A documented, citable procedure, run twice by panels that did not read each other, with a verification pass against primary sources that changed four settled positions, and a final round in which every seat wrote against the integrated record.

**What it cannot claim.** Nothing here is evidence that a medicine works. No candidate has been tested in an MVA patient or in MVA patient cells. No case report exists of any repurposed agent given to an MVA patient for the syndrome. No approved drug has a measured effect on cancer incidence in this disease, and ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 and EU CTR/CTIS return zero interventional trials for "mosaic variegated aneuploidy" or "BUB1B". Phase is unproven, residual BubR1 unmeasured, and the missense allele a variant of uncertain significance.

**First attack.** The two top-ranked outputs are a prescribing rule and a work-up, so a repurposing brief has been answered largely by declining to prescribe; five of thirteen seats said as much in their own strongest-objection sections. The reply: the surveillance changes in §6 item 2, the full blood count and the radiation rule among them, cost nothing or near it and are not a refusal, and a rule that prevents an aneugen being prescribed to a child with a segregation defect acts this month, while every mechanism-directed candidate acts in a hypothetical future.

**Second.** Independence is partial. Both teams read the same five dossiers, so their agreement measures dossier agreement as much as expert agreement, and only team 2 saw the divergent round.

**Third.** Two verified rows rest on abstracts: Baker 2013 and Wu 2023, the latter including its competing-interest statement. Body-of-paper effect sizes for Baker 2013 were not retrieved.

**Fourth.** A panel of assigned roles reproduces the caution its brief rewards. No structural defence is offered; the counter-evidence is that verification overturned four panel positions and that four seats changed rank against their own earlier argument.

**Where the panels may share a blind spot.** No member of either team found an error inside a dossier; every correction concerned a reading of one. Both reasoned from a mouse hypomorph whose allelic identity differs from this child's, although Sieben 2020 is the paper showing that allelic identity, not total level, drives outcome. Both worked without the child's age, weight, current medications, or whether ifosfamide was given, and every renal, PK and dosing conclusion turns on that last item. Both accepted p.Leu737Ter as pathogenic while the record shows 115 gnomAD v4 heterozygotes at AF 7.87e-5 and no published MVA patient carrying it, so its ClinVar classification reads as reasoning from mechanism rather than from patient evidence. **Neither team asked whether the diagnosis is right.**

**Re-identification, addressed rather than assumed away.** A child with MVA, a treated rhabdomyosarcoma, nephrocalcinosis, neonatal furosemide exposure and two named *BUB1B* variants is identifiable to anyone in the field within a described population of about 40 published cases (Scott 2006, PMID 16690728; Orphanet ORPHA 1052). Pseudonymising the case as PROBAND01 does not change that. What this report does about it: no name, initials, date of birth, sex, country, treating centre, weight or exact age appears anywhere; no image, scan, karyotype or raw sequence file is reproduced; the two variants are printed because the whole document is about them and no version of the argument survives their removal; nothing was contacted or disclosed outside the machine that produced this, and no patient-level data left it. What this report cannot do about it: the variant pair plus the phenotype pair is the identifying combination, and it is not removable without emptying the document. **The consequence is a distribution question, not a redaction question: this report is written for the treating team and the family, and it should not be posted publicly without their agreement.** That constraint should have been stated in the first draft and was not.

**One resolved discrepancy.** A dossier gave c.2209T>G where the prompt and the other files give c.2210T>G. Codon 737 is TTA at c.2209 to c.2211, so c.2210T>G gives TGA while c.2209T>G would give GTA, valine. The prompt is correct; the automated HGVS in `04_exomiser_model2.md` is wrong.

## 13. Response to referees

Three referees read the previous version: a clinical and molecular geneticist, a paediatric oncologist and clinical trialist, and a clinical pharmacologist and methodologist. All three returned major revision. Their corrections were adjudicated against primary sources in `track2/verify/V3_referee_challenges.md`, and where the adjudication went against the manuscript the text above has been changed, not defended. This section covers the remainder: three places where a referee was mistaken, and the errors that only this round found.

### Where the referees were right, and the panels and both earlier verification passes were not

Stated first, because it is the more useful half. Three findings came from the referees alone. Neither panel, neither the first nor the second verification pass, and no seat in the final objection round produced any of them.

1. **The RMS 2005 survival figures were wrong, inside the safety clause.** 85.0 against 72.4 percent should have read 86.5 against 73.7 percent, and the primary endpoint, disease-free survival, missed significance at p=0.061 and went unmentioned. Two referees found it independently. It sat twice in the document, once inside the carve-out written to stop chemotherapy being withheld, and both errors ran in the argument's own favour. Corrected in §5, §6 and §7.
2. **Mouse BubR1 1002 is human 1012.** The manuscript put "BubR1^H/L1002P" repeatedly beside "p.Asn1002Lys" without ever stating that the shared number is arithmetic. A reader would have taken gate item 6 for a mouse carrying this child's variant. Sieben 2020 says so in its own text, and nobody in this project had read that sentence. Corrected in §2 and §4.
3. **The ECG J-point defect is North 2014 in male BubR1^H/H mice, not Sieben 2020 in BubR1^H/L1002P.** Genotype and sex were both swapped in one sentence, and Sieben 2020 contains no electrocardiography at all. Corrected in §4 item 6.

Beyond those three, the referees are also owed the ClinVar VUS at c.3006T>A, the Ramirez 1997 attribution, the Welch and Floquet sentence, the carve-out contradiction with the ifosfamide substitution, the absence of radiotherapy and of a full blood count, the empty addition (c), the variance models behind the three power figures, the break-even threshold's dependence on an unstated choice, the unsourced s = 0.2, the exposure arithmetic withheld from hydroxychloroquine, the obtainability failures, the re-identification silence, and the overclaim for surveillance in the lay summary. All are applied above.

### The error found only in this final adjudication, by neither the panels nor the referees

**The 731X allele is a splice-acceptor allele, not a nonsense allele.** It is IVS16-2A>G, p.Ser714fsTer731: 713 residues of BUBR1 followed by 17 frameshift-derived residues, 730 total, about 81 kDa. It is not in Hanks 2004, and it is in neither ClinVar nor public LOVD. Every previous version of this project, including the flagship "verified correction" that the whole allele 1 argument rested on, treated it as a premature stop codon. Two consequences, both in §2. The rescue result stands, so a BUBR1 protein lacking the kinase domain is still shown to be functional when expressed. But **731X cannot support a stop-codon readthrough argument for p.Leu737Ter, because there is no premature stop codon in it to read through**, and that use of it is withdrawn. Referee 3 came closest, questioning the residue arithmetic ("a stop at codon 731 encodes 730 residues, not 715"), without reaching the allele's identity. The count is 713 native plus 17 frameshift-derived under HGVS numbering, 730 in total, and the 2010 paper's own "715" is off by one against its own supplement.

### Where the referees were mistaken

Three challenges do not survive checking. The text above is unchanged in these three places, and the defence is recorded here rather than in the body.

**1. The 731X rescue is Cancer Res 2010 Figure 5A to 5C, not Figure 4.** Referee 1 wrote that the truncation rescue is Figure 4A to 4C and that Figure 5 covers R550Q and Y155C. The premise is half true and the conclusion is wrong. Figure 5 covers all the mutants that do not affect protein stability, which is R550Q and Y155C **plus** the truncations 386X, 483X and 731X. The Results text is explicit: "LAP-731X has no kinase domain yet is fully capable of supporting the microtubule attachment function of BUBR1 (Fig. 5C). Furthermore, LAP-731X restored the response to nocodazole as well as proper chromosome segregation in unperturbed mitosis (Fig. 5A and 5B)." Figure 4's mutant panel is 194X, 753X and the five kinase-domain substitutions, confirmed by reading the x-axis labels off the deposited PMC figure images: Figure 4A runs Mock, Mock, WT, 194X, 753X, R727C, R814H, L844F, I909T, L1012P, with 731X absent, while Figures 5A, 5B and 5C each run Mock, Mock, WT, 386X, 483X, 731X, Y155C, R550Q. The probable source of the confusion is a sentence in the Results introducing the Figure 5 experiments with the phrase "as in Fig. 4A-4C", which refers to the assay design and not to where the data appear. Adjudication: V3 §1a, evidence level L1, text and figure image.

**2. The "two-fold mRNA but no protein" objection misreads the design, not the data.** Referees 1 and 3 argued that transcript at half normal with no detectable truncated protein is evidence against the transcript-level conclusion, and that the protein must therefore be unstable or the detection a false negative. Neither follows. The 0.51 measurement is on a **731X/wild-type heterozygote**, so half of normal is exactly one allele's worth, and the authors read it as complete absence of the mutant transcript rather than as partial survival of it: "the 2-fold reduction in mRNA levels of 731X/WT relative to healthy controls suggested that transcripts from the 731X allele were also absent". There is no surviving 731X transcript in patient cells to be reconciled with the absent protein, so the paradox the objection depends on does not exist. This is also why the authors' plasmid-driven experiment is the NMD-escape case: bypassing decay is what makes the protein appear, and it then rescues checkpoint, alignment and segregation. The detection artefact is closed by the paper itself. The Figure 2A legend states that antibodies recognise truncated proteins if overexpressed in HeLa, and the blot marks the expected migration position of each truncation by allele name, 386X on the lymphoblastoid panel and 753X and 731X on the fibroblast panel, so the authors looked at the right positions and found nothing. The 84, 81 and 43 kDa figures are arithmetic from the residue counts, not annotations printed on the figure. The negative is real. Two caveats a referee is entitled to press are now in §2: the 0.51 is measured on a parent and not on the patient, which the paper itself flags as a missing control, and the whole result is transient replacement in HeLa and U2OS. Adjudication: V3 §1c, evidence level L1.

**3. Metformin in Tang 2011 is sourceable, and the access route is now printed.** Referee 3 recorded the metformin exclusion as "unverifiable, and load-bearing", on the ground that the accessible record PMC3532042 is abstract-level and metformin is nowhere in it, and therefore that two dissents, a removal from the ranked list and question 5 rest on numbers in no reachable primary record. The numbers are reachable, but not where the referee looked. Metformin is named in the Results and the Discussion of the main text and is absent from the abstract; what is confined to the supplement is the concentration-response, Table S1 (metformin row, target AMPK, 1 mM, Ts13 73 plus or minus 2.9 against WT 86 plus or minus 2.4, Ts16 72 plus or minus 2.4 against WT 82 plus or minus 2.1, both P<0.005) and Figure S1A, whose x-axis is labelled "Metformin (mM)" with ticks at 0, 0.5 and 1.0. A later audit corrected this report's own earlier wording, which said metformin was absent from the main text. That route is now printed in §7 so the next reader does not have to find it, and the criticism is fair as a criticism of the manuscript's sourcing rather than of the number. Adjudication: V1 §1b.

### Two referee points not accepted as stated, and why

Recorded for completeness rather than as defence, since both change the text above in some measure. Referee 1 asked that the gate be reordered to put trio genotyping and a PCS karyotype first and alone, with an 11 kb genomic long-read amplicon as the definitive phase test. The substance is right and the long-read route is the cleaner phase test; the ordering was not changed here because the gate is already explicitly ordered by yield per cost and item 2 already runs parental genotyping alongside rather than instead. This is flagged as an open disagreement, not as a settled point. Referee 3 asked for a full obtainability appendix with licence status, formulation, mg/kg and renal adjustment per agent. The specific failures the referee named are fixed above, but no such appendix has been written, because age, weight and renal function were unavailable to this report and an appendix built without them would state confidence it does not have. That is a gap, and it is named as one.


### The audit round, run after the referees

Every claim in every deliverable was then checked against primary sources in five partitions, recorded in `track2/audit/`. **577 claims: 392 supported, 185 not**, split as 45 contradicted, 47 overstated, 45 with no source cited, 41 unverifiable and 7 misattributed. Every finding that touches this report has been applied above, and the pattern is worth stating because it is not the one the referees found.

**The errors cluster in this report's summaries of its own working files, not in the working files.** The quantitative appendix `track2/creative/F_math.md` recomputes exactly; section 8's retrospective account of it misstated its own coefficients of variation and baselines in seven places, all now corrected against a re-run of `pipeline/track2_math/f_math.py`. Four findings are worth naming individually.

1. **The "under 2 percent in controls" comparator does not exist in Kajii 2001.** That paper reports no control group at all. The carrier range is real; the comparator was invented somewhere in the chain and printed twice.
2. **Baker 2011's cause of death is "data not shown", and the rescue was narrower than claimed.** Senescent-cell clearance did not attenuate cardiac arrhythmias or arterial stiffening and did not substantially extend survival.
3. **Baker 2008 already answers a question this report printed as unresolved.** Removing p16 from the BubR1 hypomorph *increased* tumours, eight of nine being lung adenocarcinomas. That is genetic rather than pharmacological evidence, so the senolytic-drug question stays open, but the direction should have been printed where the question was raised.
4. **The RMS 2005 correction reached this report but not four other files**, and three micronucleus-baseline citations pointed at the wrong papers.

**Two cross-model passes were then run on a de-identified corpus** of 68 files, with four neutral prompts that ask for contradictions, recomputable numbers, single-source conclusions and over-reused sources (`vertex/`). GPT-5 recomputed 15 derived numbers and matched all 15, and found seven contradictory pairs, of which the live ones were the RMS 2005 figures and the dasatinib formulation tag in files that the report had already corrected. Gemini 3.1 Pro independently found the same RMS 2005 pair, caught an arithmetic error in the gate file's cost table and two rounding errors in its expression table, all corrected. Both are recorded because a check that mostly confirms is still a check: the honest summary is that the cross-model passes found less than the audit did, and nothing that the audit had not already reached.
